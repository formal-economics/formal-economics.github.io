Moral Hazard and Observability

How a principal should pay an agent whose effort it cannot observe, and when it pays to tie the contract to more information about that effort.

By
Bengt Holmström 1979
Published
The Bell Journal of Economics 10(1), Spring 1979, pp. 74–91
In the library
In progress Last worked on 18 October 2026
Cite
BibTeX for the source

About the paper

A principal hires a risk-averse agent whose effort is hidden; the outcome depends on effort and on chance. A contract that pays according to the outcome must trade insurance for the agent against incentives to work. The paper characterizes the optimal sharing rule and shows when an additional signal, beyond the outcome, should enter the contract.

  1. 1 Hidden actionWith a risk-averse agent whose effort is hidden, pay that depends on the outcome must trade efficient risk sharing against incentives, so the first-best is generally out of reach.
  2. 2 The optimal sharing rulePay rises with the likelihood ratio: outcomes that are relatively more likely under the desired effort are rewarded more.
  3. 3 InformativenessAn extra signal improves the contract exactly when it carries information about effort that the outcome does not.
