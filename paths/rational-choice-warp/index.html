Rational choice: from choice functions to the weak axiom

When can observed choices be explained by maximizing one preference?

Here the primitive is behaviour: what is chosen from each menu. The question is when such behaviour is the maximization of a single preference relation. On rich enough domains, condition α characterizes the rationalizable choice functions, conditions α and β suffice for choice correspondences, and on domains closed under intersection α and β together are exactly the weak axiom of revealed preference.

  1. 1

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 24

    Choice function

    A choice function assigns to every menu A in its domain one alternative C(A)∈A. It records behaviour only, with no reference to preferences or motives.

    Definition

    Given a context (X,D), a choice function C assigns to every A∈D an element C(A)∈A.

  2. 2

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 25

    Rational choice function

    The textbook model: given a preference relation ≿ under which every menu has a unique best element, the rational choice function C≿​ picks that element.

    Definition

    Let (X,D) be a context and ≿ a preference relation on X, and assume that every A∈D has a unique best element under ≿, an a∈A with a≿x for all x∈A. The induced (rational) choice function C≿​ assigns to each A∈D its best element under ≿.

    Open the entry
  3. 3

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 27

    Rationalizable choice

    The question in one definition: C can be rationalized if, for some preference relation ≿, every menu in the domain has a unique best element and C=C≿​ there.

    Definition

    A choice function C for a context (X,D) can be rationalized if there is a preference relation ≿ on X such that every A∈D has a unique best element under ≿ and C(A)=C≿​(A) for every A∈D.

    Open the entry

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 27

    Condition α

    Condition α: if A⊂B and the choice from B lies in A, it is also the choice from A. Removing alternatives that were not chosen cannot change the choice.

    Proposition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 28

    Rationalizability of choice functions satisfying condition α

    It is also sufficient on a rich domain: if the domain contains every menu of at most three alternatives, a choice function satisfying α is rationalizable, by the relation “x is chosen from {x,y}”.

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 31

    Choice correspondence

    To allow ties, a choice correspondence assigns to every menu a nonempty set C(A)⊆A of alternatives the decision maker is content to choose.

    Definition

  8. 8

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 32

    Weak axiom of revealed preference (choice correspondences)

    The weak axiom of revealed preference: if x,y∈A∩B, x∈C(A) and y∈C(B), then x∈C(B). Once x is revealed at least as good as y, y is never revealed strictly better.

  9. 9

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 33

    Condition α (choice correspondences)

    Condition α for correspondences: an alternative chosen from a menu is still chosen from every smaller menu in the domain that contains it.

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 33

    Condition β (choice correspondences)

    Condition β: if a and b are both chosen from A⊂B and b is chosen from B, then so is a. Alternatives tied at the top stay tied when the menu grows.

    Proposition · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, pp. 33–34

    Rationalizability by α and β

    The counterpart for correspondences: if the domain contains every menu of at most three alternatives, a choice correspondence satisfying α and β is, menu by menu, the set of best elements of some preference relation.

    Claim · Rubinstein, Lecture 3, p. 33

    The weak axiom (WARP)

    On a domain closed under nonempty intersections, conditions α and β together are equivalent to the weak axiom: there, the weak axiom says exactly what the two consistency conditions say.
