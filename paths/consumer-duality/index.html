The consumer: from the budget set to Roy’s identity and duality

Existence and shape of demand, indirect utility, expenditure, and the duality between them

The consumer picks a best bundle in the budget set B(p,w)={x∈R+K:p⋅x≤w}B(p,w)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^K_+ : p\cdot x\le w\}B(p,w)={x∈R+K​:p⋅x≤w}. The path shows that, at positive prices and wealth, a continuous preference attains such a bundle and describes it, turns to indirect preferences and Roy’s identity, then to the dual problem of minimizing expenditure, with two worked problems that reach the same utility from the indirect utility function and from the expenditure function.

  1. 1

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 4, p. 49

    Monotone preferences

    Preferences over bundles in R+K​ are monotone if having at least as much of every good is at least as good and having strictly more of every good is strictly better.

    Definition

    A preference relation ≿ on the commodity space X=R+K​ is monotone if for all bundles x,y∈X:

    • if xk​≥yk​ for every k, then x≿y;
    • if xk​>yk​ for every k, then x≻y.

  2. 2

    Claim · Rubinstein, Lecture 5, p. 67

    Existence of demand

    Existence: with positive prices and wealth, a continuous preference attains a best bundle in B(p,w). The proof goes through Debreu’s theorem: a continuous utility attains its maximum on the compact budget set.

    Claim

    Let ≿ be a continuous preference relation on X=R+K​. Then for every price vector p with pk​>0 for all k and every wealth w>0, the budget set

    B(p,w)={x∈X:p⋅x≤w}

    contains a bundle x∗ with x∗≿y for every y∈B(p,w).

    Open the entry
  3. 3

    Claim · Rubinstein, Lecture 5, p. 67

    Uniqueness of demand

    Shape: a convex preference has a convex set of best bundles, and a strictly convex one at most one. With the previous step, a continuous, strictly convex preference has exactly one best bundle at positive prices and wealth: the demand x(p,w).

    Claim

    Let ≿ be a preference relation on X=R+K​, and let pk​>0 for all k and w>0.

    1. If ≿ is convex, the set of solutions of the consumer problem at (p,w) is convex. More generally, for every convex set A of bundles, the set of best elements of A under ≿ is convex.
    2. If ≿ is strictly convex, the consumer problem at (p,w) has at most one solution.

  7. 7

    Claim · Rubinstein, Lecture 6, p. 85

    Roy’s identity

    Roy’s identity: for a monotone preference whose indirect preference is represented by a differentiable v with ∂v/∂w=0, demand is xk​=−(∂v/∂pk​)/(∂v/∂w). Walras’ law puts (p∗,w∗) on the hyperplane p⋅x(p∗,w∗)=w, along which the consumer is never worse off, and the identity compares slopes there.

    Definition · Rubinstein, Lecture 6, p. 87

    Hicksian demand and the expenditure function

    The dual problem: find the cheapest bundle at least as good as a target bundle z. When it has a unique solution, that solution is the Hicksian demand h(p,z), and its cost is the expenditure function e(p,z)=p⋅h(p,z).

    Claim · Rubinstein, Lecture 6, pp. 87–88

    Properties of Hicksian demand and the expenditure function

    Where the dual problems have unique solutions, e is homogeneous of degree one and concave in prices, Hicksian demand obeys the compensated law of demand (p′−p)⋅(h(p′,z)−h(p,z))≤0, and the plane e=p⋅h(p∗,z) supports e(⋅,z) at p∗: the dual of Roy’s identity.

    Claim · Rubinstein, Lecture 6, p. 88

    Consumer duality

    Duality: for a monotone, continuous preference, x∗ is best in B(p,w∗) exactly when it is a cheapest bundle among those at least as good as itself and costs w∗. Utility maximization and expenditure minimization describe the same choice.
