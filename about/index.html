About

A reference you can check.

A reference of definitions, theorems and solved problems from economic theory, each one stated precisely and checked by computer in Lean 4.

01 What it is

A handbook with a proof behind every line.

Formal Economics collects the definitions, theorems and problems of economic theory — today a textbook read in full, a PhD course, a qualifying exam and papers — and states each one precisely, the way a good textbook would. Every statement is backed by a proof in Lean 4, a proof assistant, which the computer checks line by line.

You do not need to read Lean to use it. Each entry gives the statement, its exact assumptions, an intuition, where it comes from, and how it connects to the rest. The Lean is one click away, for anyone who wants the guarantee itself.

02 The marks

What the badges and marks mean.

Machine-checked
Stated as the source prints it, and proved by computer.
Corrected
As printed, the statement does not hold in full generality. The counterexample is proved, and a corrected companion stands beside the original, which is kept word for word.
Open
Stated precisely, but not yet established.
Informal
A part of the source that is not a formal statement, such as an invitation to discuss.
Matches the source
An assumption that is exactly the source’s own.
Added condition
A condition a corrected statement adds; its page links the counterexample that shows why it is needed.
+Technical condition
Needed only to write the statement formally; it changes nothing in the mathematics.
In our words
The source asks a question and leaves the form of the answer to the reader; that form is ours, and it is machine-checked.

03 Checking

How entries are checked.

  1. 1

    Each statement is written in Lean 4 together with its proof, and the computer verifies every step.

  2. 2

    Each assumption of the formal statement is compared, line by line, with the printed source, so that nothing is quietly added, dropped or strengthened.

  3. 3

    Where a printed statement does not hold as written, the sentence is kept as printed, a counterexample is proved, and a corrected statement is proved beside it.

  4. 4

    Dependencies are read from the Lean code itself, so each entry shows what it rests on and what rests on it.

Behind the 219 entries: 3,868 theorems and 1,071 definitions, in 62,122 lines of Lean. Data as of 18 October 2026.

How to cite.

  author       = {Junwei Lu and Ethan X. Fang and Zhuoran Yang and Ce Li and Ruibo Song},
  howpublished = {\url{https://formal-economics.github.io/}},
