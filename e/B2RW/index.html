Exam question 3

Equilibria of a three-player game tree

A three-player extensive game has three pure Nash equilibria; two can be supported as weak perfect Bayesian equilibria, and one as a sequential equilibrium.

01The question

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

At the start, player 1 picks a, b or c. Choosing a stops play with payoffs (1,0,0). Following b, player 2 picks d or e. Following c, player 2 picks f or g; the move g stops play with payoffs (2,2,0). The histories bd, be and cf all lead to player 3, who does not know which of them occurred and picks h or i. Payoffs from h and from i, in that order, are: (3,0,2) and (0,2,1) following bd; (0,1,1) and (4,1,2) following be; (1,1,1) and (0,3,2) following cf.

  1. i

    List every Nash equilibrium in pure strategies. (Hint: the normal form helps.)

    Machine-checked statement 1

    The game in normal form#

    Answer · in our words

    Our answer: the normal form with strategy sets {a,b,c}, {d,e}×{g,f} and {h,i}, each profile paid according to the terminal history it reaches.

    Player 1 chooses a, b or c. A strategy of player 2 is a pair (x,y): an action x∈{d,e} after b and an action y∈{g,f} after c. Player 3 chooses h or i at a single information set containing the nodes after bd, be and cf. Each strategy profile reaches one terminal history, and its payoffs to players 1, 2 and 3 are those of that history:

    • a: (1,0,0);
    • b,d,h: (3,0,2), and b,d,i: (0,2,1);
    • b,e,h: (0,1,1), and b,e,i: (4,1,2);
    • c,g: (2,2,0);
    • c,f,h: (1,1,1), and c,f,i: (0,3,2).
    The definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    A strategy must say what a player does at every information set, even ones the play never reaches, which is why player 2 has four strategies rather than two moves.

    Machine-checked statement 2

    Pure-strategy Nash equilibrium of the game#

    Answer · in our words

    Our reading of “pure strategy Nash equilibrium”. Of two natural variants, letting player 2 deviate at one information set at a time gives exactly the same equilibria, while requiring every deviation to be strictly worse leaves none at all in this game.

    Player 1 chooses a, b or c. A strategy of player 2 is a pair (x,y): an action x∈{d,e} after b and an action y∈{g,f} after c. Player 3 chooses h or i at a single information set containing the nodes after bd, be and cf. On our reading, a pure-strategy profile is a Nash equilibrium if no player can raise his own payoff by changing his own strategy alone, the others’ strategies held fixed.

    The Lean statement

    Intuition

    Each player’s strategy must be a best reply to the others’ strategies; ties are allowed.
