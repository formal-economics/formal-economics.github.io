Claim

Risk aversion and concave utility

A vNM preference is risk averse exactly when its utility is concave, once the prizes form an interval.

Rubinstein, Lecture 8: Risk Aversion, p. 116 Risk attitudes · JEL D81 Entry CQGJ Citec

01The statements

1 As printed

Risk aversion is equivalent to concave utility#

Let ≿ be a preference on L(Z), Z⊆R, represented by the vNM utility function u (expected utility ∑z​p(z)u(z) represents ≿). The preference relation ≿ is risk averse if and only if u is concave.

2 Counterexample

Risk aversion without concavity on the integer prize grid#

Concavity on the prize set. Take the prize set Z=Z, which is infinite and not an interval, and u(x)=x. The preference represented by expected utility is risk averse, since [E(p)]∼p whenever E(p)∈Z. Yet u is not concave on Z, because concavity on a set requires the set to be convex.

Concavity as an inequality. Read “u is concave” instead as the inequality u(αx+(1−α)y)≥αu(x)+(1−α)u(y) for all x,y∈Z and α∈(0,1). On Z=Z, take u(x)=x at integers and u(x)=x−1 at all other points. The preference depends only on the values of u at the prizes, so it is again risk averse, yet

u(21​)=−21​<21​=21​u(0)+21​u(1).

Conclusion

The equivalence between risk aversion and concavity of u does not hold on some infinite prize set, under either reading of concavity.

The definition it is stated with

Intuition

On a prize set with gaps, risk aversion only compares lotteries whose mean is itself a prize, so it constrains u at the prizes and says nothing in between.
