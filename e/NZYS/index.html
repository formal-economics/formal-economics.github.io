The representative consumer

Aggregate demand need not behave like the demand of a single rational consumer.

Machine-checked Consumer demand and duality D11 Moderately difficult
Problem Consumer demand and duality Rubinstein (2012)

The question

Construct an example of two consumer preference relations whose average behaviour is not consistent with maximization by a representative consumer: two rational consumers choose x1x^1x1 and x2x^2x2 from budget set AAA and y1y^1y1 and y2y^2y2 from budget set BBB, so that choosing (x1+x2)/2{(x^1 + x^2)/2}(x1+x2)/2 from AAA and (y1+y2)/2{(y^1 + y^2)/2}(y1+y2)/2 from BBB is inconsistent with the model of the rational consumer.

Machine-checked statement

There are KKK, two different budget sets A=B(pA,wA){A = B(p^A, w^A)}A=B(pA,wA) and B=B(pB,wB){B = B(p^B, w^B)}B=B(pB,wB) with pA,pB≫0{p^A, p^B \gg 0}pA,pB≫0 and wA,wB>0{w^A, w^B > 0}wA,wB>0, two different preference relations ≿1,≿2{\succsim^1, \succsim^2}≿1,≿2 on R+K\mathbb{R}^K_+R+K​, and bundles xix^ixi that are ≿i\succsim^i≿i-best in AAA and yiy^iyi that are ≿i\succsim^i≿i-best in BBB, with x1≠x2{x^1 \neq x^2}x1=x2 and y1≠y2{y^1 \neq y^2}y1=y2, such that:

  • no preference relation makes (x1+x2)/2{(x^1 + x^2)/2}(x1+x2)/2 the unique best bundle in AAA and (y1+y2)/2{(y^1 + y^2)/2}(y1+y2)/2 the unique best bundle in BBB; and
  • no monotonic preference relation makes (x1+x2)/2{(x^1 + x^2)/2}(x1+x2)/2 a best bundle in AAA and (y1+y2)/2{(y^1 + y^2)/2}(y1+y2)/2 a best bundle in BBB.

Answer

Proved: such consumers exist, and the average behaviour is inconsistent with a representative consumer under full rationalization and under rationalization by a monotonic preference. The weakest notion, consistency with maximizing some preference, is not used: total indifference satisfies it for any behaviour.

Intuition

Averages of optimal choices need not be optimal for anyone. In the machine-checked example, with prices (1,2)(1, 2)(1,2) and (2,1)(2, 1)(2,1) and wealth 202020, the averaged bundles are (6,7)(6, 7)(6,7) and (7,6)(7, 6)(7,6); each costs only 191919 at the other prices, so a single consumer choosing them would have to rank each strictly above the other.
