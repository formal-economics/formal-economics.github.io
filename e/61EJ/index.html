Theorem

Social choice functions, straightforwardness and procedure invariance#

  1. A social choice function (SCF) C assigns an alternative C(≻1​,…,≻n​)∈X to every profile.
  2. C is straightforward if no individual ever gains by misrepresenting his preferences: for every profile, every individual i and every report ≻i′​, it is not the case that C(≻1​,…,≻i′​,…,≻n​)≻i​C(≻1​,…,≻i​,…,≻n​).
  3. Let Ch assign a choice function to every profile. Ch satisfies unanimity if for every profile, every choice set A and all x,y∈A: if y≻i​x for every i, then x is not chosen from A.
  4. Ch is invariant to the procedure if, for every profile and every choice set A split into two nonempty disjoint sets A′ and A′′, choosing from A′ and from A′′ and then choosing between the two picks gives the same outcome as choosing from A directly.

Technical conditions 1 · needed only to state it formally
  • The choice functions assigned by Ch are defined on a fixed domain of choice sets, which the text leaves unspecified; the conditions apply to the sets in that domain.

Intuition

These are the ingredients of three classic impossibility results: Muller–Satterthwaite (monotone social welfare functions), Gibbard–Satterthwaite (strategy-proof social choice functions) and Dutta–Jackson–Le Breton (choice rules invariant to the procedure).

Part (DJL)

1 As printed

Unanimity and procedure invariance force a dictatorship#

Dutta, Jackson, and Le Breton (2001) show that only dictatorships satisfy both unanimity and invariance to the procedure, for a function Ch that assigns a choice function to every profile. No condition is stated on the number of alternatives or on the domain of choice sets.
