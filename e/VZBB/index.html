Proposition Rubinstein (2012), Lecture 3, pp. 33–34

VZBB

Rationalizable choice correspondences (conditions α and β)

If the domain contains every menu of at most three alternatives, a choice correspondence satisfying α and β is the set of best elements of a preference relation.

Proposition

Let CCC be a choice correspondence for a context (X,D){(X, D)}(X,D) in which DDD contains every nonempty subset of XXX with at most three elements. If CCC satisfies conditions α\alphaα and β\betaβ, then there is a preference relation ≿\succsim≿ on XXX such that C≿(A)C_\succsim(A)C≿​(A) is nonempty and C(A)=C≿(A){C(A) = C_\succsim(A)}C(A)=C≿​(A) for every A∈D{A \in D}A∈D.

Assumptions

  • CCC is a choice correspondence for a context (X,D){(X, D)}(X,D). Matches the source
  • DDD contains every nonempty subset of XXX with at most three elements. Matches the source
  • CCC satisfies condition α\alphaα. Matches the source
  • CCC satisfies condition β\betaβ. Matches the source

Conclusion

CCC is rationalizable: C=C≿{C = C_\succsim}C=C≿​ on DDD for some preference relation ≿\succsim≿.

Intuition

Let x≿y{x \succsim y}x≿y mean x∈C({x,y}){x \in C(\{x, y\})}x∈C({x,y}). Condition α\alphaα makes every chosen element at least as good, pairwise, as everything in its menu; condition β\betaβ makes any alternative that is pairwise at least as good as a chosen one chosen as well.
