First-order conditions for the consumer problem

A consumed good must offer the highest subjective value per unit of price.

Rubinstein, Lecture 5: Demand: Consumer Choice, p. 68 Consumer demand and duality · JEL D11 Entry 07JS Citec

Proved as printed · 10 July 2026

01Statement

Claim. Let ≿ be a differentiable preference relation on X=R+K​ with subjective value function v, so that D≿​(x)=Dv​(x) for every x. Let pk​>0 for all k, w>0, and let x∗ solve the consumer problem at (p,w). If xk∗​>0, then

pk​vk​(x∗)​≥pj​vj​(x∗)​for every j=k.

5 Commodity k is consumed: xk∗​>0. (k : Fin K) (hk : 0 < xstar.val k) Matches the source
⇒ Then vk​(x∗)/pk​≥vj​(x∗)/pj​ for every j=k. ∀ j : Fin K, j ≠ k → (v xstar).val j / p j ≤ (v xstar).val k / p k

Intuition

Otherwise, shifting a little spending from good k to good j would be an affordable improvement direction: a unit of money would buy more subjective value in j than in k.
