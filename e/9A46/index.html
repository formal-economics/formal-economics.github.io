Claim

Walras’ law

With monotone preferences, optimal bundles exhaust the budget.

Source
Rubinstein, Lecture Notes in Microeconomic Theory Lecture 5: Demand: Consumer Choice, p. 70

01Statement

Claim. Let ≿ be a monotone preference relation on X=R+K​, and let pk​>0 for all k and w>0. If x solves the consumer problem at (p,w), then p⋅x=w.

The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
Number As Rubinstein states it Correspondence In Lean
1 ≿ is a preference relation on X=R+K​. (pref : WeakPreference ↥(nonnegOrthant K)) Matches the source
2 ≿ is monotone. (hmono : pref.IsMonotone) Matches the source
3 Prices are strictly positive, pk​>0 for every k, and wealth is positive, w>0. (hp : ∀ k, 0 < p k) (hw : 0 < w) Matches the source
4 x solves the consumer problem at (p,w). Stated by the Lean statement as a whole Matches the source
⇒ Then p⋅x=w. ∑ k, p k * x.val k = w

an assumption of the source, stated in Lean stated by the Lean statement as a whole

3 of 4 assumptions are wired to the line of Lean that states them; the Lean statement below states the others as a whole.

Intuition

If money were left over, the consumer could afford a little more of every good, which monotonicity makes strictly better.
