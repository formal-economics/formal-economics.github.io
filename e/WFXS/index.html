Problem 4

Which behaviour patterns are rationalizable?

Buying up to one unit of good 1 is fully rationalizable on its natural reading; choosing quantities in proportion to prices is not.

The problem

Determine whether these consumer behaviour patterns are fully rationalizable (K=2):

  1. (a)

    the consumer consumes up to the quantity 1 of commodity 1 and spends his remaining wealth on commodity 2;

    Machine-checked statement 1 of 2

    Buying up to one unit of good 1 is fully rationalizable#

    Answer · in our words

    Yes, fully rationalizable under this reading. One rationalizing preference ranks bundles first by min{1,x1​} and then by x2​.

    A preference relation ≿ on R+2​ fully rationalizes a demand function x(p,w) if, for every p≫0 and w>0, x(p,w) is the unique ≿-best bundle in B(p,w). Let x(p,w) satisfy, for every p≫0 and w>0,

    x1​(p,w)x2​(p,w)​=min{1, w/p1​},=p2​w−p1​x1​(p,w)​.​

    Then some preference relation on R+2​ fully rationalizes x.

    The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
    Number Assumption In Lean Correspondence
    1 The behaviour pattern, in our reading: x1​=min{1,w/p1​} and the remaining wealth is spent on commodity 2. — Matches the source

    The Lean statement below states these assumptions as a whole.

    The 2 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    The consumer values commodity 1 only up to one unit, and commodity 2 serves as the tie-breaker: a lexicographic preference with a cap. Its unique best bundle in every budget set is exactly the described choice.

    Machine-checked statement 2 of 2

    If “up to one unit” only caps good 1, rationalizability can fail#

    Answer · in our words

    Not always: under this weaker reading some demand function following the pattern is not fully rationalizable — for example, one that buys none of commodity 1 when p1​<p2​ and min{1,w/p1​} otherwise.

    A preference relation ≿ on R+2​ fully rationalizes a demand function x(p,w) if, for every p≫0 and w>0, x(p,w) is the unique ≿-best bundle in B(p,w). It is not the case that every demand function x(p,w) that is homogeneous of degree zero and satisfies, for every p≫0 and w>0,

    x1​(p,w)x2​(p,w)​≤1,=p2​w−p1​x1​(p,w)​,​

    is fully rationalized by some preference relation on R+2​.

    Assumptions and the Lean statement

    Intuition

    “Up to one unit” read as a mere cap leaves the consumer free to vary her purchases of commodity 1 with prices in ways that contradict each other across budget sets.

  2. (b)

    the consumer chooses the bundle (x1​,x2​) with x1​/x2​=p1​/p2​ that costs w. Does the utility function u(x)=x12​+x22​ rationalize this behaviour?

    Machine-checked statement 1 of 4

    Buying quantities in proportion to prices is not fully rationalizable#

    Machine-checked statement 2 of 4

    The sum of squares does not rationalize the price-ratio demand#

    Machine-checked statement 3 of 4

    The sum of squares does not fully rationalize the price-ratio demand#

    Machine-checked statement 4 of 4

    The price-ratio bundles are not even best for the sum of squares#
