Question 2

What utility maximization implies for two demand functions

Given demand formulas for two of three goods, Walras’ law yields the third, both formulas are homogeneous of degree zero, and utility maximization forces α = 100, β = γ = −5.

MIT 14.121, Problem Set 3, Question 2, p. 1 Consumer demand and duality · JEL D11 Entry WPSR Citec

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

(Adapted from MWG Exercise 3.G.6, parts (a), (b) and (c).) There are three goods, priced p1​,p2​,p3​. A consumer with wealth w>0 demands the first two goods according to

x1​x2​​=100−5p3​p1​​+βp3​p2​​+δp3​w​,=α+βp3​p1​​+γp3​p2​​+δp3​w​,​

in which α, β, γ and δ are constants, none of them zero.

Machine-checked statement 1

Walras’ law gives the demand for the third good#

Answer · in our words

Our answer: by Walras’ law, x3​=(w−p1​x1​−p2​x2​)/p3​. Without local nonsatiation this can fail: with (α,β,γ,δ)=(100,−5,−5,−1), p=(1,1,1) and w=80, the utility −((x1​−10)2+(x2​−10)2+x32​) demands only (10,10,0), which matches both formulas, yet buys 0 of good 3 rather than the residual 60.

Let α,β,γ,δ be nonzero constants, and let x1​(p,w) and x2​(p,w) be given by

x1​x2​​=100−5p3​p1​​+βp3​p2​​+δp3​w​,=α+βp3​p1​​+γp3​p2​​+δp3​w​.​

Let u be a utility function on R+3​ whose preference is locally nonsatiated, and let p≫0 and w>0 be such that demand at (p,w) is nonempty and every demanded bundle buys exactly x1​(p,w) of good 1 and x2​(p,w) of good 2. Then every demanded bundle buys the same amount of good 3, namely

x3​=p3​w−p1​x1​(p,w)−p2​x2​(p,w)​.

The definitions it is stated with

Intuition

A locally nonsatiated consumer spends all wealth, so whatever is not spent on goods 1 and 2 goes to good 3.

Machine-checked statement 2

Both demand formulas are homogeneous of degree zero#

Answer

Yes: both demand functions are homogeneous of degree zero in prices and wealth, as Walrasian demand must be.

Let α,β,γ,δ be nonzero constants, and let x1​(p,w) and x2​(p,w) be given by

x1​x2​​=100−5p3​p1​​+βp3​p2​​+δp3​w​,=α+βp3​p1​​+γp3​p2​​+δp3​w​.​

For every p≫0, w>0 and λ>0,

x1​(λp,λw)x2​(λp,λw)​=x1​(p,w),=x2​(p,w).​
Assumptions and the Lean statement

Intuition

Each formula depends on prices and wealth only through the ratios p1​/p3​, p2​/p3​ and w/p3​, which a common scaling leaves unchanged.
