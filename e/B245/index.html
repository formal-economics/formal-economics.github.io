Question 1

How deadweight loss changes with the tax

The derivatives in the tax of two deadweight-loss measures: for a locally nonsatiated consumer both are zero at t = 0; for t > 0 the measure at the old utility rises when compensated demand slopes strictly down at the taxed price, while the one at the new utility can fall.

Source
MIT 14.121 Problem Set 5, Question 1, p. 1
Field
Consumer demand and duality Part II, Consumers and producers · JEL D11

Problem Consumer demand and duality MIT 14.121

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

(MWG Exercise 3.I.2.) A consumer with wealth w>0 faces prices p0; a tax of t per unit of good 1 raises its price to p10​+t, giving prices p1, and raises revenue T=tx1​(p1,w). With u0=v(p0,w), u1=v(p1,w) and h1​(p1​,u) the Hicksian demand for good 1 (other prices fixed), MWG’s two deadweight-loss measures are, with pˉ​1​=p10​+t the taxed price, (3.I.5)

​e(p1,u1)−e(p0,u1)−T=∫p10​pˉ​1​​[h1​(p1​,u1)−h1​(pˉ​1​,u1)]dp1​​

and (3.I.6)

​e(p1,u0)−e(p0,u0)−th1​(p1,u0)=∫p10​pˉ​1​​[h1​(p1​,u0)−h1​(pˉ​1​,u0)]dp1​.​

Calculate the derivative of these measures with respect to t. Show that, evaluated at t=0, these derivatives are equal to zero, but that if h1​(p,u0) is strictly decreasing in p1​, they are strictly positive at all t>0.

1 As printed

Both deadweight-loss measures start flat, then rise#

Answer

As printed, this does not hold in full generality: the positive sign fails for L5​. Counterexample: with two goods, u=x1​x2​/(x1​+x2​), p0=(1,1), w=1 and t=24, where u0=1/4, h1​ is strictly decreasing, yet L5​ has derivative −1/3375 at t=24. The corrected statements keep both sign claims for L6​, adding that h1​ has a negative derivative at p10​+t, and the claim at t=0 for L5​, adding local nonsatiation.

A consumer with utility function u on R+L​ has wealth w>0 and faces initial prices p0≫0. A tax t per unit of good 1 moves the prices to p1, with good-1 price p10​+t>0 and all other prices unchanged. Let u0=v(p0,w) be the maximal utility on the budget set B(p0,w), with u0>u(0), and for good-1 prices r between p10​ and p10​+s, for all taxes s near t, let h(r) be a Hicksian demand bundle at level u0, other prices fixed at p0. With pˉ​1​=p10​+t, the measure (3.I.6) is

L6​(t)=∫p10​pˉ​1​​[h1​(r)−h1​(pˉ​1​)]dr.

For every tax s near t, let x(s) be a Walrasian demand bundle at the taxed prices and wealth w, with utility u1(s) above u(0), and let E(s)=e(p0,u1(s)) be the least cost at the initial prices of reaching u1(s). The deadweight loss (3.I.5) is

L5​(t)=w−tx1​(t)−E(t).

As printed: at t=0 every derivative of L5​ and of L6​ at t is 0; and at t>0, if h1​ is strictly decreasing in the good-1 price from p10​ on, every derivative of L5​ and of L6​ at t is positive.

Assumptions and the Lean statement

Intuition

The measure (3.I.5) is computed at the post-tax utility u1, which itself falls as the tax rises. At high taxes this shrinking of the yardstick can outweigh the growth of the distortion.

2 Corrected statement 1

Measure (3.I.6) has zero slope at t = 0 and positive slope after#

Answer

Shown, for the measure (3.I.6). The added condition is needed because a strictly decreasing function can be flat at a point: in a two-good example with p0=(1/2,1), w=13/6 and t=1/2, h1​ is strictly decreasing but has derivative 0 at the price 1, and the derivative of L6​ at t=1/2 is 0.

A consumer with utility function u on R+L​ has wealth w>0 and faces initial prices p0≫0. A tax t per unit of good 1 moves the prices to p1, with good-1 price p10​+t>0 and all other prices unchanged. Let u0=v(p0,w) be the maximal utility on the budget set B(p0,w), with u0>u(0), and for good-1 prices r between p10​ and p10​+s, for all taxes s near t, let h(r) be a Hicksian demand bundle at level u0, other prices fixed at p0. With pˉ​1​=p10​+t, the measure (3.I.6) is

L6​(t)=∫p10​pˉ​1​​[h1​(r)−h1​(pˉ​1​)]dr.

Then at t=0 every derivative of L6​ at t is 0; and at t>0, if h1​ is strictly decreasing in the good-1 price from p10​ on and has a negative derivative at p10​+t, every derivative of L6​ at t is positive.

The derivative of L6​ is −t∂h1​/∂p1​ at the taxed price: zero when there is no tax, and positive exactly when compensated demand slopes strictly down there.
