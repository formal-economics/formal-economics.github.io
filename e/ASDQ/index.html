Problem Rubinstein (2012), Problem Set 8, Problem 4, p. 125

ASDQ

Preferences over portfolios of risky assets

A risk-averse expected-utility investor’s ranking of asset holdings is convex, and monotone once there is at least one asset.

Corrected Risk attitudes D81 Standard

As printed, this statement does not hold in full generality; a corrected companion is proved.

Problem Risk attitudes Rubinstein (2012)

The question

A consumer has preferences over K{K}K-tuples of quantities of K{K}K uncertain assets. The return Zk{Z_k}Zk​ of asset k{k}k is random; (Z1,…,ZK){(Z_1,\dots,Z_K)}(Z1​,…,ZK​) are independent and positive with probability 1.{1}.1. Holding (x1,…,xK){(x_1,\dots,x_K)}(x1​,…,xK​) when the realized returns are (z1,…,zK){(z_1,\dots,z_K)}(z1​,…,zK​) gives total wealth ∑kxkzk.{\sum_k x_kz_k}.∑k​xk​zk​. The consumer satisfies the vNM assumptions, maximizing the expected value of a function v{v}v of total wealth that is increasing and concave. Show that the preferences he induces on the space of investments are monotonic and convex.

(i)

The induced preference over portfolios is monotone

Printed with no restriction on the number of assets; with no assets at all, the strict part of monotonicity cannot hold.

As printed

There are K{K}K assets with independent random returns Z1,…,ZK,{Z_1,\dots,Z_K},Z1​,…,ZK​, each positive and taking finitely many values. An investor holding x∈R+K{x\in\mathbb{R}^K_+}x∈R+K​ receives wealth ∑kxkZk{\sum_k x_kZ_k}∑k​xk​Zk​ and ranks holdings by expected utility, x≿y{x\succsim y}x≿y if and only if E v(∑kxkZk)≥E v(∑kykZk).\mathbb{E}\,v\big(\sum_k x_kZ_k\big)\ge\mathbb{E}\,v\big(\sum_k y_kZ_k\big).Ev(∑k​xk​Zk​)≥Ev(∑k​yk​Zk​). Assumptions

  • The returns are independent and positive, each taking finitely many values. Matches the source
  • v{v}v is strictly increasing on [0,∞).{[0,\infty)}.[0,∞). Matches the source
  • v{v}v is concave on [0,∞).{[0,\infty)}.[0,∞). Matches the source

Answer

As printed, this does not hold in full generality: it fails when there are no assets. With K=0{K=0}K=0 the only portfolio is the empty one, and the strict part of monotonicity, whose premise x≫y{x\gg y}x≫y then holds vacuously, would require that portfolio to be strictly better than itself. The corrected statement adds that there is at least one asset.

Intuition

Monotonicity’s strict clause compares portfolios that are larger in every coordinate; with no coordinates at all, every portfolio is “larger” than itself, and no preference ranks an option strictly above itself.

(ii)

The induced preference over portfolios is convex

An expected-utility investor with increasing concave utility weakly prefers a mixture of two portfolios to the worse of them.

Machine-checked

Assumptions

Answer

Shown: the induced preference is convex, for any number of assets.

Intuition

Wealth is linear in the portfolio in every state, and v{v}v is concave, so expected utility is a concave function of the portfolio. Mixing two portfolios therefore yields at least the average of their expected utilities, which is at least the smaller one.
