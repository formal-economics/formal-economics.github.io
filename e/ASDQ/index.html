Problem 4

Preferences over portfolios of risky assets

A risk-averse expected-utility investor’s ranking of asset holdings is convex, and monotone once there is at least one asset.

As printed, this statement does not hold in full generality; a corrected companion is proved.

The problem

A consumer has preferences over K-tuples of quantities of K uncertain assets. The return Zk​ of asset k is random; (Z1​,…,ZK​) are independent and positive with probability 1. Holding (x1​,…,xK​) when the realized returns are (z1​,…,zK​) gives total wealth ∑k​xk​zk​. The consumer satisfies the vNM assumptions, maximizing the expected value of a function v of total wealth that is increasing and concave. Show that the preferences he induces on the space of investments are monotonic and convex.

  1. (i)

    The source prints no separate question for this part.

    1 As printed

    The induced preference over portfolios is monotone#

    Answer

    As printed, this does not hold in full generality: it fails when there are no assets. With K=0 the only portfolio is the empty one, and the strict part of monotonicity, whose premise x≫y then holds vacuously, would require that portfolio to be strictly better than itself. The corrected statement adds that there is at least one asset.

    There are K assets with independent random returns Z1​,…,ZK​, each positive and taking finitely many values. An investor holding x∈R+K​ receives wealth ∑k​xk​Zk​ and ranks holdings by expected utility, x≿y if and only if Ev(∑k​xk​Zk​)≥Ev(∑k​yk​Zk​). As printed: if v is increasing and concave, the induced preference on R+K​ is monotone (x≥y implies x≿y, and x≫y implies x≻y), with no restriction on the number of assets K.

    Assumptions and the Lean statement

    Intuition

    Monotonicity’s strict clause compares portfolios that are larger in every coordinate; with no coordinates at all, every portfolio is “larger” than itself, and no preference ranks an option strictly above itself.

    2 Corrected statement

    With at least one asset, the induced preference is monotone#

    Answer

    With at least one asset, the induced preference is monotone.

    There are K assets with independent random returns Z1​,…,ZK​, each positive and taking finitely many values. An investor holding x∈R+K​ receives wealth ∑k​xk​Zk​ and ranks holdings by expected utility, x≿y if and only if Ev(∑k​xk​Zk​)≥Ev(∑k​yk​Zk​). If K≥1 and v is strictly increasing and concave on [0,∞), the induced preference on R+K​ is monotone: x≥y implies x≿y, and x≫y implies x≻y.

    The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
    Number Assumption In Lean Correspondence
    1 Added condition. There is at least one asset: K≥1. — Added condition
    2 The returns are independent and positive, each taking finitely many values. — Matches the source
    3 v is strictly increasing on [0,∞). (hvmono : StrictMonoOn v (Set.Ici (0 : ℝ))) Matches the source
    4 v is concave on [0,∞). (hvconc : ConcaveOn ℝ (Set.Ici (0 : ℝ)) v) Matches the source

    2 of 4 assumptions are shown beside the line of Lean that states them; the Lean statement below states the others as a whole. The added condition is the one the printed statement lacks.

    The 2 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    Returns are positive, so more of every asset means more wealth in every state; a strictly increasing v turns that into higher utility in every state, and hence higher expected utility.
