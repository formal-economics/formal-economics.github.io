Claim

Convexity and uniqueness of the consumer’s solution

Convex preferences give a convex set of optimal bundles; strictly convex preferences give at most one.

Source
Rubinstein, Lecture Notes in Microeconomic Theory Lecture 5: Demand: Consumer Choice, p. 67
Topic
Consumer demand and duality Consumers and producers · JEL D11

SSRC Cite c
Result Consumer demand and duality Rubinstein

Claim. Let ≿ be a preference relation on X=R+K​, and let pk​>0 for all k and w>0.

  1. If ≿ is convex, the set of solutions of the consumer problem at (p,w) is convex. More generally, for every convex set A of bundles, the set of best elements of A under ≿ is convex.
  2. If ≿ is strictly convex, the consumer problem at (p,w) has at most one solution.

Conclusion

Part 1: the set of solutions is convex (possibly empty). Part 2: any two solutions coincide. Neither part asserts that a solution exists.

The definition it is stated with

Intuition

A mixture of two optimal bundles is affordable and, by convexity, at least as good as one of them, hence optimal. Under strict convexity it would be strictly better than both, so two distinct optima cannot exist.
