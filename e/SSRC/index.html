Claim Rubinstein (2012), Lecture 5, p. 67

SSRC

Convexity and uniqueness of the consumer’s solution

Convex preferences give a convex set of optimal bundles; strictly convex preferences give at most one.

Machine-checked Consumer demand and duality D11
Result Consumer demand and duality Rubinstein (2012)

Claim

Let ≿\succsim≿ be a preference relation on X=R+K{X = \mathbb{R}^K_+}X=R+K​, and let pk>0{p_k > 0}pk​>0 for all kkk and w>0{w > 0}w>0.

  1. If ≿\succsim≿ is convex, the set of solutions of the consumer problem at (p,w){(p,w)}(p,w) is convex. More generally, for every convex set AAA of bundles, the set of best elements of AAA under ≿\succsim≿ is convex.
  2. If ≿\succsim≿ is strictly convex, the consumer problem at (p,w){(p,w)}(p,w) has at most one solution.

Assumptions

  • ≿\succsim≿ is a preference relation on X=R+K{X = \mathbb{R}^K_+}X=R+K​. Matches the source
  • Part 1: ≿\succsim≿ is convex (and, in the general form, AAA is a convex set of bundles). Matches the source
  • Part 2: ≿\succsim≿ is strictly convex. Matches the source
  • Prices are strictly positive, pk>0{p_k > 0}pk​>0 for every kkk, and wealth is positive, w>0{w > 0}w>0. Matches the source

Conclusion

Part 1: the set of solutions is convex (possibly empty). Part 2: any two solutions coincide. Neither part asserts that a solution exists.

Intuition

A mixture of two optimal bundles is affordable and, by convexity, at least as good as one of them, hence optimal. Under strict convexity it would be strictly better than both, so two distinct optima cannot exist.
