May’s theorem

With two alternatives, majority rule is the only social welfare function that is anonymous, neutral and positively responsive, and the three axioms are independent.

After May (1952)

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Based on May (1952). The set of social alternatives X has only two elements. A social welfare function (SWF) attaches a preference to every profile of preferences; indifference is allowed for individuals and for society. Consider the axioms:

Anonymity: if σ is a permutation of N and the profiles p={≿i​} and p′={≿i′​} satisfy ≿σ(i)′​=≿i​, then ≿(p)=≿(p′).

Neutrality: reversing every individual’s preference reverses the social preference.

Positive responsiveness: if p′ differs from p only in that one individual j moves from x∼j​y to x≻j′​y, or from y≻j​x to x∼j′​y, and x≿y under p, then x≻′y under p′.

  1. a

    Interpret the axioms. (b) Show that the majority rule satisfies all of them. (c) Prove May’s theorem: the majority rule is the only SWF satisfying them. (d) Are the three axioms independent?

  2. b

    The source prints no separate question for this part.

    Machine-checked statement

    Majority rule is anonymous, neutral and positively responsive#

    Answer

    Shown: the majority rule satisfies all three axioms.

    Let X={a,b} with a=b, and let N be a finite set of individuals, each with a preference on X (indifference allowed). A social welfare function F assigns a preference ≿=F(p) on X to every profile p. The majority rule ranks x≿y if and only if at least as many individuals strictly prefer x to y as strictly prefer y to x. Anonymity: permuting the individuals’ preferences leaves F(p) unchanged. Neutrality: reversing every individual’s preference reverses F(p). Positive responsiveness: if a single individual moves toward x, from indifference to x≻y or from y≻x to indifference, and society ranked x≿y before, then society ranks x≻y after. Then the majority rule satisfies anonymity, neutrality and positive responsiveness.

    Intuition

    Counting votes ignores the voters’ names and treats the two alternatives alike. And when society weakly prefers x, one more voter moving toward x tips the count strictly in its favour.
