Trading off quality and length of life

An expected-utility preference over lotteries on (quality, length) of life satisfies three natural conditions exactly when it maximizes the expectation of v(q)·t with v > 0.

The problem

Based on Miyamoto, Wakker, Bleichrodt and Peters (1998). A decision maker trades off longevity against quality of life. He ranks lotteries over certain outcomes (q,t), “a life of quality q and length t”, where q,t≥0. His preference satisfies the vNM assumptions and also:

  1. indifference between any two lotteries [(q,0)] and [(q′,0)];
  2. risk neutrality with respect to longevity: for fixed q, an uncertain lifetime of expected duration T is indifferent to a certain lifetime of duration T;
  3. whatever the quality of life, the longer one lives the better.
  1. (a)

    Show that the preference that maximizes the expectation of v(q)t, where v(q)>0 for all q, satisfies these assumptions.

    Machine-checked statement

    Maximizing E[v(q)·t] satisfies the three conditions#

    Answer

    Shown: maximizing E[v(q)t] is an expected-utility preference and satisfies all three conditions.

    Outcomes are pairs (q,t) with q,t≥0: a life of quality q and length t. Preferences are over lotteries with finite support on these outcomes, and [(q,t)] is the sure outcome. The conditions are: (1) [(q,0)]∼[(q′,0)] for all q,q′; (2) for each q, every lottery on outcomes (q,t) with expected length T is indifferent to [(q,T)]; (3) [(q,t)]≻[(q,t′)] whenever t>t′. Let v satisfy v(q)>0 for every q≥0, and rank lotteries by the expectation of v(q)t. Then this preference has an expected-utility representation (the form the vNM assumptions take here) and satisfies conditions (1), (2) and (3).

    Intuition

    The utility v(q)t is zero at length zero whatever the quality (condition 1), linear in length at a fixed quality, so only expected length matters (condition 2), and strictly increasing in length because v(q)>0 (condition 3).

  2. (b)

    Show that every preference satisfying them can be represented by an expected utility function of the form v(q)t with v positive.

    Machine-checked statement

    The three conditions force the form v(q)·t#

    Answer

    Shown: such a preference is represented by E[v(q)t] for some v that is positive on qualities.

    Outcomes are pairs (q,t) with q,t≥0: a life of quality q and length t. Preferences are over lotteries with finite support on these outcomes, and [(q,t)] is the sure outcome. The conditions are: (1) [(q,0)]∼[(q′,0)] for all q,q′; (2) for each q, every lottery on outcomes (q,t) with expected length T is indifferent to [(q,T)]; (3) [(q,t)]≻[(q,t′)] whenever t>t′. Let ≿ have an expected-utility representation and satisfy conditions (1), (2) and (3). Then there is a function v with v(q)>0 for all q≥0 such that ≿ is represented by the expectation of v(q)t.

    Intuition

    Condition (2) makes U(q,t) affine in t at each quality, U(q,t)=a(q)+b(q)t. Condition (1) makes a(q) a constant that can be dropped, and condition (3) makes b(q) positive, so U=v(q)t with v=b.
