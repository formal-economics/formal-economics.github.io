Problem 5 Moderately difficult

Threshold procedures (Kalai, Rubinstein and Spiegler)

Two procedures that switch between two criteria at a threshold: one is always rationalizable, the other need not be.

After Kalai, Rubinstein and Spiegler (2002)

Problem Choice and rationalizability Rubinstein

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Interpret the following procedures and determine whether either is consistent with the rational man paradigm. The primitives are two one-to-one functions u and v on X.

Machine-checked statement

Answer

(a) Not consistent with rationality in general: a machine-checked example violates condition α. (b) Consistent with rationality: every choice function following the procedure is rationalizable.

Let u,v:X→R be one-to-one and, for a menu A, let a∗ maximize u and b∗ maximize v on A.

  • (a) Procedure (a) chooses a∗ if v(a∗)≥v∗ and b∗ otherwise. For some X, domain, one-to-one u,v and threshold v∗, a choice function that follows procedure (a) is not rationalizable.
  • (b) Procedure (b) chooses a∗ if u(a∗)≥u∗ and b∗ otherwise. On every context, for one-to-one u,v and every threshold u∗, any choice function that follows procedure (b) is rationalizable.

The definitions it is stated with

Intuition

In (b) the switch depends on the best attainable value of u, so it only asks whether the menu contains an acceptable alternative; the procedure maximizes one fixed ranking — acceptable alternatives ranked by u, above unacceptable ones ranked by v. In (a) the switch depends on v of the u-best alternative, which changes as the menu shrinks.

  1. a

    With a number v∗: for a choice problem A, let a∗ maximize u and b∗ maximize v over A; choose a∗ if v(a∗)≥v∗ and b∗ if v(a∗)<v∗.

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  2. b

    With a number u∗: choose the u-maximizer a∗ if u(a∗)≥u∗, and the v-maximizer b∗ if u(a∗)<u∗.

    This part has no machine-checked answer.
