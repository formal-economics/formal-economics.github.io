01The question

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

The source states this question in the parts below.

  1. a

    There are two goods, A and B, with prices pA​,pB​>0 and income M>0.

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  2. a)(i

    Derive the demand functions xA​(pA​,pB​,M) and xB​(pA​,pB​,M) implied by maximizing the Cobb–Douglas utility U(xA​,xB​)=xAα​xB1−α​.

    Machine-checked statement

    Cobb–Douglas utility has a demand function#

    Answer

    Shown, with the familiar demands

    xA​=pA​αM​,xB​=pB​(1−α)M​.

    The bounds on α are needed: at α=2, with pA​=pB​=M=1, halving the amount of good B doubles U=xA2​/xB​ wherever U>0, so no affordable bundle maximizes it; at α=−1 the problem likewise has no solution.

    Let 0≤α≤1 and U(xA​,xB​)=xAα​xB1−α​ on R+2​. There are demand functions x(pA​,pB​,M) such that, at all pA​,pB​,M>0, the bundle x(pA​,pB​,M) is affordable, pA​xA​+pB​xB​≤M, and maximizes U over the budget set.

    1 Added condition The exponent is nonnegative: 0≤α. (hα₀ : 0 ≤ α) A condition the corrected statement adds
    2 Added condition The exponent is at most one: α≤1. (hα₁ : α ≤ 1) A condition the corrected statement adds

    The definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    With Cobb–Douglas utility the consumer spends the fixed shares α and 1−α of income on the two goods, whatever the prices.

  3. a)(ii

    A consumer has demand functions xA​=a1​M/pA​+a2​M/pB​ and xB​=b1​M/pA​+b2​M/pB​ for all pA​,pB​,M>0. For what parameter values do these demands satisfy the Slutsky symmetry conditions?

    Machine-checked statement 1

    Slutsky symmetry for the linear demand system#

    Machine-checked statement 2

    Slutsky symmetry holds exactly when a₂ = b₁ = 0#

  4. a)(iii

    For these demand functions, describe conditions on the parameters ai​ and bi​ that are necessary and sufficient for a complete and transitive preference order to give rise to them.

    Machine-checked statement 1

    A preference gives rise to the demands: best-element reading#

    Machine-checked statement 2

    A preference gives rise to the demands: unique-best reading#

    Machine-checked statement 3

    Best-element reading: a₂ = b₁ = 0, a₁, b₂ ≥ 0, a₁ + b₂ ≤ 1#

    Machine-checked statement 4

    Unique-best reading: a₂ = b₁ = 0, and a₁ + b₂ = 1 or a₁ = b₂ = 0#

  5. b

    Consider the von Neumann utility index u(x)=x​ for x≤100 and u(x)=10+x−100​ for x>100. Graph it. Does u imply risk aversion over all lotteries with nonnegative prizes? If yes, prove it; if not, provide a lottery that demonstrates the violation.

    Machine-checked statement 1

    Does this utility index imply risk aversion?#

    Machine-checked statement 2

    This utility index does not imply risk aversion#

  6. c

    Can an expected utility preference order give rise to the rankings

    (200,181​;100,91​;40,92​;0,1811​)≻(100,31​;40,32​)≻(200,121​;0,1211​)?​

    Provide your reasoning. Here (x1​,p1​;x2​,p2​;…) is the lottery that gives prize xi​ with probability pi​.

    Machine-checked statement 1

    Can an expected-utility order rank L₁ ≻ L₂ ≻ L₃?#

    Machine-checked statement 2

    No expected-utility order ranks L₁ ≻ L₂ ≻ L₃#
