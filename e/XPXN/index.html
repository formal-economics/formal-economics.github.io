Strictly concave preferences

Problem Consumer demand and duality Rubinstein

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

In a world with two goods, a consumer’s preference relation is strongly monotone, continuous and strictly “concave”: if a≿b, then a≻δa+(1−δ)b for all δ∈(0,1).

Machine-checked statement

Strict “concavity” of a preference#

A preference ≿ on R+2​ is strictly concave if a≿b implies a≻δa+(1−δ)b for every δ∈(0,1).

Intuition

It is the opposite of convexity: mixing a bundle with a worse one makes it strictly worse, so such a consumer prefers extremes to mixtures. Taken literally the clause also covers b=a, where it would require a≻a; the results of this problem therefore impose it for distinct bundles.

  1. a

    Show that for every t≥0 there is s with (0,t)∼(s,0), and analogously that for every s there is t with (0,t)∼(s,0).

    1 As printed 1

    Every bundle (0, t) is indifferent to some (s, 0)#

    Answer

    As printed, this statement is vacuous: with b=a the strict-concavity clause demands a≻a, so no preference relation satisfies the hypotheses. Requiring strict concavity only for distinct bundles, the statement holds — the corrected statement.

    A preference ≿ on R+2​ is strictly concave if a≿b implies a≻δa+(1−δ)b for every δ∈(0,1). If ≿ is strongly monotone, continuous and strictly concave, then for every t≥0 there is s≥0 with (0,t)∼(s,0).

    Assumptions and the Lean statement

    Intuition

    A hypothesis that nothing satisfies makes any conclusion trivially true, which is why the clause is read for a=b in the corrected statement.

    As printed 2

    Every bundle (s, 0) is indifferent to some (0, t)#

    2 Corrected statement 1

    Every bundle (0, t) is indifferent to some (s, 0)#

    Corrected statement 2

    Every bundle (s, 0) is indifferent to some (0, t)#
