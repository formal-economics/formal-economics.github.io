Exam question 4

A sales tournament as an incentive contract

When effort is unobservable, a sales tournament with a bonus and agent-specific salaries meets the first-order conditions at first-best effort and yields first-best profit, provided winning responds to effort there.

As printed, this statement does not hold in full generality; a corrected companion is proved.

Problem Contracts and incentives BU Qualifying Exam (2020)

The question

Two sales agents, r and e, achieve sales xi​=ai​+εi​, where ai​ is effort and εr​,εe​ are independent with a common density g. Both are risk neutral and effort averse: agent r’s payoff is wr​−ar2​/2 and agent e’s is we​−βae2​/2, with β∈(0,1), where wi​ is the wage. Both have the outside option Uˉ, and the company’s payoff is xr​+xe​−wr​−we​.

  1. (a)

    If the company can monitor effort and contract on it, derive the first-best contracts and effort levels.

    Machine-checked statement 1 of 2

    First-best contracts under observable effort#

    Answer · in our words

    Our reading of the first-best contracts the question asks for: maximize the company’s payoff subject to the two participation constraints wr​−ar2​/2≥Uˉ and we​−βae2​/2≥Uˉ.

    Agents r and e achieve sales xi​=ai​+εi​, where ai​ is effort and the noise terms are independent with a common density g. Agent r’s payoff is wr​−ar2​/2 and agent e’s is we​−βae2​/2, with 0<β<1, where wi​ is the wage; both have the outside option Uˉ. The company’s payoff is xr​+xe​−wr​−we​. A pair of contracts, efforts (ar​,ae​) with wages (wr​,we​), is first-best under observable effort if it gives each agent at least Uˉ and no other pair doing so gives the company a higher payoff. On our reading, the comparison is made at every realization of the noise.

    The 5 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    With effort observable, the company can dictate effort and pay for it directly, so only participation constrains it.

    Machine-checked statement 2 of 2

    First-best contracts exist#

    Answer

    Shown, with efforts ar​=1 and ae​=1/β and wages wr​=Uˉ+1/2 and we​=Uˉ+1/(2β).

    Agents r and e achieve sales xi​=ai​+εi​, where ai​ is effort and the noise terms are independent with a common density g. Agent r’s payoff is wr​−ar2​/2 and agent e’s is we​−βae2​/2, with 0<β<1, where wi​ is the wage; both have the outside option Uˉ. The company’s payoff is xr​+xe​−wr​−we​. A pair of contracts, efforts (ar​,ae​) with wages (wr​,we​), is first-best under observable effort if it gives each agent at least Uˉ and no other pair doing so gives the company a higher payoff. For every 0<β<1 and every Uˉ, a first-best pair of contracts exists.

    Assumptions and the Lean statement

    Intuition

    The company pays each agent exactly his outside option plus his effort cost, and sets effort where the marginal revenue 1 equals the marginal cost: ar​=1 and βae​=1.

  2. (b)

    The company now observes only which agent achieved the higher sales, and runs a tournament: the winner receives a bonus B on top of a salary Li​ for agent i. Express each agent’s expected payoff as a function of the efforts.

    Machine-checked statement 1 of 2

    Expected payoffs in the tournament#

    Machine-checked statement 2 of 2

    The expressions are the agents’ expected payoffs#

  3. (c)

    Ignoring participation constraints, show that there is a bonus B satisfying necessary first-order conditions for the first-best effort pair to be a Nash equilibrium.

    1 As printed

    A bonus meets the first-order conditions at first-best#

    2 Corrected statement

    A bonus meets the first-order conditions at the first-best efforts#

    Is it possible for the company to achieve first-best profit, ignoring second-order conditions? If not, explain why; otherwise derive the salaries and bonus that achieve it.
