Monotone, quasi-linear and homothetic preferences

With two goods and no continuity, three classic properties pin preferences down to lexicographic rankings by linear indices.

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

For the case K=2, characterize the preference relations that are strictly monotonic, quasi-linear in both commodities and homothetic (but not necessarily continuous).

Machine-checked statement

Answer · in our words

The characterization (our answer; the book poses only the question): exactly the preferences that rank bundles lexicographically by a pair of linear indices (a⋅x, b⋅x) that is lexicographically positive on every nonzero bundle. With b=0 these are the linear preferences a⋅x≥a⋅y with a≫0; with a=(1,0) and b=(0,1), the lexicographic order.

Let ≿ be a preference relation on R+2​; continuity is not assumed. Then ≿ is

  • strongly monotone (x≥y coordinatewise and x=y imply x≻y),
  • quasi-linear in both commodities (x≿y⟺x+εek​≿y+εek​ for k=1,2 and every ε≥0), and
  • homothetic (x≿y implies tx≿ty for every t≥0)

if and only if there are a,b∈R2 such that, writing ℓ(x)=(a⋅x, b⋅x), we have ℓ(d)>lex​(0,0) for every nonzero d∈R+2​ and

x≿y⟺ℓ(x)≥lex​ℓ(y).

Intuition

Quasi-linearity in both goods makes the ranking invariant under common shifts, and homotheticity under common scaling, so whether x≿y depends only on the direction of x−y: the acceptable directions form a half-plane. Without continuity, the boundary line of that half-plane can be split by a second linear index — the lexicographic pattern.
