N79K

Monotone, quasi-linear and homothetic preferences

With two goods and no continuity, three classic properties pin preferences down to lexicographic rankings by linear indices.

Machine-checked Consumer preferences D11 Difficult
Problem Consumer preferences Rubinstein (2012)

The question

Assumptions

  • ≿\succsim≿ is a preference relation on R+2\mathbb{R}^2_+R+2​; no continuity is assumed. Matches the source

Answer · in our words

The characterization (our answer; the book poses only the question): exactly the preferences that rank bundles lexicographically by a pair of linear indices (a⋅x, b⋅x){(a \cdot x,\ b \cdot x)}(a⋅x, b⋅x) that is lexicographically positive on every nonzero bundle. With b=0{b = 0}b=0 these are the linear preferences a⋅x≥a⋅y{a \cdot x \ge a \cdot y}a⋅x≥a⋅y with a≫0{a \gg 0}a≫0; with a=(1,0){a = (1, 0)}a=(1,0) and b=(0,1){b = (0, 1)}b=(0,1), the lexicographic order.

Intuition

Quasi-linearity in both goods makes the ranking invariant under common shifts, and homotheticity under common scaling, so whether x≿y{x \succsim y}x≿y depends only on the direction of x−y{x - y}x−y: the acceptable directions form a half-plane. Without continuity, the boundary line of that half-plane can be split by a second linear index — the lexicographic pattern.
