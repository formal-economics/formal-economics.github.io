CES demand and the elasticity of substitution

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

(MWG Exercise 3.D.5, parts (a), (c) and (d).) Consider the CES utility function u(x)=(x1ρ​+x2ρ​)1/ρ on a two-commodity world, the function of MWG Exercise 3.C.6 with α1​=α2​=1.

1 As printed

The CES elasticity of substitution is 1/(1 − ρ)#

Answer

As printed, this does not hold in full generality: it fails at the two parameters where the CES form degenerates. At ρ=1 utility is linear; with p=(2,2) and w=4 the bundle (1,1) is optimal, but so is every bundle on the budget line, and no elasticity of substitution exists. At ρ=0 the exponent 1/ρ is undefined; with the utility read as constant there, every affordable bundle is optimal, (1,1) among them, and again no elasticity exists. The corrected statement excludes ρ=0 and ρ=1.

Let uρ​(x)=(x1ρ​+x2ρ​)1/ρ on R+2​, and let p≫0 and w>0. Walrasian demand x(p,w) is the set of utility-maximizing bundles in B(p,w), and the value of the problem is the indirect utility. A number ξ is the elasticity of substitution at (p,w) if, for p1​ near its value with p2​ fixed, demand is a single strictly positive bundle (x1​,x2​) whose ratio x1​/x2​ is differentiable in p1​, and

ξ=−∂(p1​/p2​)∂(x1​/x2​)​⋅x1​/x2​p1​/p2​​.

As printed: for every real ρ, ξ12​(p,w)=1/(1−ρ); that is, every elasticity of substitution equals 1/(1−ρ), and 1/(1−ρ) is the elasticity whenever demand contains a strictly positive bundle.

Assumptions and the Lean statement

Intuition

The elasticity of substitution is defined through a single-valued demand that responds smoothly to relative prices; perfect substitutes, at equal prices, have a whole interval of optimal bundles instead.

2 Corrected statement

For ρ ≠ 0, 1, the CES elasticity of substitution is 1/(1 − ρ)#

Answer

With ρ=0,1, ξ12​(p,w)=1/(1−ρ): a constant elasticity, the defining property of CES utility.

Let uρ​(x)=(x1ρ​+x2ρ​)1/ρ on R+2​, and let p≫0 and w>0. Walrasian demand x(p,w) is the set of utility-maximizing bundles in B(p,w), and the value of the problem is the indirect utility. A number ξ is the elasticity of substitution at (p,w) if, for p1​ near its value with p2​ fixed, demand is a single strictly positive bundle (x1​,x2​) whose ratio x1​/x2​ is differentiable in p1​, and

ξ=−∂(p1​/p2​)∂(x1​/x2​)​⋅x1​/x2​p1​/p2​​.

If ρ=0 and ρ=1, every elasticity of substitution at (p,w) equals 1/(1−ρ), and 1/(1−ρ) is the elasticity whenever demand contains a strictly positive bundle:

ξ12​(p,w)=1−ρ1​.

The definitions it is stated with

Intuition

With interior demand the first-order condition gives x1​/x2​=(p1​/p2​)1/(ρ−1), a power of the price ratio. The elasticity of a power function is its exponent, so ξ12​=−1/(ρ−1)=1/(1−ρ).
