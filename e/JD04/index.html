A consumer with a utility floor

Securing a utility floor and then maximizing the amount of good 1 is rationalizable, spends all wealth and gives continuous demand; a closed floor set ensures a unique choice.

Problem Consumer demand and duality Rubinstein

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

A consumer has a continuous, monotonic and strictly convex preference relation, represented by a utility function u. He maximizes utility up to the level u0: if the budget set allows that level, he chooses the bundle in it with the highest quantity of commodity 1 subject to utility at least u0.

The setting, stated precisely

A consumer has a continuous, monotonic, strictly convex preference ≿ on R+K​, represented by a utility function u, and a utility level u0. Where the floor is attainable — at prices p≫0 and wealth w>0 such that B(p,w) contains a bundle with u≥u0 — the utility-floor procedure selects the bundles that maximize x1​ over {x∈B(p,w):u(x)≥u0}.

    Formulate the consumer’s problem.

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  2. b

    Show that the procedure yields a unique bundle.

    1 As printed

    The utility-floor procedure selects a unique bundle#

    Answer

    As printed, this statement does not hold in full generality. A continuous preference can be represented by a discontinuous u; when u jumps over the level u0, the set {u≥u0} can fail to be closed, and then the largest attainable x1​ on it can be approached without being reached. The machine-checked example is a continuous, monotonic, strictly convex preference with such a representation. Each of three added conditions restores the claim — {u≥u0} closed, u continuous, or u0 a value of u — giving the corrected statements.

    Wherever the floor is attainable, exactly one bundle maximizes x1​ over {x∈B(p,w):u(x)≥u0}.

    Assumptions and the Lean statement

    Intuition

    The procedure is stated through u, not through ≿ alone, and a representation can misbehave even when the preference is continuous.

    2 Corrected statement 1

    Unique selection when the floor set is closed#

    Answer

    Proved: the procedure yields exactly one bundle.

    If the set {x:u(x)≥u0} is closed, then wherever the floor is attainable exactly one bundle maximizes x1​ over {x∈B(p,w):u(x)≥u0}.
