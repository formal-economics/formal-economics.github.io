Claim

Utility representation on finite sets

Every preference relation on a finite set has a utility representation with natural-number values.

Rubinstein, Lecture 2: Utility, p. 14 Utility representation · JEL D11, D01 Entry 476N Citec

Proved as printed · 3 July 2026

01Statement

Claim. Let X be finite and let ≿ be a preference relation on X. Then there is N:X→N such that for all x,y∈X,

x≿y⟺N(x)≥N(y).
The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
Number As Rubinstein states it Correspondence In Lean
1 X is finite. [Finite X] Matches the source
2 ≿ is a preference relation on X. (w : WeakPreference X) Matches the source
⇒ Then ≿ has a utility representation with values in N. ∃ N : X → ℕ, Represents w (fun x => (N x : ℝ))

An assumption of the source, stated in Lean

Each assumption is stated by the line of Lean beside it. Point at a row to find its line in the full statement below.

Intuition

Peel the set from the bottom: the worst alternatives get the lowest value, the worst of the remaining ones the next value, and so on. Finiteness guarantees the procedure ends.
