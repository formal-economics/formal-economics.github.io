Problem 8 Moderately difficult

Information gathering and a non-convex cost function

A firm surveys TV viewers in four periods and must pool the answers with one worker; its cost function, computed exactly, is not convex.

Rubinstein, Problem Set 6, Problem 8, p. 96 Producers and the firm · JEL D21, D24 Entry QWKT Citec

Answered and proved · 6 September 2026

Problem Producers and the firm Rubinstein

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Based on Radner (1993). A firm runs a telephone survey right after a TV program, aiming to collect information about as many viewers as possible within 4 units of time. Each worker is paid w, even when idle. In one unit of time a worker can talk to one respondent or take part in a transfer of information to or from exactly one colleague. At the end of the 4 units, all the collected information must be in the hands of one colleague, who announces the results. Define the firm’s product, calculate the cost function and examine its convexity.

  1. Machine-checked statement 1

    The survey firm’s product, and a best schedule exists#

    Answer

    The product is the number of distinct respondents whose information reaches the announcer. With any workforce, a best schedule exists; no schedule surveys more than 8 respondents.

    A firm with n≥1 workers runs a survey over four periods. In each period a worker either talks to one respondent, or takes part in one transfer of information with one colleague, or is idle; what a worker knows grows only through his own interview or through what his transfer partner knew. At the end, everything collected must be held by one worker, the announcer. The firm’s product is the number of distinct respondents interviewed. For every workforce size n, the set of products the firm can achieve has a largest element.

    Technical conditions needed only to state it formally
    • The workforce is a set of n≥1 workers, its size left free.
    The definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    Interviews are quick but pooling is slow. In each period the announcer can at most add a colleague’s information to his own, so the information he can end up with grows at most like 1,2,4,8 over the four periods.

    Machine-checked statement 2

    The survey firm’s cost function#

    Answer

    C(y)=w for y≤4, 2w for y∈{5,6}, 3w for y=7 and 4w for y=8, for every product level the firm can achieve.

    A firm with n≥1 workers runs a survey over four periods. In each period a worker either talks to one respondent, or takes part in one transfer of information with one colleague, or is idle; what a worker knows grows only through his own interview or through what his transfer partner knew. At the end, everything collected must be held by one worker, the announcer. The firm’s product is the number of distinct respondents interviewed. Each worker costs w>0, busy or idle, and C(y) is the least wage bill of a workforce that can achieve the product y. The least wage bill exists exactly when some workforce achieves y, and then

    C(y)=⎩⎨⎧​w,2w,3w,4w,​y≤4,y∈{5,6},y=7,y≥8.​
    Assumptions and the Lean statement

    Intuition

    One worker can interview in all four periods. Every additional worker brings interviews but also uses up periods in transfers: two workers reach 6 respondents, three reach 7, and four reach the maximum of 8.

    Machine-checked statement 3

    The survey firm’s cost function is not convex#
