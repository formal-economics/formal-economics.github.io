Question 1

How much insurance to buy

A strictly risk-averse consumer insures fully when insurance is actuarially fair; when it is loaded and utility still rises at the insured wealth, she insures at most fully.

Problem Risk attitudes MIT 14.121

The problem

A consumer satisfies the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms and is strictly risk averse. Her wealth is w; with probability p she loses L. She can buy insurance that pays q dollars if the loss occurs, at a cost of πq, where π is the premium per dollar of coverage.

  1. (a)

    How much insurance will she buy, that is, what q (as a function of w,p,L,π) maximizes her expected utility? An equation that implicitly defines q is enough.

    Machine-checked statement

    Optimal coverage equates marginal benefit and marginal cost#

    Answer · in our words

    Our answer: the optimal coverage solves the first-order condition p(1−π)u′(w−L+(1−π)q)=π(1−p)u′(w−πq), with supporting slopes in place of derivatives where u has a kink.

    A consumer with a strictly concave von Neumann–Morgenstern utility u:R→R and wealth w loses L with probability p∈[0,1]. Coverage q∈R costs πq, so her final wealth is w−πq without the loss and w−L+(1−π)q with it, and her expected utility is

    U(q)​=(1−p)u(w−πq)+pu(w−L+(1−π)q).​

    Coverage q is optimal if it maximizes U over all real q. Then q is optimal if and only if u has supporting slopes s0​ at w−πq and s1​ at w−L+(1−π)q (so that u(t)≤u(a)+s(t−a) for all t at the corresponding point a) with

    p(1−π)s1​=π(1−p)s0​.

    The 3 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    One more dollar of coverage pays 1−π net in the loss state and costs π in the other. At the optimum the expected marginal utility of what it adds equals that of what it costs; concavity makes this local condition global.

  2. (b)

    How much will she buy when insurance is actuarially fair, π=p? What if π>p? Discuss.

    Machine-checked statement

    With actuarially fair insurance, she insures fully#

    Answer · in our words

    Our answer: full coverage, q=L, whenever the loss is uncertain (0<p<1); if p=0 or p=1, every coverage level is optimal.

    A consumer with a strictly concave von Neumann–Morgenstern utility u:R→R and wealth w loses L with probability p∈[0,1]. Coverage q∈R costs πq, so her final wealth is w−πq without the loss and w−L+(1−π)q with it, and her expected utility is

    U(q)​=(1−p)u(w−πq)+pu(w−L+(1−π)q).​

    Coverage q is optimal if it maximizes U over all real q. If insurance is actuarially fair, π=p, then q is optimal if and only if q=L or p∈{0,1}.

    2 p is a probability: 0≤p≤1. (hp0 : 0 ≤ p) (hp1 : p ≤ 1) Matches the source
    3 Insurance is actuarially fair: π=p. (hfair : π = p) Matches the source

    The 3 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    Fair insurance moves wealth between the two states without changing its expected value, and full coverage makes wealth certain. A strictly risk-averse consumer strictly prefers certainty to any gamble with the same mean.

    Machine-checked statement

    With loaded insurance, coverage is on one side of full#
