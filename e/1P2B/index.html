Claim

Convex preferences need not have a concave utility

Some continuous and convex preference on ℝ is represented by no concave function.

Source
Rubinstein, Lecture Notes in Microeconomic Theory Lecture 4: Consumer Preferences, p. 54

01The statements

1 As printed

A continuous convex preference with no concave utility#

Continuous and convex preferences might not have a utility representation by any concave function; for example, the relation on R defined by x≿y if x≥y or y<0. Read as a claim about this relation: it is continuous, it is convex, and no concave function represents it.

Conclusion

The relation is continuous, convex, and has no concave utility representation. Convexity and the absence of a concave representation hold; continuity does not hold for this relation (see the counterexample).

As printed, this statement has no Lean proof: it does not hold in full generality. The corrected statement is machine-checked.

Intuition

The example is meant to show that convexity of preferences, an ordinal property, does not deliver concavity of utility, a cardinal one.

2 Counterexample

The printed example relation is not continuous#

The relation on R defined by x≿y if and only if x≥y or y<0 is a preference relation, and it is not continuous: 0≻−1, yet every x<0, however close to 0, satisfies x∼−1. It violates each of the formulations of continuity used in the library: the open-ball forms of Lectures 2 and 4, the closed-graph form, and the closed and open contour-set forms.

Conclusion

The relation is a preference relation on R and is not continuous under any of these formulations.

Intuition

All negative numbers are indifferent to one another and ranked below every non-negative number, so the preference jumps at 0: a strict preference for 0 over −1 does not survive a move slightly to the left of 0.
