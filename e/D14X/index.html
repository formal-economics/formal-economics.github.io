Hotelling’s lemma and the profit function

Homogeneity, continuity and convexity of the profit function; its derivative is the supply.

Result Producers and the firm Rubinstein

01Statement

Claim. Let Z⊆RK be a technology with profit function π and supply function z. Each statement assumes that the maxima and unique solutions it mentions exist.

  1. π(λp)=λπ(p) for every λ>0.
  2. If Z is closed, convex and bounded above, π is continuous wherever the supply is defined.
  3. π is convex: π(λp+(1−λ)p′)≤λπ(p)+(1−λ)π(p′) for λ∈[0,1].
  4. (Hotelling’s lemma.) p⋅z(p∗)≤π(p) for every p, with equality at p=p∗, so the hyperplane {(p,π):π=p⋅z(p∗)} supports the graph of π at p∗. If π is differentiable, and the maximum defining it is attained at all prices near p∗, then ∂π/∂pk​(p∗)=zk​(p∗).
  5. If π is twice continuously differentiable, its mixed second derivatives are symmetric: ∂2π/∂pk​∂pj​(p∗)=∂2π/∂pj​∂pk​(p∗). The book combines this with Hotelling’s lemma to conclude ∂zj​/∂pk​(p∗)=∂zk​/∂pj​(p∗).

Conclusion

Properties 1 to 5.

Intuition

Profit is the maximum of functions that are linear in prices, so it is convex and lies above each linear function p↦p⋅z(p∗), touching it at p∗; at a smooth point the slope of π is therefore the supply.
