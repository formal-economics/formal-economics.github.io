Strict convexity on the unit interval

On the unit interval, strictly convex continuous preferences are the single-peaked ones.

Machine-checked Consumer preferences D11 Easy
Problem Consumer preferences Rubinstein (2012)

The question

Then ≿\succsim≿ is strictly convex — a≿y{a \succsim y}a≿y, b≿y{b \succsim y}b≿y, a≠b{a \neq b}a=b and λ∈(0,1){\lambda \in (0,1)}λ∈(0,1) imply λa+(1−λ)b≻y{\lambda a + (1 - \lambda) b \succ y}λa+(1−λ)b≻y — if and only if there is x∗∈[0,1]{x^* \in [0,1]}x∗∈[0,1] such that

Assumptions

  • ≿\succsim≿ is a continuous preference relation on [0,1][0,1][0,1]. Matches the source

Answer

Proved, in both directions: the strictly convex continuous preferences on [0,1][0,1][0,1] are exactly the single-peaked ones.

Intuition

On a line, strict convexity forbids flat stretches and dips: everything at least as good as yyy must form an interval whose interior is strictly better than yyy. With continuity this leaves exactly one shape — strictly rising up to a peak x∗x^*x∗, strictly falling after it.
