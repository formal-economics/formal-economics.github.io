Second-order stochastic dominance

Second-order dominance, the unanimous verdict of risk-averse expected-utility maximizers, follows from first-order dominance and from adding fair noise, and has a partial-sum test.

The problem

Based on Rothschild and Stiglitz (1970). Say that p second-order stochastically dominates q, written pD2​q, if p≿q for every preference ≿ satisfying the vNM assumptions, monotonicity and risk aversion.

  1. (a)

    Explain why pD1​q implies pD2​q.

    Machine-checked statement

    First-order dominance implies second-order dominance#

    Answer

    Shown: pD1​q implies pD2​q.

    Prizes are sums of money in a set Z⊆R, and lotteries have finite support. pD2​q means that p≿q for every preference ≿ represented by an expected utility Eu that is monotone (a>b implies [a]≻[b]) and risk averse ([Ep]≿p for every p whose expectation lies in Z). If p first-order stochastically dominates q (pD1​q: every expected-utility preference that is monotone in money weakly prefers p to q), then pD2​q.

    The assumption is stated by the line of Lean beside it.

    Intuition

    Second-order dominance asks for the unanimous verdict of a smaller group of decision makers, the risk-averse ones among those who prefer more money. A lottery that the larger group unanimously prefers also wins the smaller group.
