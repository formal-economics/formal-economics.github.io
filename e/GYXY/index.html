Comparing income distributions by equality

Income distributions look like lotteries, but an egalitarian ranking can violate independence; mixture quasi-convexity is proposed instead, with two rankings satisfying it.

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

There is a finite number of income levels, and an income distribution gives the proportion of individuals at each level, so it has the mathematical structure of a lottery. Consider the relation “one distribution is more egalitarian than another”.

  1. a

    Why is the von Neumann–Morgenstern independence axiom inappropriate for characterizing this type of relation?

    Machine-checked statement

    An egalitarian ranking of income distributions violates independence#

    Answer

    Independence is inappropriate because mixing two perfectly equal distributions creates inequality. With two income levels a=b, an egalitarian ranking has [a]∼[b], yet mixing each half and half with [b] yields the unequal 0.5[a]⊕0.5[b] and the equal [b], which it cannot rank as equally good, as independence would require.

    Call a preference ≿ over income distributions on a finite set Z of income levels egalitarian if every perfectly equal distribution [z], in which everybody earns z, is weakly preferred to every distribution and strictly preferred to every distribution that is not perfectly equal. There are a finite set of income levels and an egalitarian preference on its distributions that violates the independence axiom.

    The definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    Independence treats a mixture as neutral, preserving the ranking of its components. For distributions, mixing two societies merges their income levels, and the merged society can be unequal even when both parts are perfectly equal.

  2. b

    Suggest and formulate a property that is appropriate, in your opinion, as an axiom for this relation. Give two examples of preference relations that satisfy it.

    Machine-checked statement 1

    A proposed axiom: mixture quasi-convexity#

    Answer · in our words

    Our answer: mixture quasi-convexity. A mixture of two distributions is never strictly preferred to both of them.

    Our proposal: a preference ≿ over income distributions on a finite set of income levels satisfies mixture quasi-convexity if for all distributions p,q and every α∈(0,1),

    p≿αp⊕(1−α)qorq≿αp⊕(1−α)q.​

    Intuition

    Pooling two populations keeps the inequality within each and adds the gap between them: for instance, the variance of a mixture is at least the smaller of the two variances. So the pooled distribution should never be strictly more equal than both parts.
