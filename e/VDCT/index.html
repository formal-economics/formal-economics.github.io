Convex technologies and concave production functions

When a technology and a production function describe the same single-output producer, the technology is convex exactly when the production function is concave.

Rubinstein, Problem Set 6, Problem 5, p. 95 Producers and the firm · JEL D21, D24 Entry VDCT Citec

Answered and proved · 5 September 2026

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

One producer turns inputs 1,…,K−1 into commodity K. He is described both by a technology Z and by a production function f. Prove: f is concave exactly when Z is convex.

Machine-checked statement

Answer

Shown, in both directions: Z is convex if and only if f is concave.

Let Z⊆RK be a technology and f:R+K−1​→R a production function describing the same producer, who produces commodity K from inputs 1,…,K−1: for every v∈R+K−1​,

f(v)=max{x:(−v,x)∈Z},

and Z consists of the plans (−w,x) with x≤f(v) and w≥v for some v∈R+K−1​. Then Z is convex if and only if f is concave on R+K−1​.

As the problem states it

  1. 1 f is derived from Z: for every input vector v≥0 the maximum of {x:(−v,x)∈Z} exists and equals f(v).
  2. 2 Z is derived from f: Z consists of the plans (−w,x) with x≤f(v) and w≥v for some v≥0.

The Lean statement below states these assumptions together.

Technical conditions needed only to state it formally
  • The output commodity K exists; any number K−1≥0 of inputs is allowed.
  • Concavity of f is taken on its convex domain R+K−1​; the convexity of that domain adds nothing.

Intuition

Mixing two feasible plans of a convex technology is feasible, so the mixture of two input vectors can produce at least the mixture of their outputs: that is concavity of f. Conversely, when f is concave, the plans on or below its graph, with inputs freely disposable, form a convex set.
