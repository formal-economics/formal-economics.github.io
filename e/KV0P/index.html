Problem 4 Moderately difficult

Aggregating recommendations

When individuals recommend actions instead of ranking them, unanimity and independence force a dictator once there are three actions, and each condition is needed.

Problem Social choice Rubinstein

01The problem2 parts

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

In a society N, each individual chooses a single object from a set X, interpreted as the social action he recommends. Consider functions that aggregate the individuals’ recommendations (not preferences, just recommendations) into a social decision, and the axioms:

Par: if all individuals recommend x∗, society chooses x∗;

I: if the same individuals recommend an alternative x in two profiles of recommendations, then x is chosen in one profile if and only if it is chosen in the other.

  1. a

    Show that if X has at least three elements, the only aggregation method that satisfies Par and I is a dictatorship. (b) Show that each of the three conditions Par, I and ∣X∣≥3 is needed for this conclusion.

    Machine-checked statement

    Unanimity and independence force a dictator#

    Answer · in our words

    Shown: with at least three actions, the aggregation methods satisfying Par and I are exactly the dictatorships, “dictatorship” meaning that one individual’s recommendation is always the social choice.

    A society N chooses from a set X of actions. An aggregation method F maps each profile of recommendations p=(pi​)i∈N​∈XN to a social choice F(p)∈X. It satisfies Par if F(p)=x whenever every individual recommends x, and I if, for every x, whether F(p)=x depends only on the set of individuals who recommend x. It is a dictatorship if for some individual i∗, F(p)=pi∗​ at every profile. If N is finite and X has at least three elements, then F satisfies Par and I if and only if it is a dictatorship.

    The 3 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    By I, whether an action is chosen depends only on the coalition recommending it. With three actions these winning coalitions turn out to be the same for every action and to be closed under intersection, and on a finite society that leaves a single individual whose recommendation always prevails.

  2. b

    The source prints no separate question for this part.

    Machine-checked statement 1 of 3

    Without Par, a non-dictatorial method satisfies I#

    Answer

    Par is needed: some method satisfies I without being a dictatorship. For instance, always choosing the same action, whatever is recommended, satisfies I.

    A society N chooses from a set X of actions. An aggregation method F maps each profile of recommendations p=(pi​)i∈N​∈XN to a social choice F(p)∈X. It satisfies Par if F(p)=x whenever every individual recommends x, and I if, for every x, whether F(p)=x depends only on the set of individuals who recommend x. It is a dictatorship if for some individual i∗, F(p)=pi∗​ at every profile. If N is finite and X has at least three elements, some aggregation method satisfies I and is not a dictatorship.
