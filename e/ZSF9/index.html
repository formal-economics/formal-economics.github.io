The betweenness axiom

Betweenness follows from independence, means straight but not necessarily parallel indifference lines, and is compatible with the Allais paradox.

Problem Choice under uncertainty MIT 14.121

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

(MWG Exercise 6.B.5.) There are three possible outcomes. The betweenness axiom (Dekel, 1986) requires that for all lotteries L,L′ and λ∈(0,1), L∼L′ implies λL+(1−λ)L′∼L.

  1. a

    Show that a preference relation on lotteries satisfying the independence axiom also satisfies betweenness.

    Machine-checked statement

    Independence implies betweenness#

    Answer

    Shown.

    Lotteries are probability vectors over three outcomes, and ≿ is a binary relation on lotteries, with L∼L′ when L≿L′ and L′≿L. Betweenness: L∼L′ implies λL+(1−λ)L′∼L for every λ∈(0,1). If ≿ satisfies independence: L≿L′ if and only if αL+(1−α)L′′≿αL′+(1−α)L′′, for all L,L′,L′′ and α∈(0,1), then it satisfies betweenness.

  2. b

    Using the simplex representation, show that if the continuity and betweenness axioms hold, indifference curves are straight lines; conversely, show that straight indifference curves imply betweenness. Are these straight lines parallel?

    Machine-checked statement

    Continuity and betweenness make indifference curves straight#

    Answer

    Shown: the indifference curves are straight lines.

    Lotteries are probability vectors over three outcomes, and ≿ is a binary relation on lotteries, with L∼L′ when L≿L′ and L′≿L. Betweenness: L∼L′ implies λL+(1−λ)L′∼L for every λ∈(0,1). If ≿ satisfies continuity: for all L,L′,L′′ the sets {α∈[0,1]:αL+(1−α)L′≿L′′} and {α∈[0,1]:L′′≿αL+(1−α)L′} are closed, together with betweenness, then its indifference curves are straight lines: L∼L′ implies αL+(1−α)L′∼L for every α∈[0,1].
