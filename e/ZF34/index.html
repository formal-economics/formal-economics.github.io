Claim Rubinstein (2012), Lecture 6, p. 90

ZF34

The returns maximizer and preference maximization

Whether maximizing profit per unit of cost can be described by preferences over plans and profits.

Corrected Producers and the firm D21D24

As printed, this statement does not hold in full generality; a corrected companion is proved.

Result Producers and the firm Rubinstein (2012)

The returns maximizer cannot be represented

Stated for the returns maximizer: no preference over plan–profit pairs represents maximizing profit per unit of cost, since it depends on how profit splits.

As printed

As printed (example 6): with K=2{K = 2}K=2, commodity 1 the input and commodity 2 the output, a returns maximizer, who maximizes the ratio of profits to costs π/(paa){\pi/(p_a a)}π/(pa​a), cannot be represented as maximizing a preference relation on X=Z×R{X = Z \times \mathbb{R}}X=Z×R, since the behavior depends on the breakdown of profit into revenues and costs and not just on profit.

Assumptions

  • Two commodities: commodity 1 is the input and commodity 2 the output. Matches the source
  • The returns maximizer chooses a plan maximizing π/(paa){\pi / (p_a a)}π/(pa​a). Matches the source

Conclusion

No preference on Z×R{Z \times \mathbb{R}}Z×R reproduces the returns maximizer’s choices. With a single input, as printed, this does not hold (see the counterexample).

With one input, the returns maximizer is representable

With one input, ranking plans by profit per unit of input reproduces the returns maximizer’s choices, for every technology and every positive input price.

Counterexample

Let K=2{K = 2}K=2, with commodity 1 the input. For every technology Z⊆R2{Z \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2}Z⊆R2 there is a preference relation ≿\succsim≿ on Z×R{Z \times \mathbb{R}}Z×R such that, at every price vector with p1>0{p_1 > 0}p1​>0, a plan zzz maximizes π/(p1a){\pi / (p_1 a)}π/(p1​a) over ZZZ, where a=−z1{a = -z_1}a=−z1​, if and only if (z,p⋅z){(z, p \cdot z)}(z,p⋅z) is best in B(p)B(p)B(p) under ≿\succsim≿. One such preference ranks pairs by g(z,π)=π/a{g(z, \pi) = \pi / a}g(z,π)=π/a, the profit per unit of input.

Assumptions

  • Z⊆R2{Z \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2}Z⊆R2 is any technology. Matches the source

Conclusion

The returns maximizer’s choices coincide with the maximization of a preference on Z×R{Z \times \mathbb{R}}Z×R at every price vector with a positive input price.

Intuition

With a single input, π/(p1a)=(π/a)/p1\pi / (p_1 a) = (\pi / a) / p_1π/(p1​a)=(π/a)/p1​. At given prices, dividing by the positive constant p1p_1p1​ does not change the ranking, so maximizing returns is maximizing profit per unit of input, a function of the pair (z,π){(z, \pi)}(z,π) alone.

With two or more inputs, the returns maximizer is not representable

With at least two inputs and one output, some technology admits no preference over plan–profit pairs that reproduces the returns maximizer’s choices.

Corrected statement

Let the commodities be LLL inputs followed by MMM outputs, with at least two inputs (L≥2{L \ge 2}L≥2) and at least one output (M≥1{M \ge 1}M≥1), and let the returns maximizer choose z∈Z{z \in Z}z∈Z to maximize profit over input cost,

p⋅z∑i≤Lpi (−zi).\frac{p \cdot z}{\sum_{i \le L} p_i\,(-z_i)}.∑i≤L​pi​(−zi​)p⋅z​.

Then there is a technology Z⊆RL+M{Z \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{L+M}}Z⊆RL+M for which no preference relation on Z×R{Z \times \mathbb{R}}Z×R reproduces the returns maximizer’s choices at every price vector with positive input prices.

Assumptions

  • The commodities are LLL inputs followed by MMM outputs. Matches the source
  • Added condition There are at least two inputs, L≥2{L \ge 2}L≥2. Why it is needed → Added condition
  • Added condition There is at least one output, M≥1{M \ge 1}M≥1. Why it is needed → Added condition
  • Input prices are positive. Matches the source

Conclusion

For some technology ZZZ, no preference on Z×R{Z \times \mathbb{R}}Z×R has the returns maximizer’s choices.

Intuition

With two inputs, the cost of a plan depends on relative input prices, which a ranking of plan–profit pairs cannot see; here the printed reason, that the behavior depends on how profit splits into revenue and cost, applies.
