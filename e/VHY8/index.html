Problem 7

The output-maximizing producer with a profit target

A producer who maximizes output subject to earning at least ρ: uniqueness, monotone and continuous supply, and the effect of ρ, each under explicitly stated assumptions.

Problem Producers and the firm Rubinstein

The problem

A producer makes one commodity from L inputs. He maximizes output subject to earning a profit of at least ρ, and produces nothing if he cannot. Show that, under reasonable assumptions:

  1. (a)

    the producer’s problem has a unique solution for every price vector;

    1 As printed

    A unique solution at every price vector#

    Answer

    Without stated assumptions this does not hold in full generality. Counterexample: one input, the whole plane as the technology, ρ=0 and p=(1,1). The idle plan earns the target, but any plan that earns it is beaten by the plan producing one unit more, so there is no solution. The corrected statement supplies the assumptions.

    Under reasonable assumptions, the problem of a producer who maximizes output subject to achieving a profit level ρ, and does not produce at all if he cannot, has a unique solution for every price vector. The assumptions are not stated.

    As printed, this statement has no Lean proof: it does not hold in full generality. The corrected statement is machine-checked.

    Intuition

    Existence needs the set of plans meeting the profit target to have a highest output, and uniqueness needs that highest point to be unique; neither is automatic for an arbitrary technology.

    2 Corrected statement

    Unique solution for a closed, bounded, strictly convex technology#

    Answer

    With these assumptions the solution exists and is unique at every p≫0, for every ρ. Each assumption is needed: an open quadrant, the whole plane, a closed quadrant and the unit disc at the price vector (0,−1) break the statement when closedness, boundedness, strict convexity or positive prices, respectively, are dropped.

    A producer has a technology Z⊆RL+1 (inputs 1,…,L, output K=L+1) and faces prices p. A plan z solves his problem at profit target ρ if either some plan w∈Z earns p⋅w≥ρ, and z is such a plan with the largest output zK​; or no plan earns ρ, and z=0. If Z is closed, bounded above and strictly convex, then for every profit level ρ and every price vector p≫0 the problem has exactly one solution.

    The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
    Number Assumption In Lean Correspondence
    1 Added condition. Z is closed and bounded above: for some B, zk​≤B for every z∈Z and every k. (hZclosed : IsClosed Z) Added condition
    2 Added condition. Z is strictly convex: for distinct z,z′∈Z and 0<λ<1, λz+(1−λ)z′ is an interior point of Z. (hZsc : StrictConvex ℝ Z) Added condition
    3 Added condition. Prices are strictly positive: p≫0. — Added condition

    2 of 3 assumptions are shown beside the line of Lean that states them; the Lean statement below states the others as a whole. The added conditions are the ones the printed statement lacks.

    The definition it is stated with

    Intuition

    Closedness, an upper bound and positive prices make the set of plans that meet the target compact, so a highest output exists. Strict convexity rules out two distinct plans with the same highest output, since their midpoint would lie in the interior of Z and could be pushed to produce more.

  2. (b)

    the producer’s supply function is monotonic in prices;

    1 As printed

    Supply is monotonic in prices#

    2 Corrected statement

    Output rises with its own price and falls with an input’s price#

  3. (c)

    the supply function is continuous in prices when ρ=0;

    1 As printed

    Supply is continuous in prices when ρ = 0#

    2 Corrected statement

    Supply is continuous at ρ = 0 for closed, strictly convex technologies#

  4. (d)

    the supply function is monotonic in ρ.

    1 As printed

    Supply is monotonic in the profit target#

    2 Corrected statement

    Output falls weakly as the profit target rises#
