The output-maximizing producer with a profit target

A producer who maximizes output subject to earning at least ρ: uniqueness, monotone and continuous supply, and the effect of ρ, each under explicitly stated assumptions.

Corrected Producers and the firm D21D24 Moderately difficult

As printed, this statement does not hold in full generality; a corrected companion is proved.

Problem Producers and the firm Rubinstein (2012)

The question

A producer makes one commodity from L{L}L inputs. He maximizes output subject to earning a profit of at least ρ,{\rho},ρ, and produces nothing if he cannot. Show that, under reasonable assumptions:

  • (a) the producer’s problem has a unique solution for every price vector;
  • (b) the producer’s supply function is monotonic in prices;
  • (c) the supply function is continuous in prices when ρ=0;{\rho=0};ρ=0;
  • (d) the supply function is monotonic in ρ.{\rho}.ρ.

    • Unique solution for a closed, bounded, strictly convex technology ↓

Part (b)

  1. 01As printed

  2. 02Corrected statement

Part (c)

  1. 01As printed

  2. 02Corrected statement

    • Supply is continuous at ρ = 0 for closed, strictly convex technologies ↓

Part (d)

  1. 01As printed

    • Supply is monotonic in the profit target ↓

  2. 02Corrected statement

Parts (a)(b)(c)(d)

(a)

A unique solution at every price vector

Printed under unstated reasonable assumptions; without them the producer’s problem can have no solution at all.

As printed

As printed: under reasonable assumptions, the problem of a producer who maximizes output subject to achieving a profit level ρ,{\rho},ρ, and does not produce at all if he cannot, has a unique solution for every price vector. The assumptions are not stated.

Answer

Without stated assumptions this does not hold in full generality. Counterexample: one input, the whole plane as the technology, ρ=0{\rho=0}ρ=0 and p=(1,1).{p=(1,1)}.p=(1,1). The idle plan earns the target, but any plan that earns it is beaten by the plan producing one unit more, so there is no solution. The corrected statement supplies the assumptions.

Intuition

Existence needs the set of plans meeting the profit target to have a highest output, and uniqueness needs that highest point to be unique; neither is automatic for an arbitrary technology.

Unique solution for a closed, bounded, strictly convex technology

If the technology is closed, bounded above and strictly convex, the output-maximizing producer with a profit target has exactly one solution at every positive price vector.

Corrected statement

A producer has a technology Z⊆RL+1{Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{L+1}}Z⊆RL+1 (inputs 1,…,L,{1,\dots,L},1,…,L, output K=L+1{K=L+1}K=L+1) and faces prices p.{p}.p. A plan z{z}z solves his problem at profit target ρ{\rho}ρ if either some plan in Z{Z}Z earns p⋅z≥ρ,{p\cdot z\ge\rho},p⋅z≥ρ, and z{z}z is such a plan with the largest output zK;{z_K};zK​; or no plan earns ρ,{\rho},ρ, and z=0.{z=0}.z=0. If Z{Z}Z is closed, bounded above and strictly convex, then for every profit level ρ{\rho}ρ and every price vector p≫0{p\gg 0}p≫0 the problem has exactly one solution.

  • Added condition Z{Z}Z is closed and bounded above: for some B,{B},B, zk≤B{z_k\le B}zk​≤B for every z∈Z{z\in Z}z∈Z and every k.{k}.k. Added condition
  • Added condition Z{Z}Z is strictly convex: for distinct z,z′∈Z{z,z'\in Z}z,z′∈Z and 0<λ<1,{0<\lambda<1},0<λ<1, λz+(1−λ)z′{\lambda z+(1-\lambda)z'}λz+(1−λ)z′ is an interior point of Z.{Z}.Z. Added condition

Answer

With these assumptions the solution exists and is unique at every p≫0,{p\gg 0},p≫0, for every ρ.{\rho}.ρ. Each assumption is needed: an open quadrant, the whole plane, a closed quadrant and the unit disc at the price vector (0,−1){(0,-1)}(0,−1) break the statement when closedness, boundedness, strict convexity or positive prices, respectively, are dropped.

Intuition

Closedness, an upper bound and positive prices make the set of plans that meet the target compact, so a highest output exists. Strict convexity rules out two distinct plans with the same highest output, since their midpoint would lie in the interior of Z{Z}Z and could be pushed to produce more.

(b)

Supply is monotonic in prices

Printed under unstated reasonable assumptions; without them output can fall as its own price rises.

As printed

As printed: under reasonable assumptions, the supply function of the output-maximizing producer with a profit target satisfies monotonicity in prices. The assumptions are not stated.

Answer

Without stated assumptions this does not hold in full generality. Counterexample: one input, a technology with the two plans (0,−1){(0,-1)}(0,−1) and (10,−2){(10,-2)}(10,−2) (input coordinate first), ρ=−3/2,{\rho=-3/2},ρ=−3/2, and prices (1,1){(1,1)}(1,1) and (1,3),{(1,3)},(1,3), which differ only in the price of the produced commodity. The problem has a unique solution at each price, yet the output falls from −1{-1}−1 to −2{-2}−2 as its own price rises. The corrected statement adds that every plan produces a nonnegative amount of the output.

Intuition

When plans may use up the produced commodity, a higher price of that commodity can make the high-output plans unaffordable under the profit target and push the producer to a plan with lower output.

Output rises with its own price and falls with an input’s price

When every plan yields a nonnegative output, output is nondecreasing in its own price, and nonincreasing in the price of a commodity every plan uses as an input.

Corrected statement

Suppose every plan in Z{Z}Z yields a nonnegative output, zK≥0.{z_K\ge 0}.zK​≥0. Let z{z}z solve the problem at prices p{p}p and z′{z'}z′ at prices p′,{p'},p′, with the same ρ.{\rho}.ρ.

Answer

With these conditions, supply is monotonic in prices: output is nondecreasing in its own price and nonincreasing in the price of an input, for every profit target and whichever solutions are chosen. Without nonnegative output or without the input condition, two-plan technologies reverse each conclusion.

Intuition

A higher output price raises the profit of every plan with positive output, so the set of plans meeting the target can only grow and the best output can only rise. A higher input price lowers the profit of every plan that uses the input, so that set can only shrink.

(c)

Supply is continuous in prices when ρ = 0

Printed under unstated reasonable assumptions; without them supply can jump at prices where a plan just breaks even.

As printed

As printed: under reasonable assumptions, the supply function of the output-maximizing producer with a profit target satisfies continuity in prices when ρ=0.{\rho=0}.ρ=0. The assumptions are not stated.

Answer

Without stated assumptions this does not hold in full generality. Counterexample: one input, a technology with the single plan (−1,1){(-1,1)}(−1,1) and ρ=0.{\rho=0}.ρ=0. At prices (1,1){(1,1)}(1,1) the plan just breaks even and is supplied; at (1,1−t){(1,1-t)}(1,1−t) for any t>0{t>0}t>0 it loses money and the producer stays idle, so supply jumps at (1,1).{(1,1)}.(1,1). The corrected statement adds that the technology is closed, contains the idle plan and is strictly convex, and that prices are positive.

Intuition

With a single isolated plan, the producer switches between that plan and inactivity as its profit crosses zero, so supply jumps there.

Supply is continuous at ρ = 0 for closed, strictly convex technologies

If the technology is closed, strictly convex and allows inactivity, the solution at profit target zero varies continuously with positive prices.

(d)

Supply is monotonic in the profit target

Printed under unstated reasonable assumptions; without them output can first fall and then rise as the target rises.

As printed

As printed: under reasonable assumptions, the supply function of the output-maximizing producer with a profit target is monotonic in ρ.{\rho}.ρ. The assumptions are not stated.

Answer

Without stated assumptions this does not hold in full generality. Counterexample: one input, a technology with the two plans (−10,5){(-10,5)}(−10,5) and (0,−3),{(0,-3)},(0,−3), prices (1,1).{(1,1)}.(1,1). The output is 5{5}5 at ρ=−6,{\rho=-6},ρ=−6, −3{-3}−3 at ρ=−4{\rho=-4}ρ=−4 and 0{0}0 at ρ=−2{\rho=-2}ρ=−2: it first falls and then rises. The corrected statement adds that every plan produces a nonnegative amount of the output.

Intuition

Once negative outputs are possible, the idle default can beat the plans that still meet a higher target, so output need not move in one direction as the target rises.

Output falls weakly as the profit target rises

When every plan yields a nonnegative output, the output chosen at a higher profit target is at most the output chosen at a lower one.

Corrected statement

A producer has a technology Z⊆RL+1{Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{L+1}}Z⊆RL+1 (inputs 1,…,L,{1,\dots,L},1,…,L, output K=L+1{K=L+1}K=L+1) and faces prices p.{p}.p. If ρ≤ρ′,{\rho\le\rho'},ρ≤ρ′, z{z}z solves the problem at ρ{\rho}ρ and z′{z'}z′ solves it at ρ′,{\rho'},ρ′, both at the same prices p,{p},p, then zK′≤zK.{z'_K\le z_K}.zK′​≤zK​.

Assumptions

  • Added condition Every plan yields a nonnegative output: zK≥0{z_K\ge 0}zK​≥0 for all z∈Z.{z\in Z}.z∈Z. Added condition
  • The profit targets are ordered: ρ≤ρ′.{\rho\le\rho'}.ρ≤ρ′. Matches the source

Answer

With this condition, supply is monotonic in ρ{\rho}ρ: output is nonincreasing in the profit target, at any prices and whichever solutions are chosen.

Intuition

A higher target can only shrink the set of plans that meet it, so the highest output in that set can only fall; and if no plan meets the target, the idle plan produces 0,{0},0, which is no more than any feasible output.
