Problem Rubinstein (2012), Problem Set 6, Problem 1, p. 94

3WZA

Is v = w/p₁ + w/p₂ a rational consumer’s indirect utility?

Roy’s identity turns v = w/p₁ + w/p₂ into a demand; the utility √x₁ + √x₂ generates that demand with indirect utility √v, and its square recovers v exactly.

Problem Consumer demand and duality Rubinstein (2012)

The question

A paper uses the indirect utility function v(p1,p2,w)=w/p1+w/p2.{v(p_1,p_2,w) = w/p_1 + w/p_2}.v(p1​,p2​,w)=w/p1​+w/p2​. Check that it is consistent with the model of the rational consumer.

  • (a) Use Roy’s identity to derive the demand function.
  • (b) Show that if this demand is derived from a smooth utility function, the indifference curve at (x1,x2){(x_1,x_2)}(x1​,x2​) has slope −x2/x1.{-\sqrt{x_2}/\sqrt{x_1}}.−x2​​/x1​​.
  • (c) Construct a utility function whose ratio of partial derivatives at (x1,x2){(x_1,x_2)}(x1​,x2​) is x2/x1.{\sqrt{x_2}/\sqrt{x_1}}.x2​​/x1​​.
  • (d) Compute the indirect utility of that function. Is it the original v{v}v? If not, can v{v}v be derived from another utility function with the property in (c)?

(a)

Roy’s identity applied to v = w/p₁ + w/p₂

The function v = w/p₁ + w/p₂ is differentiable, and the ratio in Roy’s identity gives the demand of a consumer who spends more on the cheaper good.

(d2)

The utility of part (c) does not return the original v

Having the ratio of part (c) and indirect utility √v does not give indirect utility v: the first computation does not arrive at the original function.

Another utility with the same ratio yields exactly v

Some utility with the marginal-utility ratio of part (c) has indirect utility exactly w/p₁ + w/p₂, so the original v is consistent with rational choice.
