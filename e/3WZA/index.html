Problem 1

Is v = w/p₁ + w/p₂ a rational consumer’s indirect utility?

Roy’s identity turns v = w/p₁ + w/p₂ into a demand; the utility √x₁ + √x₂ generates that demand with indirect utility √v, and its square recovers v exactly.

Problem Consumer demand and duality Rubinstein

The problem

A paper uses the indirect utility function v(p1​,p2​,w)=w/p1​+w/p2​. Check that it is consistent with the model of the rational consumer.

  1. (a)

    Use Roy’s identity to derive the demand function.

    Machine-checked statement

    Roy’s identity applied to v = w/p₁ + w/p₂#

    Answer

    The demand is, for {i,j}={1,2},

    xi​(p,w)=pi​+pj​pj​​⋅pi​w​.

    The consumer splits wealth in the proportion p1​x1​:p2​x2​=p2​:p1​, so the cheaper good receives the larger share.

    Let v(p1​,p2​,w)=w/p1​+w/p2​. Then v is differentiable at every (p,w) with p1​,p2​,w>0, and there, for {i,j}={1,2}, the ratio in Roy’s identity is

    −∂v/∂w∂v/∂pi​​(p,w)=pi​+pj​pj​​⋅pi​w​.​
    The 2 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    Raising pi​ lowers v at the rate w/pi2​, while extra wealth raises it at the rate 1/p1​+1/p2​. Their ratio is the extra wealth that keeps utility constant per unit rise in pi​, and Roy’s identity says that this equals the quantity of good i bought.

  2. (b)

    Show that if this demand is derived from a smooth utility function, the indifference curve at (x1​,x2​) has slope −x2​​/x1​​.

    Machine-checked statement

    Any smooth utility behind this demand has slope −√x₂/√x₁#

    Answer

    At every demanded bundle (x1​,x2​) the derivative of u along (1,−x2​​/x1​​) vanishes, so the indifference curve there has slope −x2​​/x1​​.

    Let x(p,w) be the demand of part (a) and let u:R+2​→R be differentiable at every bundle x≫0. Suppose that for all p≫0 and w>0 the bundle x(p,w) maximizes u on the budget set B(p,w)={y∈R+2​:p⋅y≤w}. Then at every demanded bundle x=x(p,w) the indifference curve of u has slope −x2​​/x1​​: the derivative of u in the direction (1,−x2​​/x1​​) is zero.

    The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
    Number Assumption In Lean Correspondence
    1 u is differentiable at every x≫0: for some gradient ∇u(x), limε→0​[u(x+εd)−u(x)]/ε=d⋅∇u(x) in every direction d. — Matches the source
    2 The demand of part (a) is derived from u: for all p≫0 and w>0, u(y)≤u(x(p,w)) for every y∈B(p,w). (hderived : ∀ p : Fin 2 → ℝ, (∀ k, 0 < p k) → ∀ w : ℝ, 0 < w → ∀ x : ↥(nonnegOrthant 2), x.val = candidateRoyDemand p w → ∀ y ∈ budgetSet p w, u y ≤ u x) Matches the source

    1 of 2 assumptions are shown beside the line of Lean that states them; the Lean statement below states the others as a whole.

    The 2 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    At an interior optimum the indifference curve is tangent to the budget line, of slope −p1​/p2​. Along the demand of part (a), x2​/x1​=p12​/p22​, so the price ratio can be read off the bundle: p1​/p2​=x2​​/x1​​.

  3. (c)

    Construct a utility function whose ratio of partial derivatives at (x1​,x2​) is x2​​/x1​​.

    Machine-checked statement

    A utility whose marginal-utility ratio is √x₂/√x₁#

  4. (d)

    Compute the indirect utility of that function. Is it the original v? If not, can v be derived from another utility function with the property in (c)?

    Machine-checked statement

    A utility with that ratio has indirect utility √(w/p₁ + w/p₂)#

    Machine-checked statement

    The utility of part (c) does not return the original v#

    Machine-checked statement

    Another utility with the same ratio yields exactly v#
