Majority rule on single-peaked preferences

With an odd number of individuals whose preferences over [0, 1] are single-peaked, pairwise majority voting yields a complete and transitive social preference.

Problem Social choice Rubinstein

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Let X=[0,1] and assume that every individual’s preference is single-peaked: for each i there is an alternative ai∗​ such that b≻i​c whenever ai∗​≥b>c or c>b≥ai∗​. Show that for any odd n, if the domain of preferences is restricted to single-peaked preferences, the majority rule induces a well-behaved social welfare function.

Machine-checked statement

Answer

Shown: the majority relation is complete and transitive, which is how “well-behaved” is read here.

Let X=[0,1] and let N be a finite set with an odd number of individuals. Each individual i has a strict ordering ≻i​ of X that is single-peaked: for some peak ai∗​, b≻i​c whenever ai∗​≥b>c or c>b≥ai∗​. Define the majority relation by x≿y if and only if at least as many individuals prefer x to y as prefer y to x. Then ≿ is a preference relation: complete and transitive.

The definitions it is stated with

Intuition

With single-peaked preferences, of any three alternatives the middle one is nobody’s worst. That restriction rules out Condorcet cycles, and with an odd number of individuals there are no majority ties, so the majority relation is transitive.
