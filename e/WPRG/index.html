Problem MIT 14.121, Problem Set 1, Question 4

WPRG

Do survey answers reveal preferences?

A discussion question on contingent valuation: which preference axioms might survey answers violate, and how could a survey test for such violations?

Informal Behavioral decision theory D91D81
Problem Behavioral decision theory MIT 14.121

The question

A study surveys 2034 Australians to place a dollar value on preserving the Kakadu Conservation Zone and finds benefits far exceeding costs. Critics argue that survey answers may not reflect true preferences: respondents have had little time to think, may worry about the interviewer’s opinion, or may enjoy seeing themselves as virtuous. Using the material of the course, how could one assess whether the survey results reflect true preferences? Which preference axioms might the answers violate, how might a survey be designed to detect such violations, and what should be done if they are found?

Not a formal statement

Not a formal statement

The question asks for a discussion of contingent valuation and of how to test survey answers against the axioms of rational preference. It contains no formal statement to check.

Intuition

Completeness and transitivity can be tested by asking the same respondent several related comparisons and checking that the answers fit together, for example that values are not reversed when the question is framed differently.
