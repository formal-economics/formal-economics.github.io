Kreps's definition of preferences

Taking strict preference as the primitive, with asymmetry and negative transitivity, is equivalent to the traditional definition.

Machine-checked Preferences D01D11 Standard
Problem Preferences Rubinstein (2012)

The question

Kreps (1990) takes as primitive a relation PPP, read “strictly preferred”, and requires asymmetry (for no x,yx, yx,y both x P yx\,P\,yxPy and y P xy\,P\,xyPx) and negative transitivity (if x P yx\,P\,yxPy, then for any zzz, x P zx\,P\,zxPz or z P yz\,P\,yzPy). Explain the sense in which this formalization is equivalent to the traditional definition of a preference relation.

Machine-checked statement

Call a relation PPP on a set XXX a Kreps preference if it is asymmetric (never both x P yx\,P\,yxPy and y P xy\,P\,xyPx) and negatively transitive (if x P yx\,P\,yxPy, then for every zzz, x P zx\,P\,zxPz or z P yz\,P\,yzPy).

  1. If ≿\succsim≿ is a preference relation, its strict part, x≻y  ⟺  (x≿y{x \succ y \iff (x \succsim y}x≻y⟺(x≿y and not y≿x){y \succsim x)}y≿x), is a Kreps preference.
  2. If PPP is a Kreps preference, the relation x≿y  ⟺  ¬ (y P x)x \succsim y \iff \neg\, (y\,P\,x)x≿y⟺¬(yPx) is a preference relation: reflexive, complete and transitive.
  3. The two translations are mutually inverse: from ≿\succsim≿ to its strict part and back returns ≿\succsim≿, and from PPP to ≿\succsim≿ and back to the strict part returns PPP.

Assumptions

  • From the traditional side: ≿\succsim≿ is a preference relation on XXX. Matches the source
  • From Kreps’s side: PPP is asymmetric and negatively transitive. Matches the source

Answer

Proved: both translations, and both round trips, hold pointwise for all x,yx, yx,y. The two primitives carry exactly the same information.

Intuition

“Weakly preferred” is just “not strictly worse”. Asymmetry of PPP turns into completeness and reflexivity of ≿\succsim≿, and negative transitivity of PPP is the contrapositive of transitivity of ≿\succsim≿.
