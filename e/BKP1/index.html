Claim

The prime and dual turtle

A one-dimensional illustration of duality: maximal distance in given time versus minimal time for a given distance.

Rubinstein, Lecture 6: More Economic Agents, p. 86 Consumer demand and duality · JEL D11 Entry BKP1 Citec

Proved as printed · 12 July 2026

Result Consumer demand and duality Rubinstein

01Statement

Claim. Let M:R→R be strictly increasing and continuous, where M(t) is the maximal distance a turtle can travel in time t. Then for all t,x∈R, M(t)=x if and only if t is the least s with x≤M(s).

The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
Number As Rubinstein states it Correspondence In Lean
1 M:R→R gives the maximal distance traveled in each time. (M : ℝ → ℝ) Matches the source
2 M is strictly increasing. (hmono : StrictMono M) Matches the source
3 M is continuous. (hcont : Continuous M) Matches the source
⇒ Then The prime statement (the maximal distance in time t is x) and the dual statement (the minimal time to travel x is t) are equivalent. M t = x ↔ IsLeast {s : ℝ | x ≤ M s} t

An assumption of the source, stated in Lean

Each assumption is stated by the line of Lean beside it. Point at a row to find its line in the full statement below.

Intuition

Strict monotonicity gives the passage from the prime to the dual statement; continuity gives the converse, since a continuous increasing function cannot jump over the distance x. The same logic links utility maximization and expenditure minimization.
