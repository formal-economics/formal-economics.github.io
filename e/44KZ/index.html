Borda rules violate IIA

With at least three alternatives and two individuals, every Borda scoring rule violates independence of irrelevant alternatives.

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Prove that no Borda rule satisfies IIA when there are at least three alternatives and at least two individuals.

Machine-checked statement

Answer

Shown: every Borda rule fails IIA under these conditions.

Let the sets of alternatives X and individuals N be finite, with ∣X∣≥3 and ∣N∣≥2, and let the weights satisfy w(1)>w(2)>⋯>w(∣X∣). Each individual has a strict ordering ≻i​ of X, and ri​(x) is the number of alternatives i ranks at least as high as x, so his top alternative has ri​=1. The Borda rule ranks x≿y if and only if

i∈N∑​w(ri​(x))≥i∈N∑​w(ri​(y)).

Then the Borda rule violates IIA: for some two profiles in which every individual ranks x against y in the same way, society ranks x against y differently.

The definitions it is stated with

Intuition

Moving a third alternative z between x and y in one individual’s ranking changes the gap between the scores of x and y while leaving everyone’s ranking of x against y untouched. With two individuals who disagree about x and y, such a move can flip the social ranking.
