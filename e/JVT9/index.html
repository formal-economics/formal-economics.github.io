Three choice correspondences and the weak axiom

Three correspondences built from medians and maxima, tested against the weak axiom of revealed preference.

Then CCC satisfies the weak axiom — if x,y∈A∩B{x, y \in A \cap B}x,y∈A∩B, x∈C(A){x \in C(A)}x∈C(A) and y∈C(B){y \in C(B)}y∈C(B), then x∈C(B){x \in C(B)}x∈C(B) — for every finite XXX and every VVV, whereas for some finite XXX and some VVV, respectively some pair of orderings, DDD and EEE violate it.

Assumptions

  • XXX is finite; VVV is an arbitrary real function on XXX, ties allowed. Matches the source

Answer

CCC satisfies the weak axiom. DDD need not: four alternatives with distinct values of VVV give a violation. EEE need not: two opposite orderings of three alternatives give a violation.

Intuition

CCC applies a fixed test that ignores the menu — lying in the top half of all of XXX — so it behaves rationally. DDD applies the same test within the menu, and a menu’s median moves as alternatives are added; EEE mixes two maximizations whose winners trade places as the menu changes.
