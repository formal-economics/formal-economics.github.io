Question 1

Recovering utility from the expenditure function

From e(p, u) = p₁p₂u/(p₁ + p₂) one recovers utility on the axes, each indifference curve x₂ = (√u₀ − √x₁)², and finally u = (√x₁ + √x₂)².

MIT 14.121 Problem Set 4, Question 1, p. 1

Problem Consumer demand and duality MIT 14.121

01The problem4 parts

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

A consumer has a continuous, strictly increasing, quasi-concave utility function, and the consumer’s expenditure function is known to be

e(p1​,p2​,u)=p1​+p2​p1​p2​​u.

1 As printed

Bundles costing at most e(1, p₂, u₀) give at most u₀#

Answer

As printed, this does not hold in full generality, because nothing excludes p2​=0. Counterexample: u=(x1​​+x2​​)2 satisfies every assumption; take u0​=1, x1​=0 and p2​=0. The wealth is then 0, yet the bundle (0,2) costs 0 and has utility 2>1. The corrected statement adds p2​>0.

Let u be a utility function on R+2​ that is continuous, strictly increasing and quasi-concave, with expenditure function e(p1​,p2​,uˉ)=p1​p2​uˉ/(p1​+p2​): at all prices p1​,p2​>0, the cheapest bundle at least as good as a bundle x costs exactly e(p1​,p2​,u(x)). Let u0​ be a utility level and 0≤x1​≤u0​. As printed: fixing p1​=1, for a price p2​, every x2​ such that (x1​,x2​) is affordable at prices (1,p2​) with wealth e(1,p2​,u0​)=p2​u0​/(1+p2​) has u(x1​,x2​)≤u0​. No condition is placed on p2​.

Assumptions

As printed, this statement has no Lean proof: it does not hold in full generality. The corrected statement is machine-checked.

Intuition

When good 2 is free, the consumer can take as much of it as she likes on any budget, so the budget no longer caps utility.

2 Corrected statement

With p₂ > 0, bundles costing at most e(1, p₂, u₀) give at most u₀#

Answer

Shown, for every positive price p2​: the values of x2​ affordable with wealth e(1,p2​,u0​) give utility at most u0​.

Let u be a utility function on R+2​ that is continuous, strictly increasing and quasi-concave, with expenditure function e(p1​,p2​,uˉ)=p1​p2​uˉ/(p1​+p2​): at all prices p1​,p2​>0, the cheapest bundle at least as good as a bundle x costs exactly e(p1​,p2​,u(x)). Let u0​ be a utility level, 0≤x1​≤u0​ and p2​>0. Then every x2​ such that (x1​,x2​) is affordable at prices (1,p2​) with wealth e(1,p2​,u0​)=p2​u0​/(1+p2​) has u(x1​,x2​)≤u0​.

The definition it is stated with

Intuition

Wealth e(1,p2​,u0​) is the least cost of reaching u0​. A bundle within that budget with utility above u0​ could, by continuity, be trimmed to a cheaper bundle still reaching u0​, which is impossible.

Machine-checked statement 1 of 5

Utility on the axes: u(x₁, 0) = x₁ and u(0, x₂) = x₂#

Machine-checked statement 2 of 5

Which amounts of good 1 lie on the indifference curve u = u₀#

Machine-checked statement 3 of 5

The largest affordable x₂ bounds the indifference curve from below#

Machine-checked statement 4 of 5

The indifference curve is x₂ = (√u₀ − √x₁)²#

Machine-checked statement 5 of 5

The utility function is u = (√x₁ + √x₂)²#

  1. a

    Show that by considering what happens when one price goes to infinity it is easy to find u(x1​,0) and u(0,x2​).

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  2. b

    For what values of x1​ does there exist a nonnegative x2​ such that u(x1​,x2​)=u0​?

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  3. c

    Let x1​ be in the range identified in part (b). By fixing p1​=1 and considering what happens when the consumer faces prices (1,p2​) and has wealth e(1,p2​,u0​), one can find a set of values of x2​ for which u(x1​,x2​)≤u0​. What do you know about the largest x2​ in this set? Use this approach to describe the indifference curve u(x1​,x2​)=u0​, that is, find the function x2​(x1​,u0​) with u(x1​,x2​(x1​,u0​))=u0​.

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  4. d

    Can you see how to use this information to very quickly find u(x1​,x2​)?

    This part has no machine-checked answer.
