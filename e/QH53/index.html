A convex but discontinuous preference on the line

A preference on the real line that is convex and not continuous; its differentiability depends on whether values may be negative.

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Verify that the preferences represented by u:R→R, u(x)=x for x≤0 and u(x)=1−x for x>0, are convex and differentiable but not continuous.

1 As printed

Convex and differentiable, but not continuous#

Answer

Convexity and the failure of continuity are both proved. Differentiability, as printed, does not hold: the book’s definition asks for a value function with values in R+​, but at x=1 the direction d=−1 is an improvement direction (u(1−λ)=λ>0=u(1)), while d⋅v(1)>0 would require v(1)<0. Allowing the value function to take negative values, the preference is differentiable — the corrected statement.

The preference on R represented by u(x)=x for x≤0 and u(x)=1−x for x>0 is convex and differentiable, but not continuous.

This printed statement has no Lean proof of its own; the corrected statement below is the one machine-checked.

Intuition

To the right of 0, moving left improves, so the good has negative value there; a value function confined to R+​ cannot express that.

2 Corrected statement

Convex and discontinuous; differentiable with a signed value function#

Answer

Proved: convex; not continuous; differentiable with v=+1 on (−∞,0] and v=−1 on (0,∞); and not differentiable with nonnegative values.

Let ≿ on R be represented by u(x)=x for x≤0 and u(x)=1−x for x>0, and let D(x) be its set of improvement directions. Then

  • ≿ is convex: x≿y and α∈(0,1) imply αx+(1−α)y≿y;
  • ≿ is not continuous;
  • for every x there is v:R→R with D(x)={d:d⋅v(x)>0}.

With v restricted to nonnegative values, as in the book’s definition, the last property fails.

As the problem states it

  1. 1 Added condition In the definition of differentiability, the value function may take any sign; the book’s definition requires values in R+K​.

The Lean statement below states these assumptions together.

The definitions it is stated with

Intuition

The utility jumps from 0 up to almost 1 just right of 0, which breaks continuity of the preference; beyond the jump it falls, so the good’s “value” there is negative.
