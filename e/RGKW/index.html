Problem 10 Moderately difficult

A producer–consumer run by three centers

An agent who produces and consumes delegates to three centers: profit maximization delivers his optimum, while maximizing output subject to no loss produces weakly more and leaves him weakly worse off.

Rubinstein, Lecture Notes in Microeconomic Theory Problem Set 6, Problem 10, p. 97

Problem Producers and the firm Rubinstein

01The problem3 parts

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

An agent is both a producer and a consumer. He owns a0​ units of good 1 and can use some of it to produce good 2 with a production function f that is monotonic, continuous and strictly concave; his preferences are monotonic, continuous and convex. Using a≤a0​ units in production, he can consume the bundle (a0​−a,f(a)). A pricing center declares prices (p1​,p2​). A production center takes them as given and follows Rule 1, maximizing profit p2​f(a)−p1​a, or Rule 2, maximizing output subject to not making a loss, p2​f(a)−p1​a≥0. A consumption center treats (a0​−a,f(a)) as an endowment and picks the best bundle it can afford. Prices are chosen to create harmony: the consumption center finds the production center’s outcome optimal.

  1. a

    Show that under Rule 1 the agent consumes the bundle (a0​−a∗,f(a∗)) that maximizes his preferences.

    Machine-checked statement

    Profit maximization at harmony prices yields the agent’s optimum#

    Answer

    Shown: under Rule 1 and harmony, the agent consumes a bundle he weakly prefers to every bundle (a0​−a,f(a)) he could produce.

    An agent owns a0​ units of good 1 and can turn a∈[0,a0​] of them into f(a) units of good 2, so he can consume any bundle in F={(a0​−a,f(a)):0≤a≤a0​}. The function f is nondecreasing, continuous and strictly concave on [0,a0​], and his preference ≿ on R+2​ is monotone, continuous and convex. Prices p create harmony at a bundle x if x is a best bundle in the budget set B(p,p⋅x). Let a∗ maximize profit p2​f(a)−p1​a over [0,a0​] (Rule 1) and let x∗=(a0​−a∗,f(a∗)). If p creates harmony at x∗, then x∗ is a best bundle in F: x∗≿(a0​−a,f(a)) for every a∈[0,a0​].

    Intuition

    Every feasible bundle is worth p1​(a0​−a)+p2​f(a)=p1​a0​+[p2​f(a)−p1​a], so profit maximization makes x∗ the most valuable feasible bundle. Every feasible bundle is therefore affordable with x∗’s value as wealth, and harmony says x∗ is the best affordable one.

  2. b

    Characterize the agent’s consumption under Rule 2.

    Machine-checked statement

    Under Rule 2 the agent buys as if his wealth were max{p₁a₀, p₂f(a₀)}#

    Answer · in our words

    Our answer: the agent consumes the output-maximizing no-loss bundle, and harmony holds exactly when that bundle is his best choice at wealth max{p1​a0​,p2​f(a0​)}: the larger of the value of his whole stock of good 1 and the value of what that stock produces at full scale.

    An agent owns a0​ units of good 1 and can turn a∈[0,a0​] of them into f(a) units of good 2, so he can consume any bundle in F={(a0​−a,f(a)):0≤a≤a0​}. The function f is nondecreasing, continuous and strictly concave on [0,a0​], and his preference ≿ on R+2​ is monotone, continuous and convex. Prices p create harmony at a bundle x if x is a best bundle in the budget set B(p,p⋅x). Say that a follows Rule 2 at prices p if it maximizes f(a) over the a∈[0,a0​] with p2​f(a)−p1​a≥0, and that x is a Rule-2 outcome if x=(a0​−a,f(a)) for such an a. Then for every bundle x: x is a Rule-2 outcome at which p creates harmony if and only if x is a Rule-2 outcome that is a best bundle in

    B(p, max{p1​a0​, p2​f(a0​)}).

    Intuition

    Under Rule 2 the production center runs at full scale when that does not lose money, and otherwise expands until profit is exactly zero. In the first case the outcome is worth p2​f(a0​)≥p1​a0​; in the second it is worth p1​a0​, the value of the initial stock. Either way its value is the larger of the two.
