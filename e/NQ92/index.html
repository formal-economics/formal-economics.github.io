Question 4

A monopolist’s output never rises with marginal cost

For any demand curve whatever, a profit-maximizing monopolist with constant unit cost c produces weakly less when c is higher: a pure revealed-preference argument.

MIT 14.121, Problem Set 5, Question 4, p. 1 Producers and the firm · JEL D21, D24 Entry NQ92 Citec

Answered and proved · 17 October 2026

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Consider a monopolist with cost function c(q)=cq. Use a simple revealed preference argument to show that the monopolist’s output is weakly decreasing in c. (One can do this very generally, without making any assumptions about the demand curve.)

Machine-checked statement

Answer

Shown, with no assumption at all on the demand curve.

Let the demand curve be any set D of price–quantity points (p,x), and let q(c) be an output chosen at each unit cost c. Consider the costs c at which q(c) is the quantity of a point of D that maximizes profit px−cx over D. On those costs q is weakly decreasing:

c<c′ ⟹ q(c)≥q(c′).

Intuition

Let (p,q) be optimal at cost c and (p′,q′) at cost c′. Each plan is at least as profitable as the other at its own cost, and adding the two inequalities leaves (c′−c)(q−q′)≥0. So a higher cost cannot come with a higher output.

Source: Glenn Ellison, 14.121 Microeconomic Theory I, problem sets (2005). Massachusetts Institute of Technology: MIT OpenCourseWare, ocw.mit.edu. Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
