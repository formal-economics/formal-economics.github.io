Linear preferences

For continuous, strongly monotone preferences, quasi-linearity in every commodity forces a linear utility representation.

Rubinstein, Problem Set 4, Problem 4, p. 64 Consumer preferences · JEL D11 Entry 516W Citec

Answered and proved · 9 July 2026

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Complete the proof, for all K, that a continuous preference relation satisfying strong monotonicity and quasi-linearity in all commodities can be represented by a utility function ∑k=1K​αk​xk​ with αk​>0 for all k.

Machine-checked statement

Answer

Proved for every K.

For every number of commodities K: let ≿ on R+K​ be continuous, strongly monotone, and quasi-linear in every commodity — x≿y implies x+εek​≿y+εek​ for every k and every real ε that keeps both bundles in R+K​. Then there is α∈RK with αk​>0 for all k such that

u(x)=k=1∑K​αk​xk​

represents ≿.

Intuition

Quasi-linearity in good k makes trade-offs against good k the same at every bundle. Imposing it in every good leaves no room for curvature: indifference curves are parallel hyperplanes, and monotonicity makes all their weights positive — perfect substitutes.
