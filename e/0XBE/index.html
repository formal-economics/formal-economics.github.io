Problem 4

Convergence of preferences

A sequence of preferences represented by power sums converges to the preference represented by the maximum.

The problem

Let ≿n on R+2​ be represented by un​(x1​,x2​)=x1n​+x2n​. Say that ≿n converges to ≿∗ if for every x and y with x≻∗y there is N such that x≻ny for all n>N. Show that ≿n converges to the preferences represented by max{x1​,x2​}.

Machine-checked statement

Answer

Proved.

On R+2​ let ≿n be represented by un​(x1​,x2​)=x1n​+x2n​ and ≿∗ by u∗(x1​,x2​)=max{x1​,x2​}. Then ≿n converges to ≿∗: for all x,y with x≻∗y there is N such that x≻ny for every n>N.

Technical conditions 3 · needed only to state it formally
  • The sequence is indexed by all natural numbers; the extra term n=0 is never reached by the condition n>N.
  • The notion of convergence is defined for preferences on any set and used here on R+2​.
  • The threshold N is a natural number.
The 8 definitions it is stated with

Intuition

(x1n​+x2n​)1/n→max{x1​,x2​}: as n grows the larger coordinate dominates the sum, and the indifference curves of un​ bend toward the right-angled contours of the max function. A strict ranking under max therefore shows up once n is large enough.
