Question 4

Demand for the utility max{ax, ay} + min{x, y}

With u = max{ax, ay} + min{x, y}, indifference curves are kinked on the diagonal, and Marshallian and Hicksian demand pick a corner, the kink, or a segment by the price ratio.

MIT 14.121, Problem Set 3, Question 4, p. 1 Consumer demand and duality · JEL D11 Entry EN4K Citec

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

A consumer of two goods has utility function u(x,y)=max{ax,ay}+min{x,y}, with 0<a<1.

Machine-checked statement 1

Indifference curves are kinked on the diagonal#

Answer · in our words

Our answer: the indifference curve at level c consists of two straight segments meeting on the diagonal at (c/(1+a),c/(1+a)), with slope −a below the diagonal, down to (c/a,0), and slope −1/a above it, up to (0,c/a).

Let 0<a<1 and u(x,y)=max{ax,ay}+min{x,y} on R+2​. Let ≿ be the preference that u represents. A bundle z is indifferent to b if and only if z lies on the indifference curve at level c=u(b):

{z: z2​≤z1​, az1​+z2​=c}∪ {z: z1​≤z2​, z1​+az2​=c}.​

The definitions it is stated with

Intuition

Below the diagonal good 1 is the larger amount and utility is ax+y, so the goods are perfect substitutes at rate a; above it the roles swap. The curves are therefore convex, with a kink where the two amounts are equal.

Machine-checked statement 2

Marshallian demand: a corner, the kink, or a segment#

Answer · in our words

Our answer: the five cases above. Without positive prices this can fail: at a=1/2, p=(0,1) and w=1, good 1 is free, utility is unbounded on the budget set, and no bundle is demanded.

Let 0<a<1 and u(x,y)=max{ax,ay}+min{x,y} on R+2​. Let p≫0 and let w be any wealth level. A bundle x is in the Marshallian demand at (p,w) if and only if:

  • p2​<ap1​: x=(0,w/p2​);
  • p2​=ap1​: p⋅x=w and x1​≤x2​;
  • ap1​<p2​ and ap2​<p1​: x1​=x2​=w/(p1​+p2​);
  • p1​=ap2​: p⋅x=w and x2​≤x1​;
  • p1​<ap2​: x=(w/p1​,0).
Assumptions and the Lean statement

Intuition

The budget line has slope −p1​/p2​; the indifference curves have slopes −a and −1/a. If p1​/p2​>1/a the budget line is steeper than both parts and only good 2 is bought; if p1​/p2​<a it is flatter than both and only good 1 is bought; in between, the kink is optimal.
