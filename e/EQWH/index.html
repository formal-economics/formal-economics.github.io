Question 3

Quasi-linear preferences have no wealth effects

With preferences quasi-linear in the first good, demand for the other goods ignores wealth, Hicksian demand ignores the target utility, v = w + φ(p) and e = c(u) − φ(p).

MIT 14.121, Problem Set 3, Question 3, p. 1 Consumer demand and duality · JEL D11 Entry EQWH Citec

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Let the consumption set be (−∞,∞)×R+n−1​, and suppose that preferences are strictly convex and quasi-linear in the first good. Normalize p1​=1.

Machine-checked statement 1

Demand for goods 2 to n does not depend on wealth#

Answer

Shown: the Marshallian demand for goods 2,…,n is independent of wealth.

The consumption set is X=R×R+n−1​ with n≥1 goods: any real amount of the first good, and nonnegative amounts of the others. A preference ≿ on X is strictly convex if a≿y, b≿y and a=b imply that every strict mixture of a and b is strictly better than y, and quasi-linear in the first good if x≿y implies x+εe1​≿y+εe1​ for every real ε. Prices are normalized so that p1​=1, and a bundle is demanded at (p,w) if it is affordable, p⋅x≤w, and at least as good as every affordable bundle. If x is demanded at (p,w) and x′ at (p,w′), then, for every good k=2,…,n,

xk​=xk′​.

The definitions it is stated with

Intuition

Raising wealth by Δ shifts the whole budget set by Δ units of the first good. Quasi-linearity shifts the optimum in the same way, and strict convexity makes the optimum unique, so only the amount of the first good changes.

Machine-checked statement 2

Hicksian demand for goods 2 to n does not depend on the target utility#

Answer

Shown: the Hicksian demand for goods 2,…,n is independent of the target utility.

The consumption set is X=R×R+n−1​ with n≥1 goods: any real amount of the first good, and nonnegative amounts of the others. A preference ≿ on X is strictly convex if a≿y, b≿y and a=b imply that every strict mixture of a and b is strictly better than y, and quasi-linear in the first good if x≿y implies x+εe1​≿y+εe1​ for every real ε. Prices are normalized so that p1​=1, and a bundle is demanded at (p,w) if it is affordable, p⋅x≤w, and at least as good as every affordable bundle. Let u represent ≿. A bundle h is a Hicksian demand at (p,uˉ) if u(h)≥uˉ and p⋅h≤p⋅y for every y with u(y)≥uˉ. If h is a Hicksian demand at (p,uˉ) and h′ at (p,uˉ′), then, for every good k=2,…,n,

hk​=hk′​.
Assumptions and the Lean statement

Intuition

The indifference sets of a quasi-linear preference are translates of one another along the first axis, so a higher target is met most cheaply by adding units of the first good alone.
