From indirect utility to welfare and price indexes

From v = I/√(p₁p₂): expenditure ū√(p₁p₂), Hicksian demand, a compensating variation of −400 for a fourfold price rise, and Paasche 8/5 < 2 < Laspeyres 5/2.

Problem Consumer demand and duality MIT 14.121

The problem

Consider a consumer with indirect utility function v(p1​,p2​,I)=I/p1​p2​​.

Machine-checked statement 1 of 5

The expenditure function is e(p, ū) = ū√(p₁p₂)#

Answer · in our words

Our answer: e(p,uˉ)=uˉp1​p2​​, attained exactly when uˉ>0. Both added conditions are needed. With u=2x1​x2​​, at p=(0,1) and uˉ=1 the formula gives 0, yet every bundle reaching level 1 costs more; at p=(1,1) and uˉ=0 the zero bundle reaches the level at cost 0, while the description requires uˉ>0.

Let u be a utility function on R+2​ with indirect utility v(p,I)=I/p1​p2​​: at all prices p≫0 and income I>0, the greatest utility on the budget set is I/p1​p2​​. Let p≫0 and uˉ>u(0). A number E is the least cost of a bundle with utility at least uˉ if and only if uˉ>0 and

E=uˉp1​p2​​.
The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
Number Assumption In Lean Correspondence
1 The indirect utility is v(p,I)=I/p1​p2​​ at all prices p≫0 and income I>0. — Matches the source
2 Added condition. Prices are positive: p≫0. (hp : ∀ k, 0 < p k) Added condition
3 Added condition. The target exceeds the utility of the zero bundle: uˉ>u(0). — Added condition

Technical conditions 1 · needed only to state it formally
  • The question names no utility level; the result holds at every level uˉ above u(0).
The definition it is stated with

Intuition

Expenditure inverts indirect utility in income: solving uˉ=e/p1​p2​​ for e gives e=uˉp1​p2​​.

Machine-checked statement 2 of 5

The Hicksian demand splits spending equally#

Answer · in our words

Our answer: h(p,uˉ)=((uˉ/2)p2​/p1​​,(uˉ/2)p1​/p2​​) for uˉ>0. Positive prices are needed: with u=2x1​x2​​, p=(−1,−1) and uˉ=2, the formula gives (1,1), yet (2,2) reaches the level at the lower cost −4.

Let u be a utility function on R+2​ with indirect utility v(p,I)=I/p1​p2​​: at all prices p≫0 and income I>0, the greatest utility on the budget set is I/p1​p2​​. Let p≫0 and uˉ>u(0). A bundle h has utility at least uˉ and least cost among such bundles if and only if uˉ>0 and

h=(2uˉ​p1​p2​​​, 2uˉ​p2​p1​​​).
Assumptions and the Lean statement

Intuition

By Shephard’s lemma, Hicksian demand is the price gradient of the expenditure function: the derivative of uˉp1​p2​​ in p1​ is (uˉ/2)p2​/p1​​, and symmetrically for good 2. Each good receives half of the spending.
