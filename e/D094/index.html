Claim

De Finetti’s theorem on subjective probability

Continuous, weakly monotone and additive preferences over bets are expected-payoff maximization under subjective probabilities.

Rubinstein, Lecture 7: Expected Utility, p. 108 Choice under uncertainty · JEL D81 Entry D094 Citec

Proved as printed · 10 July 2026

01Statement

Claim. Let B=RK be the set of bets on K exclusive and exhaustive events, xk​ being the payment if event k occurs, and let ≿ be a preference relation on B. Then ≿ is continuous, weakly monotone (xk​>yk​ for all k implies x≻y) and additive (x≿y implies x+z≿y+z for every bet z) if and only if there is (π1​,…,πK​) with πk​≥0 and ∑k​πk​=1 such that

x≿y⟺k∑​πk​xk​≥k∑​πk​yk​.

Intuition

Additivity makes indifference sets translates of one another, and with continuity they are parallel hyperplanes, so the preference is linear in payments; weak monotonicity makes the weights non-negative, and normalized to sum to one they are subjective probabilities.
