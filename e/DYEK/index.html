Question 2

Pricing against a shifting demand curve

A firm facing demand Q(p, θ) raises its price with θ when log Q has increasing differences, that is, when demand grows more inelastic; concave demand is not necessary for this.

Source
MIT 14.121 Problem Set 6, Question 2, p. 1
Field
Producers and the firm Part II, Consumers and producers · JEL D21, D24
Entry
DYEK Cite it c

Outcome

  1. As printedThe problem, word for word
  2. CorrectedAdds: The unit cost is at most every price in the region: c≤ p for all p∈ P.

Problem Producers and the firm MIT 14.121

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

Let Q(p,θ) be a downward-sloping demand curve, let θ be a parameter of the demand curve, and suppose that Q is differentiable in p and strictly positive in the relevant region of prices. (Maximizing a strictly positive function f is equivalent to maximizing logf.)

1 As printed

Concave demand makes the profit function concave#

Answer

As printed, this does not hold in full generality, because nothing relates the cost c to the prices in P. Counterexample: on P=(0,2) with c=5, the demand Q=5−p2 is concave, decreasing and positive, yet the profit (p−5)(5−p2) has second derivative 10−6p>0 for p<5/3, so it is not concave. The corrected statement adds that c is at most every price in P.

Let P be an open interval of prices and Q(p,θ) a demand curve that, for every θ, is non-increasing, differentiable and strictly positive in p on P. The firm chooses p∈P to maximize the profit (p−c)Q(p,θ) for a constant unit cost c. As printed: concave demand is sufficient to ensure that the profit function is concave. Nothing is said about how c relates to the prices in P.

Assumptions

As printed, this statement has no Lean proof: it does not hold in full generality. The corrected statement is machine-checked.

Intuition

When the firm sells below cost, the margin p−c is negative, and multiplying a concave demand by a negative margin can make the profit convex over part of the region.

2 Corrected statement

With price at least cost, concave demand makes profit concave#

Answer

Shown: with a nonnegative margin on the whole region, concave demand ensures a concave profit function.

Let P be an open interval of prices and Q(p,θ) a demand curve that, for every θ, is non-increasing, differentiable and strictly positive in p on P. The firm chooses p∈P to maximize the profit (p−c)Q(p,θ) for a constant unit cost c. If c≤p for every p∈P, then for every θ: whenever Q(⋅,θ) is concave on P, so is the profit p↦(p−c)Q(p,θ).

Intuition

For smooth demand the profit has second derivative 2Qp​+(p−c)Qpp​. Downward-sloping demand makes the first term nonpositive, and concave demand with a nonnegative margin makes the second nonpositive too.

Demand grows more inelastic iff log Q has increasing differences#

Machine-checked statement 2

Increasing differences of log Q make the price rise with θ#

Machine-checked statement 3

Concave demand is not necessary for the prediction: second reading#

  1. a

    Show that Q becomes more inelastic with θ if and only if logQ(p,θ) has increasing differences.

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  2. b

    Suppose the firm solves maxp​(p−c)Q(p,θ). Derive a sufficient condition for p to increase (weakly) with θ and relate your answer to part (a).

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  3. c

    It is often assumed that the demand curve is concave in p, in order to validate the approach based on first-order conditions. Show that concave demand is sufficient to ensure that the profit function is concave. Can you give an economic justification for concave demand? Is this necessary for the comparative statics prediction?

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

02Related notionsWhat implies what →

  • DDefines Demand becoming more inelastic as the parameter rises
  • DDefines log Q has increasing differences in (price, parameter)
  • DDefines Greatest optimal price nondecreasing in the parameter
  • DDefines Concave demand curve
  • DDefines Concave profit function
  • ⇔Shows Demand becoming more inelastic as the parameter rises is equivalent to log Q has increasing differences in (price, parameter) (demand Q(p,θ)Q(p,\theta)Q(p,θ) decreasing, differentiable and positive in the price on an open interval of prices)
  • ⇒Shows log Q has increasing differences in (price, parameter) implies Greatest optimal price nondecreasing in the parameter (demand Q(p,θ)Q(p,\theta)Q(p,θ) decreasing, differentiable and positive in the price on an open interval of prices; constant marginal cost ccc; profit (p−c) Q(p,θ)(p - c)\,Q(p,\theta)(p−c)Q(p,θ))
  • ⇒Shows Concave demand curve implies Concave profit function (demand Q(p,θ)Q(p,\theta)Q(p,θ) decreasing, differentiable and positive in the price on an open interval of prices; ccc at most every price in the interval (added))
  • ⇏Separates Greatest optimal price nondecreasing in the parameter does not imply Concave demand curve (demand Q(p,θ)Q(p,\theta)Q(p,θ) decreasing, differentiable and positive in the price on an open interval of prices)
