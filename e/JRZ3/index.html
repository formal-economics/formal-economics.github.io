01The question

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

There are two states, s=1,2, and two securities, a stock and a bond, with prices 1 and B≥0. The stock pays Rs​ in state s, and the bond pays r>0 in each state. Assume that R1​>rB>R2​>0.

  1. i

    Prove that this security market is arbitrage-free.

    1 As printed

    The stock-and-bond market is arbitrage-free#

    Answer

    As printed, this does not hold in full generality. Counterexample: (R1​,R2​,r,B)=(3,1,4,1/2) satisfies R1​>rB>R2​>0, since rB=2. Selling one share of the stock and buying one bond costs −1/2, yet pays 1 in state 1 and 3 in state 2. The corrected statements either add R2​B<r<R1​B or rule out short sales.

    There are two states, s=1,2. A stock with price 1 pays Rs​ in state s; a bond with price B≥0 pays r>0 in each state; and R1​>rB>R2​>0. A portfolio holds some amount of each security, and it is an arbitrage if it costs at most 0, pays at least 0 in each state, and either costs strictly less than 0 or pays strictly more than 0 in some state. As printed: this security market is arbitrage-free.

    Assumptions

    As printed, this statement has no Lean proof: it does not hold in full generality. The corrected statement is machine-checked.

    Intuition

    Per unit of money invested, the bond returns r/B and the stock R1​ or R2​. No arbitrage needs the bond’s return to lie strictly between the stock’s two returns, which the printed chain does not imply.

    2 Corrected statement 1

    With R₂B < r < R₁B, the market is arbitrage-free#

    Answer

    Shown: with these two conditions the market admits no arbitrage, and each condition is needed on its own.

    There are two states, s=1,2. A stock with price 1 pays Rs​ in state s; a bond with price B≥0 pays r>0 in each state; and R1​>rB>R2​>0. A portfolio holds some amount of each security, and it is an arbitrage if it costs at most 0, pays at least 0 in each state, and either costs strictly less than 0 or pays strictly more than 0 in some state. If also R2​B<r<R1​B, then no portfolio, short positions included, is an arbitrage.

    • The question places no restriction on portfolios; all are considered, short positions included.
    The definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    Since B>0, the two conditions say R2​<r/B<R1​. Positive state prices then value both securities exactly, so every portfolio costs a positive weighting of its payoffs, and none can be a free gain.

    Corrected statement 2

    Without short sales, the market is arbitrage-free#

  2. ii

    A call option on the stock is added: a third security paying max{0,Rs​−c} in state s, where the strike price satisfies 0<c<R1​. Say as much as you can about the price at which the option would trade in the absence of arbitrage.

    Machine-checked statement 1

    With short sales, the option has a single arbitrage-free price#

    Machine-checked statement 2

    Without short sales, any positive option price is arbitrage-free#

  3. iii

    Does the answer in (ii) depend on the probabilities of the two states? Give intuition for your answer.

    Machine-checked statement 1

    Do the arbitrage-free option prices depend on the probabilities?#

    Machine-checked statement 2

    The arbitrage-free option prices do not depend on the probabilities#

  4. iv

    Does your answer in (ii) change if the stock is removed from the market as the option is introduced? Explain.

    Machine-checked statement 1

    With short sales, does removing the stock change the answer?#

    Machine-checked statement 2

    Without short sales, does removing the stock change the answer?#

    Machine-checked statement 3

    With short sales, removing the stock changes the answer#

    Machine-checked statement 4

    Without short sales, removing the stock changes nothing#
