Bayesian updating and time consistency

With vNM preferences and Bayesian updating, learning that a common outcome will not occur never changes the optimal action; without either, time consistency can fail.

Problem Choice under uncertainty Rubinstein (2012)

The question

A decision maker chooses an action from a set A;{A};A; the set of consequences is Z.{Z}.Z. For every action a,{a},a, a consequence z∗{z^*}z∗ occurs with probability α,{\alpha},α, and each z≠z∗{z\neq z^*}z=z∗ occurs with probability r(a,z)=(1−α)q(a,z).{r(a,z)=(1-\alpha)q(a,z)}.r(a,z)=(1−α)q(a,z).

  • (a) After choosing, he is told that z∗{z^*}z∗ will not occur and is given a chance to change his decision. Show that if he updates by Bayes’ rule and follows the vNM axioms, he will not change his decision.
  • (b) Give an example in which a decision maker whose preferences are not of the expected-utility type is not time-consistent.
  • (c) Give an example in which a decision maker who does not update by Bayes’ rule is not time-consistent.
Parts (a)(b)(c)

(a)

With vNM preferences and Bayes’ rule, the decision never changes

If preferences satisfy independence and continuity and beliefs are updated by Bayes’ rule, the best actions before and after learning that z* will not occur coincide.

(b)

Without expected utility, time consistency can fail

There is a decision situation in which beliefs are updated by Bayes’ rule, the preference is not an expected utility, and the best action changes after the news.

Such an example exists: with a preference that no expected utility represents, Bayesian updating can turn an action that was best before the news into one that is not best after it.

Intuition

Without independence, the common chance of z∗{z^*}z∗ can matter for how the remaining consequences are compared, as in the common-consequence effect of Allais. Removing it then reverses the ranking of actions.

(c)

Without Bayesian updating, time consistency can fail

There is a decision situation in which the preference satisfies the vNM axioms, beliefs are not updated by Bayes’ rule, and the best action changes after the news.

Answer

Such an example exists: even with vNM preferences, updating beliefs other than by Bayes’ rule can make an action that was best before the news stop being best after it.

Intuition

Time consistency in part (a) comes from the fact that the original lotteries are mixtures of the updated ones with a common consequence. If beliefs are revised in some other way, the updated lotteries no longer stand in that relation and the ranking can change.
