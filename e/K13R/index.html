Problem 3

Deciding on Sunday or on Monday

A contingent plan made in advance is a choice among mixed lotteries; under the independence axiom it selects exactly what the decision maker would choose later.

The problem

A decision maker with preferences ≿ over L(Z) learns on Sunday that on Monday he will have to choose either between L1​ and L2​ (with probability α, 0<α<1) or between L3​ and L4​ (with probability 1−α). Under Approach 1 he waits and decides on Monday; under Approach 2 he decides on Sunday what he will do in each case.

  1. (a)

    Formulate Approach 2 as a choice between lotteries.

    Machine-checked statement

    A contingent plan is a choice among mixed lotteries#

    Answer

    Approach 2 is the choice from {αp⊕(1−α)q:p∈{L1​,L2​}, q∈{L3​,L4​}}: a plan that picks p in the first case and q in the second yields the lottery αp⊕(1−α)q.

    Let Z be finite, let L1​,L2​,L3​,L4​∈L(Z) and 0<α<1. Approach 2 is a choice problem over L(Z): a choice from the nonempty menu of the mixtures αp⊕(1−α)q with p∈{L1​,L2​} and q∈{L3​,L4​}, each plan corresponding to the mixture of the two lotteries it would pick.

    1 The set of prizes Z is finite. [Fintype Z] Matches the source

    The 3 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    Seen from Sunday, a plan is a compound lottery: with probability α the first menu arises and the planned option is played, otherwise the second. Reducing it gives a mixture.

  2. (b)

    Show that if his preferences satisfy the independence axiom, his choice under Approach 2 is always the same as under Approach 1.

    Machine-checked statement

    Under independence, planning ahead and waiting choose alike#

    Answer

    Shown: under independence, the plans chosen on Sunday are exactly the combinations of the choices that would be made on Monday, so the two approaches agree.

    Let Z be finite, let ≿ be a preference on L(Z) satisfying the independence axiom, and let L1​,…,L4​∈L(Z) and 0<α<1. Then the best elements of the menu {αp⊕(1−α)q:p∈{L1​,L2​}, q∈{L3​,L4​}} are exactly the mixtures αp⊕(1−α)q in which p is a best element of {L1​,L2​} and q a best element of {L3​,L4​}.

    The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
    Number Assumption In Lean Correspondence
    1 The set of prizes Z is finite. [Fintype Z] Matches the source
    2 The first contingency has probability α, with 0<α<1. (hα₀ : 0 < α) (hα₁ : α < 1) Matches the source
    3 ≿ satisfies independence: p≿q if and only if βp⊕(1−β)r≿βq⊕(1−β)r, for all p,q,r and β∈(0,1). (hI : w.SatisfiesIndependence) Matches the source

    Each assumption is stated by the line of Lean beside it.

    The 5 definitions it is stated with

    Intuition

    Independence lets the decision maker evaluate each contingency separately: improving the choice in one contingency, holding the other fixed, improves the plan, because the two plans are then mixtures of the two choices with the same lottery.
