Choice by rationales (Cherepanov, Feddersen and Sandroni)

Choosing the best of the alternatives one can rationalize is characterized by a weak form of the weak axiom; the warm-glow procedure is an example.

After Cherepanov, Fedderson and Sandroni (2008)

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

A decision maker on a finite set X has in mind an asymmetric, complete relation ≻ (not necessarily transitive) and rationales ≻1​,…,≻K​, each asymmetric and transitive; from A he chooses the ≻-best alternative among those maximal in A for at least one rationale (an alternative x is maximal in A for a relation if it is ranked above every other y∈A). A choice function satisfies WWARP if, for {x,y}⊂B1​⊂B2​ with x=y and c{x,y}=c(B2​)=x, we have c(B1​)=y.

Machine-checked statement 1

The weak weak axiom characterizes choice by rationales#

Answer

Proved, in both directions (part (a)).

Let X be finite and c a choice function on all nonempty subsets of X.

  • c satisfies WWARP if, whenever x=y, {x,y}⊆B1​⊆B2​ and c({x,y})=c(B2​)=x, we have c(B1​)=y.
  • c is rationalized if there are an asymmetric, complete (not necessarily transitive) relation ≻ and finitely many rationales ≻1​,…,≻K​, each asymmetric and transitive, such that c(A) is the ≻-best element among the alternatives that at least one rationale ranks above every other element of A.

Then c satisfies WWARP if and only if it is rationalized.

Intuition

WWARP forbids only the starkest reversal: x chosen over y both from the pair and from a large set, yet y chosen from a set in between. Choosing the best among the alternatives one can “rationalize” produces exactly this much consistency, and one rationale per choice problem suffices for the converse.

Machine-checked statement 2

Part (b): the warm-glow procedure satisfies WWARP#

Answer

Proved.

Let ≿M​ (moral) and ≿S​ (selfish) be preference relations on a finite set X, each with a unique best element in every nonempty A⊆X, and let l:X→X satisfy (i) s≿S​l(s) and (ii) s≿S​s′⇒l(s)≿S​l(s′). In each menu A, with m the ≿M​-best and s the ≿S​-best element, the warm-glow procedure chooses m if m≿S​l(s) and s otherwise. The warm-glow procedure satisfies WWARP: whenever x=y, {x,y}⊆B1​⊆B2​ and c({x,y})=c(B2​)=x, we have c(B1​)=y.

Assumptions and the Lean statement

Intuition

If x wins the large menu it is the moral or the selfish champion there, and it stays that champion on every smaller menu containing it. Because the threshold l(s) moves monotonically with the selfish champion, the comparison that let x beat y on the pair and on the large menu also rules out y in between.

Machine-checked statement 3

Part (c): the warm-glow procedure is rationalized, directly#

  1. a

    Show that a choice function satisfies WWARP iff it is rationalized in this sense (construct one rationale for each choice problem).
