Claim

Independence of the conditions of Arrow’s theorem

Examples show that Pareto, transitivity of the social order, and the presence of three alternatives are each needed.

Rubinstein, Lecture 9: Social Choice, p. 131 Social choice · JEL D71, D72 Entry FJWH Citec

01The statements

Corrected statement

Dropping Pareto or three alternatives admits nondictatorial rules#

  1. (Par.) For any individual i0​ and ∣X∣≥3, the anti-dictatorial SWF, whose social preference is the reverse of i0​’s ordering, satisfies IIA, is not dictatorial, and violates Par.
  2. (∣X∣≥3.) If X has exactly two alternatives and N is finite with at least two individuals, there is an SWF that satisfies Par and IIA and is not dictatorial; the majority rule is one.
  3. With a single individual, no SWF satisfies Par and IIA without being dictatorial.

Conclusion

Par and ∣X∣≥3 cannot be dropped from Arrow’s theorem. Transitivity of the social order is treated in part (iii).

The definitions it is stated with

Intuition

Each ingredient of Arrow’s theorem matters: without Pareto, a reversed dictator works; with only two alternatives, majority rule is transitive and treats individuals symmetrically.

Part (iii)

1 As printed

Majority rule shows that transitivity is indispensable#

The majority rule satisfies IIA and Par for ∣X∣≥3 but can induce a relation that is not transitive regardless of the number of individuals; it is offered as an example of a nondictatorial rule showing that transitivity of the social order cannot be dropped from Arrow’s theorem.

As Rubinstein states it

  1. 1 X has at least three alternatives.
  2. 2 N is a finite set of individuals, with no lower bound on their number.
  3. 3 Individuals have orderings.

Conclusion

As printed, this does not hold in full generality: with no individuals the majority rule violates Par, and with a single individual the majority relation is transitive at every profile and that individual is a dictator. The corrected statement requires at least two individuals.

This printed statement has no Lean proof of its own; the corrected statement below is the one machine-checked.

2 Corrected statement

Majority rule satisfies Pareto and IIA yet may be intransitive#

Part (iv)

1 As printed

With two alternatives, majority rule is nondictatorial#
