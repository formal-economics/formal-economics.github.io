Claim

Roy’s identity

Demand is recovered from the indirect utility function as minus the ratio of its price and wealth derivatives.

Rubinstein, Lecture 6: More Economic Agents, p. 85 Consumer demand and duality · JEL D11 Entry AWMW Citec

01Statement

Claim. Let ≿ be a monotone preference relation on X=R+K​, let x(p,w) be a best bundle of B(p,w) under ≿ for every p≫0 and w>0, and fix p∗≫0 and w∗>0.

  1. The point (p∗,w∗) lies on H={(p,w):p⋅x(p∗,w∗)=w}, and every (p,w)∈H with p≫0, w>0 satisfies (p,w)≿∗(p∗,w∗).
  2. (Roy’s identity.) If ≿∗ is represented by a differentiable indirect utility function v(p,w), then for every k:
−∂v/∂w(p∗,w∗)∂v/∂pk​(p∗,w∗)​=xk​(p∗,w∗).

Technical conditions needed only to state it formally
  • The marginal utility of wealth ∂v/∂w at (p∗,w∗) is nonzero, so that the ratio in Roy’s identity is defined.

Conclusion

Part 1: H is a supporting hyperplane of the indirect preference at (p∗,w∗). Part 2: Roy’s identity holds.

Intuition

Along H the old bundle x(p∗,w∗) remains exactly affordable, so the consumer is at least as well off everywhere on H. Hence H is tangent to the indifference surface of v at (p∗,w∗), and comparing slopes gives Roy’s identity.
