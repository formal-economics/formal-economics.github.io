Problem 6 Moderately difficult

Who is an economist?

Aggregating the faculty’s views of who is an economist: with three or more members, consensus and independence leave only methods that copy one member’s view.

01The problem

Hide every answer, then reveal them one part at a time.

This problem follows Kasher and Rubinstein (1997). Who counts as an economist? View this as pooling the opinions of a department’s members. The faculty is N={1,…,n} with n≥3. Member i∈N reports a set Ei​ satisfying ∅⊂Ei​⊂N, namely those he regards as “real economists”. An aggregation method F sends every profile (E1​,…,En​) to a proper nonempty subset of N, read as the members the group regards as economists. The axioms:

Consensus: whenever j∈Ei​ for every i∈N, also j∈F(E1​,…,En​); whenever j∈/Ei​ for every i∈N, also j∈/F(E1​,…,En​).

Independence: whenever two profiles (E1​,…,En​) and (G1​,…,Gn​) satisfy [j∈Ei​⟺j∈Gi​] for every i, we have [j∈F(E1​,…,En​)⟺j∈F(G1​,…,Gn​)].

Machine-checked statement

Consensus and independence leave only dictatorships#

Answer

Shown: the methods satisfying both axioms are exactly those that copy the view of one fixed member.

Let N be a finite society with at least three members. A profile gives each member i a view Ei​ with ∅⊊Ei​⊊N, and an aggregation method F assigns to every profile a set F(E) with ∅⊊F(E)⊊N. Consensus: a member whom everyone counts as an economist is in F(E), and one whom nobody counts is not. Independence: whether j∈F(E) depends only on which members count j as an economist. Then F satisfies Consensus and Independence if and only if there is a member i∗ with F(E)=Ei∗​ for every profile E.

Intuition

By Independence, whether j is an economist depends only on who considers j one. As in Arrow’s theorem, Consensus and the requirement that the outcome be a proper nonempty subset force the coalitions whose verdict prevails to shrink to a single member, whose view is then always adopted.

  2. b

    Give a method meeting Consensus but violating Independence, and a second method meeting Independence but violating Consensus.

    Machine-checked statement

    A method with Consensus but not Independence#

    Answer

    Such a method exists, so Independence does not follow from Consensus.

    Let N be a finite society with at least three members. A profile gives each member i a view Ei​ with ∅⊊Ei​⊊N, and an aggregation method F assigns to every profile a set F(E) with ∅⊊F(E)⊊N. Consensus: a member whom everyone counts as an economist is in F(E), and one whom nobody counts is not. Independence: whether j∈F(E) depends only on which members count j as an economist. Then some aggregation method satisfies Consensus but not Independence.
