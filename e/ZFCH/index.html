Question 2

Substitutes in compensated demand

Substitutability need not be symmetric or the same at all prices; with enough regularity every good has a substitute; coffee can raise or lower what one pays to keep tea.

As printed, this statement does not hold in full generality; a corrected companion is proved.

Problem Consumer demand and duality MIT 14.121

The problem

Goods i and j are said to be substitutes at (p,u) if ∂hi​(p,u)/∂pj​≥0.

1 As printed

Every good has at least one substitute#

Answer

As printed, this does not hold in full generality. Counterexample: with a single good there is no other good to be its substitute. Other counterexamples use a negative price, or a level that no bundle reaches, so that Hicksian demand is empty. The corrected statement adds at least two goods, positive prices, and a Hicksian demand that is a single bundle near p and differentiable at p.

For a utility function u on R+L​, the Hicksian demand h(p,uˉ) consists of the bundles of least cost p⋅x among those with u(x)≥uˉ. Goods i and j are substitutes at (p,uˉ) if, for good-j prices near pj​ with the other prices fixed, the Hicksian demand is a single bundle whose i-th coordinate has a derivative ∂hi​/∂pj​≥0 at pj​. As printed: for every utility function u, price vector p, level uˉ and good i, there is a good j=i such that i and j are substitutes at (p,uˉ).

Assumptions and the Lean statement

Intuition

The argument needs room to work: another good to absorb the substitution, and a demand regular enough to have the derivatives that define substitutes.

2 Corrected statement

With at least two goods, every good has a substitute#

Answer

Shown: under these conditions every good has at least one substitute.

For a utility function u on R+L​, the Hicksian demand h(p,uˉ) consists of the bundles of least cost p⋅x among those with u(x)≥uˉ. Goods i and j are substitutes at (p,uˉ) if, for good-j prices near pj​ with the other prices fixed, the Hicksian demand is a single bundle whose i-th coordinate has a derivative ∂hi​/∂pj​≥0 at pj​. Suppose there are at least two goods, p≫0, and near p the Hicksian demand at level uˉ is a single bundle, each coordinate of which is differentiable at p. Then every good i has a good j=i such that i and j are substitutes at (p,uˉ).

Technical conditions 1 · needed only to state it formally
  • The question names no utility function; the result holds for every utility function on R+L​.
The 2 definitions it is stated with

Intuition

Hicksian demand does not change when all prices are scaled together, so ∑j​pj​∂hi​/∂pj​=0. The own-price term is never positive, so with positive prices some cross term ∂hi​/∂pj​, j=i, must be nonnegative.
