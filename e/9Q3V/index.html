Definition

Nonsatiation

Arbitrarily close to a bundle there is a strictly better one.

Source
Rubinstein, Lecture Notes in Microeconomic Theory Lecture 4: Consumer Preferences, p. 49
Topic
Consumer preferences Consumers and producers · JEL D11
Status
Machine-checked First checked 9 July 2026
Entry
9Q3V Cite c
Definition Consumer preferences Rubinstein

Definition. A preference relation ≿ on X=R+K​ is nonsatiated at the bundle y if for every ε>0 there is x∈X with d(x,y)<ε and x≻y, where d(x,y)=∑k​(xk​−yk​)2​ is the Euclidean distance.

The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
Number Assumption In Lean Correspondence
1 The commodity space is X=R+K​, bundles of K goods. — Matches the source
2 ≿ is a preference relation on X. (w : WeakPreference ↥(nonnegOrthant K)) Matches the source
3 y is a bundle in X. (y : ↥(nonnegOrthant K)) Matches the source

2 of 3 assumptions are shown beside the line of Lean that states them; the Lean statement below states the others as a whole.

Intuition

There is no bliss point at y: an arbitrarily small change can make the consumer strictly better off.
