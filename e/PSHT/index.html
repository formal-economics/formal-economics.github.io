Adam’s apples

Four mild-looking conditions on preferences over apple streams imply that Adam would give up two apples a day from day 4 onward for a single apple today.

After Rubinstein (2002)

01The problem4 parts

Based on Rubinstein (2002). Adam lives in the Garden of Eden and eats only apples. Time is discrete (t=1,2,…), apples come in discrete units, and Adam has preferences over streams of apple consumption. Assume:

Machine-checked statement

Answer

Shown, for a life of any finite length T: Adam weakly prefers one apple right away to two apples a day from day 4 to the end of his life, so he would accept the exchange.

Let ≿ be a preference over streams (x1​,…,xT​) of apples, xt​∈{0,1,2,…}, over a finite life of T days, satisfying:

  • (a) one more apple on a day with at most one apple is strictly better, and a third apple on a day with two is strictly worse;
  • (b) if xt​≤1 and xt+1​≥1, moving one apple from day t+1 to day t is strictly better;
  • (c) if xt​=0 and xt+1​≥2, getting one apple on day t in exchange for two on day t+1 is strictly better.

Then one apple on day 1 and nothing afterwards is weakly preferred to two apples a day from day 4 to day T.

The 6 definitions it is stated with

Intuition

Conditions (b) and (c) let consumption be pulled one day earlier at a strict gain, condition (c) at the steep rate of one apple today for two tomorrow. Chaining such moves pulls the generous stream earlier and merges it, two neighbouring apples at a time, into a single early apple, with Adam better off at every step.

    Adam likes to eat up to 2 apples a day and cannot bear to eat 3 apples a day;

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  2. b

    Adam is impatient: he would be delighted to increase his consumption on day t from 0 to 1, or from 1 to 2, at the expense of an apple promised a day later;

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  3. c

    on any day on which he has no apple, he would prefer 1 apple immediately to 2 apples tomorrow;

    This part has no machine-checked answer.

  4. d

    Adam expects to live for 120 years.

    Show that if Adam is offered a stream of 2 apples a day starting on day 4 for the rest of his expected life, he would be willing to exchange that offer for 1 apple right away.

    This part has no machine-checked answer.
