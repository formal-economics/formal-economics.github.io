Claim

Satisficing satisfies condition α

The satisficing procedure produces choices consistent with condition α.

Source
Rubinstein, Lecture Notes in Microeconomic Theory Lecture 3: Choice, p. 29
Topic
Choice and rationalizability Individual choice · JEL D01, D11
Status
Machine-checked First checked 5 July 2026
Entry
GPFF Cite c
Result Choice and rationalizability Rubinstein

Claim. Let (X,D) be a context, v a valuation, v∗ a threshold and O an ordering of X such that every A∈D has a unique satisficing choice. Then the satisficing choice function Cv,v∗,O​ satisfies condition α.

The assumptions of the statement, each beside the line of the Lean statement that states it
Number Assumption In Lean Correspondence
1 (X,D) is a context. (ctx : Context X) Matches the source
2 v:X→R is a valuation and v∗∈R a threshold. (v : X → ℝ) Matches the source
3 O is an ordering of X (complete, asymmetric and transitive). (hO : IsCompleteOrdering O) Matches the source
4 Every A∈D has exactly one satisficing choice. (hwd : ∀ A ∈ ctx.domain, ∃! a, IsSatisficingChoice O v vStar A a) Matches the source
⇒ Then Cv,v∗,O​ satisfies condition α. (satisficingChoiceFunction ctx v vStar O hO hwd).SatisfiesConditionAlpha

Each assumption is stated by the line of Lean beside it.

Intuition

If the alternative picked from B is still available in a smaller problem A, the search through A stops at the same alternative: either it is the first satisfactory one in both, or no alternative in either is satisfactory and it is the last one in both.
