Arrow’s impossibility theorem

Rubinstein, Lecture 9, p. 131

As printed

Let N be a finite set of individuals and let X contain at least three alternatives. If a social welfare function F satisfies Par and IIA, then F is dictatorial: there is an individual i∗ such that for every profile and all x,y∈X, society strictly prefers x to y if and only if x≻i∗​y.

In Lean

theorem arrow_impossibility {X N : Type*} [Finite N] (F : SWF X N)
    (hX : ∃ a b c : X, a ≠ b ∧ a ≠ c ∧ b ≠ c) (hPar : Par F) (hIIA : IIA F) :
    Dictatorial F

Debreu’s theorem

Rubinstein, Lecture 2, p. 18

Let X⊆Rn be convex and let ≿ be a continuous preference relation on X. Then there is a continuous function U:X→R that represents ≿.

Expected utility theorem

Rubinstein, Lecture 7, p. 104

As printed

Let Z be a finite set of prizes and ≿ a preference relation on L(Z) that satisfies the independence axiom and the continuity axiom. Then there are numbers (v(z))z∈Z​ such that the expected value

U(p)=z∈Z∑​p(z)v(z)

represents ≿: for all p,q∈L(Z), p≿q if and only if U(p)≥U(q).

In Lean

theorem _root_.Rubinstein.Lecture01.WeakPreference.exists_expectedUtility_representation
    (w : WeakPreference (Lottery Z)) (hI : w.SatisfiesIndependence)
    (hC : w.SatisfiesContinuity) :
    ∃ v : Z → ℝ, Represents w (Lottery.expectedUtility v)

Roy’s identity

Rubinstein, Lecture 6, p. 85

As printed

Let ≿ be a monotone preference relation on X=R+K​, let x(p,w) be a best bundle of B(p,w) under ≿ for every p≫0 and w>0, and fix p∗≫0 and w∗>0.

  1. The point (p∗,w∗) lies on H={(p,w):p⋅x(p∗,w∗)=w}, and every (p,w)∈H with p≫0, w>0 satisfies (p,w)≿∗(p∗,w∗).
  2. (Roy’s identity.) If ≿∗ is represented by a differentiable indirect utility function v(p,w), then for every k:
−∂v/∂w(p∗,w∗)∂v/∂pk​(p∗,w∗)​=xk​(p∗,w∗).

Slutsky’s compensated law of demand

Rubinstein, Lecture 5, p. 78

As printed

Let x be a demand function (with x(p,w)∈B(p,w), homogeneous of degree zero) that satisfies Walras’ law and the weak axiom of revealed preference. Let p≫0 and w>0, let p′≫0, and let w′=p′⋅x(p,w) be the compensated wealth. Then either x(p′,w′)=x(p,w), or

(p′−p)⋅(x(p′,w′)−x(p,w))<0.

In Lean

Hotelling’s lemmaHomogeneity, continuity and convexity of the profit function; its derivative is the supply. Gibbard–Satterthwaite theoremMuller–Satterthwaite for monotone social welfare functions, Gibbard–Satterthwaite for strategy-proof social choice, Dutta–Jackson–Le Breton for procedure-invariant choice rules. First-order stochastic dominanceA lottery is preferred by every expected-utility maximizer who likes money exactly when its distribution function lies below the other’s. Risk aversion and concavityA vNM preference is risk averse exactly when its utility is concave, once the prizes form an interval. Constant absolute risk aversionMonotone, risk-averse preferences that are invariant to wealth have linear or exponential utility on the real line. Consumer dualityFor monotone and continuous preferences, a bundle solves the prime problem exactly when it solves the corresponding dual problem. The lexicographic counterexampleThe lexicographic preference on the unit square is complete and transitive, yet no real-valued function represents it. Rationalizability by α and βIf the domain contains every menu of at most three alternatives, a choice correspondence satisfying α and β is the set of best elements of a preference relation. Existence of demandA continuous preference attains a best bundle in every budget set with positive prices and wealth. Uniqueness of demandConvex preferences give a convex set of optimal bundles; strictly convex preferences give at most one. Utility on countable setsEvery preference relation on a countable set has a utility representation with values in (0, 1). Utility is ordinalComposing a utility function with any strictly increasing function gives another representation of the same preference. Convexity and quasi-concavityA represented preference is convex exactly when its utility function is quasi-concave. Homothetic utilityHomothetic, continuous and monotone preferences have a continuous utility that is homogeneous of degree one. The method

How every entry is checked

Stated as printed, proved by machine, with nothing added in between.

  1. As printed

    Each statement is written out exactly as its source prints it, with the page, so you can hold it against the book.

  2. In Lean

    The same statement is written in the Lean proof assistant. Each assumption of the source is matched to one line of Lean, and nothing is added.

  3. Checked

    Lean verifies every step of the proof. Where the printed statement is false, the counterexample is checked too, beside a corrected version.
