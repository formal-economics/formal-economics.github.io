Part II · Consumers and producers

Consumer preferences

Preferences over bundles carry economic structure: monotonicity, continuity, convexity, homotheticity, quasi-linearity and differentiability. Each has a counterpart in utility, such as quasi-concavity for convexity and homogeneity for homotheticity.

Monotonicity, convexity, continuity and differentiability of preferences over bundles.

Definitions20

  • MGX1 Monotone preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked More of every good is at least as good; strictly more of every good is strictly better.
  • 90P0 Strongly monotone preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked Any increase in any good, keeping the others, is strictly better.
  • 9Q3V Nonsatiation Lecture 4 Machine-checked Arbitrarily close to a bundle there is a strictly better one.
  • YD1M Continuity of consumer preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked Strict preferences between bundles survive small changes in either bundle.
  • E0BR Convex preferences: first definition Lecture 4 Machine-checked Moving toward a weakly better bundle never makes things worse.
  • QY3Y Convex preferences: second definition Lecture 4 Machine-checked A mixture of two bundles is at least as good as one of them.
  • ABKE Convex preferences: third definition Lecture 4 Machine-checked Upper contour sets are convex.
  • D7P4 Strictly convex preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked Strict mixtures of distinct bundles that are at least as good as y are strictly better than y.
  • PQ80 Concave and quasi-concave functions Lecture 4 Informal A reminder of the mathematical definitions used in the link between convexity and utility.
  • T01P Λ-convex preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked Convexity as the existence, at every alternative, of a criterion that must improve.
  • 0T4N Λ-strictly convex preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked The strict version of Λ-convexity, covering moves to equally good alternatives.
  • 815D Single-peaked preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked Preferences on an interval with a unique ideal point, declining on both sides.
  • X4NB Algebraic linear orderings Lecture 4 Machine-checked Orderings of bundles by a fixed linear value vector.
  • GTMM Homothetic preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked Scaling two bundles by the same factor preserves their comparison.
  • 3WW2 Quasi-linear preferences Lecture 4 Corrected Adding the same amount of the numeraire good to two bundles preserves their comparison.
  • QVFF Quasi-linearity in all commodities Lecture 4 Machine-checked Every good acts like money: adding a common amount of any good preserves comparisons.
  • BV7P Directions valued by subjective marginal values Lecture 4 Machine-checked The directions with positive value under a vector of subjective marginal values.
  • 8B31 Improvement directions Lecture 4 Machine-checked The directions in which small moves make the consumer better off.
  • RCCW Differentiable preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked A preference is differentiable when its improvement directions are those with positive value under a marginal-value vector.
  • A4FG Λ-convex preferences Richter and Rubinstein (2019) Machine-checked Richter and Rubinstein’s definition of convexity without algebra: b is at least as good as a whenever, by every criterion, b beats something at least as good as a.

Results15

  • JADN Utility representation of monotone continuous preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked Monotone and continuous preferences over bundles have a continuous utility representation.
  • Y2RM Equivalence of the three definitions of convexity Lecture 4 Machine-checked The three definitions of convex preferences coincide for every preference relation.
  • KKZP Convexity and quasi-concave utility Lecture 4 Machine-checked A represented preference is convex exactly when its utility function is quasi-concave.
  • 1P2B Convex preferences need not have a concave utility Lecture 4 Corrected Some continuous and convex preference on ℝ is represented by no concave function.
  • C6DS Λ-convexity on finite sets: the bottom-up construction Lecture 4 Machine-checked On a finite set, Λ-convex preferences are exactly those built by placing Λ-minimal alternatives one at a time.
  • Q5N3 Single-peaked preferences as Λ-strictly convex preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked On [0, 1], continuous single-peaked preferences are the Λ-strictly convex ones for the left and right criteria.
  • NPWN Convexity and Ψ-convexity Lecture 4 Corrected Ψ-convexity, the separating-hyperplane form of convexity, implies convexity; the converse needs interior bundles.
  • A62M Utility representation of homothetic preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked Homothetic, continuous and monotone preferences have a continuous utility that is homogeneous of degree one.
  • 9RT8 Utility representation of quasi-linear preferences Lecture 4 Machine-checked Monotone, continuous preferences that are quasi-linear and strongly monotone in good 1 have a utility equal to the amount of good 1 plus a function of the other goods.
  • Y4VK Valuing a bundle in units of the numeraire Lecture 4 Machine-checked For monotone, continuous preferences that are quasi-linear and strongly monotone in good 1, every bundle without good 1 is indifferent to some amount of good 1 alone.
  • A7N1 Linear utility from quasi-linearity in all goods Lecture 4 Machine-checked Continuous, strongly monotone preferences that are quasi-linear in every good have a positive linear utility.
  • 2V58 Properties of improvement directions Lecture 4 Machine-checked Four basic facts about improvement directions under scaling, convexity and monotonicity.
  • ZA82 Differentiable utility and differentiable preferences Lecture 4 Open Stated in the text: a differentiable, strongly increasing, quasi-concave utility gives a differentiable preference; a machine-checked proof is still open.
  • R0PH Standard convexity as Ψ-convexity Richter and Rubinstein (2019) Machine-checked On a convex, closed subset of ℝᴺ, a continuous preference is convex in the usual sense exactly when it is Λ-convex for the linear orderings x ↦ v · x.
  • CTK2 Two definitions of Λ-convexity compared Richter and Rubinstein (2019) Machine-checked The textbook’s Λ-convexity, read with the strict parts of the orderings, implies Richter and Rubinstein’s; the converse does not hold in general.

Problems10

  • 9YEJ Strict convexity on the unit interval Problem Set 4 Machine-checked On the unit interval, strictly convex continuous preferences are the single-peaked ones.
  • N79K Monotone, quasi-linear and homothetic preferences Problem Set 4 Machine-checked With two goods and no continuity, three classic properties pin preferences down to lexicographic rankings by linear indices.
  • N5T6 Convexity as a diminishing rate of substitution Problem Set 4 Machine-checked A discrete diminishing-rate-of-substitution condition is equivalent to convexity for strongly monotone, continuous preferences.
  • 516W Linear preferences Problem Set 4 Machine-checked For continuous, strongly monotone preferences, quasi-linearity in every commodity forces a linear utility representation.
  • 3HYA Quasi-linear preferences and the numeraire Problem Set 4 Machine-checked Under continuity and monotonicity, with quasi-linearity and strong monotonicity in good 1, every bundle can be priced in units of good 1.
  • KE3Z Separable preferences Problem Set 4 Machine-checked Additive utility implies independence across groups of goods and the hexagon condition, but independence alone is not enough for additivity.
  • BWAT Differentiable preferences Problem Set 4 Corrected The book's direction-based notion of differentiable preferences, tested on lexicographic, flat and discontinuous examples.
  • QH53 A convex but discontinuous preference on the line Problem Set 4 Corrected A preference on the real line that is convex and not continuous; its differentiability depends on whether values may be negative.
  • MQ4V Λ-strictly convex preferences on the unit interval Problem Set 4 Machine-checked For the two monotone orderings of the unit interval, Λ-strict convexity is ordinary strict convexity.
  • Q2AC Convexity through strict upper contour sets Problem Set 4 Corrected Convex preferences can be tested on strict upper contour sets, provided the set of alternatives is convex.

Also relevant6

  • XPXN Strictly concave preferences Problem Set 5 Corrected Consumers who prefer extremes to mixtures choose corners, and their demand jumps at a threshold price.
  • Z1TD Continuity of the Hicksian demand Problem Set 6 Machine-checked Monotone, continuous and strictly convex preferences make the Hicksian demand h(p, z) continuous in prices and in the reference bundle.
  • ASDQ Preferences over portfolios of risky assets Problem Set 8 Corrected A risk-averse expected-utility investor’s ranking of asset holdings is convex, and monotone once there is at least one asset.
  • S9ZZ CES demand and the elasticity of substitution MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 3 Corrected CES demand and indirect utility, their linear and Leontief limits, and the elasticity of substitution: 1/(1 − ρ) for ρ ≠ 0, 1; 0 for Leontief; 1 for Cobb–Douglas; none for linear.
  • EQWH Quasi-linear preferences have no wealth effects MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 3 Machine-checked With preferences quasi-linear in the first good, demand for the other goods ignores wealth, Hicksian demand ignores the target utility, v = w + φ(p) and e = c(u) − φ(p).
  • EN4K Demand for the utility max{ax, ay} + min{x, y} MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 3 Machine-checked With u = max{ax, ay} + min{x, y}, indifference curves are kinked on the diagonal, and Marshallian and Hicksian demand pick a corner, the kink, or a segment by the price ratio.
