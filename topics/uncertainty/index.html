Part III · Uncertainty and risk

Choice under uncertainty

Choice under uncertainty ranks lotteries over prizes. Preferences satisfying independence and continuity have an expected-utility representation (von Neumann–Morgenstern), and de Finetti’s theorem derives subjective probabilities from preferences over bets.

Lotteries, independence, continuity, expected utility.

Problems9

  • 65GY Two procedural preferences over lotteries Problem Set 7 Machine-checked Ranking lotteries by the size of their support, or by their most likely prize, violates both the independence and the continuity axioms and is exposed to framing.
  • 2A4D Mean–variance preferences and expected utility Problem Set 7 Machine-checked Ranking lotteries by mean minus a quarter of the variance violates the vNM assumptions, while mean minus squared mean minus variance is an expected utility.
  • K13R Deciding on Sunday or on Monday Problem Set 7 Machine-checked A contingent plan made in advance is a choice among mixed lotteries; under the independence axiom it selects exactly what the decision maker would choose later.
  • JMAR Bayesian updating and time consistency Problem Set 7 Machine-checked With vNM preferences and Bayesian updating, learning that a common outcome will not occur never changes the optimal action; without either, time consistency can fail.
  • GYXY Comparing income distributions by equality Problem Set 7 Machine-checked Income distributions look like lotteries, but an egalitarian ranking cannot satisfy independence; mixture quasi-convexity is proposed instead, with two rankings satisfying it.
  • 9E1M Trading off quality and length of life Problem Set 7 Machine-checked An expected-utility preference over lotteries on (quality, length) of life satisfies three natural conditions exactly when it maximizes the expectation of v(q)·t with v > 0.
  • RPWH Preferences over a day’s schedule Problem Set 7 Machine-checked Time-additive utility over daily schedules requires indifference to how time is arranged and invariance to appending a common schedule; a taste for balance escapes it.
  • 540E The two envelopes paradox Problem Set 8 Corrected Amounts 2ⁿ and 2ⁿ⁺¹ are drawn with probability 2ⁿ/3ⁿ⁺¹; when envelopes are assigned by a fair coin, your friend’s envelope is always worth more in expectation.
  • ZSF9 The betweenness axiom MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 2 Machine-checked Betweenness follows from independence, means straight but not necessarily parallel indifference lines, and is compatible with the Allais paradox.
