Fields of economics/Part III · Uncertainty and risk

Choice under uncertainty

Choice under uncertainty ranks lotteries over prizes. Preferences satisfying independence and continuity have an expected-utility representation (von Neumann–Morgenstern), and de Finetti’s theorem derives subjective probabilities from preferences over bets.

Lecture Notes in Microeconomic Theory

  1. Problem 1. Two procedural preferences over lotteries Ranking lotteries by the size of their support violates both the independence and the continuity axioms, ranking them by their most likely prize can violate both, and each is exposed to framing. Problem Set 7 Standard
  2. Problem 2. Mean–variance preferences and expected utility Ranking lotteries by mean minus a quarter of the variance violates the vNM assumptions, while mean minus squared mean minus variance is an expected utility. Problem Set 7 Standard
  3. Problem 3. Deciding on Sunday or on Monday A contingent plan made in advance is a choice among mixed lotteries; under the independence axiom it selects exactly what the decision maker would choose later. Problem Set 7 Easy
  4. Problem 4. Bayesian updating and time consistency With vNM preferences and Bayesian updating, learning that a common outcome will not occur never changes the optimal action; without either, time consistency can fail. Problem Set 7 Standard
  5. Problem 5. Comparing income distributions by equality Income distributions look like lotteries, but an egalitarian ranking can violate independence; mixture quasi-convexity is proposed instead, with two rankings satisfying it. Problem Set 7 Standard
Show every problem from RubinsteinShow fewer
  1. Problem 6. Trading off quality and length of life An expected-utility preference over lotteries on (quality, length) of life satisfies three natural conditions exactly when it maximizes the expectation of v(q)·t with v > 0. Problem Set 7 Difficult
  2. Problem 8. Preferences over a day’s schedule Time-additive utility over daily schedules requires indifference to how time is arranged and invariance to appending a common schedule; a taste for balance escapes it. Problem Set 7 Standard
  3. Problem 7. The two envelopes paradox Corrected statement Amounts 2ⁿ and 2ⁿ⁺¹ are drawn with probability 2ⁿ/3ⁿ⁺¹; when envelopes are assigned by a fair coin, your friend’s envelope is always worth more in expectation. Problem Set 8

MIT 14.121 Microeconomic Theory I

  1. Question 3. Betweenness and the Allais paradox Betweenness follows from independence, means straight but not necessarily parallel indifference lines, and is compatible with the Allais paradox. Problem Set 2
