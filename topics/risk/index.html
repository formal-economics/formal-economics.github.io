Fields of economics/Part III · Uncertainty and risk

Risk attitudes

For monetary lotteries, attitudes toward risk are read off the vNM utility. On an interval of prizes, risk aversion is concavity and being more risk averse is a concave transformation; first-order stochastic dominance is the ranking that every expected-utility maximizer who prefers more money shares.

The landmarks of the field, to read first.

  1. u1​(t)=ϕ(u2​(t)), ϕ concave Claim Arrow–Pratt Three definitions of “more risk averse” agree for increasing, continuous utilities on an interval of prizes. Lecture 8, p. 118 Corrected
  2. pD1​q⟺G(p,x)≥G(q,x)  ∀x Claim First-order stochastic dominance A lottery is preferred by every expected-utility maximizer who likes money exactly when its distribution function lies below the other’s. Lecture 8, p. 114
  3. ≿ risk averse⟺u concave Claim Risk aversion and concavity A vNM preference is risk averse exactly when its utility is concave, once the prizes form an interval. Lecture 8, p. 116 Corrected
  4. u(x)=a−be−αx Claim Constant absolute risk aversion Monotone, risk-averse preferences that are invariant to wealth have linear or exponential utility on the real line. Lecture 8, p. 122 Corrected

Lecture Notes in Microeconomic Theory

  1. Problem 1. Sign conditions for linear forms A linear form is nonnegative on positive vectors iff its coefficients are, and on positive decreasing vectors iff its partial sums are: the algebra of stochastic dominance. Problem Set 8 Standard
  2. Problem 2. Second-order stochastic dominance Second-order dominance, the unanimous verdict of risk-averse expected-utility maximizers, follows from first-order dominance and from adding fair noise, and has a partial-sum test. Problem Set 8 Standard
  3. Problem 4. Preferences over portfolios of risky assets Corrected statement A risk-averse expected-utility investor’s ranking of asset holdings is convex, and monotone once there is at least one asset. Problem Set 8 Standard
  4. Problem 8. Who pays more for information? Is a more risk-averse agent ready to pay more to learn the state of nature? Under expected utility it could go either way, and both cases are exhibited. Problem Set 8

MIT 14.121 Microeconomic Theory I

  1. Question 1. How much insurance to buy A strictly risk-averse consumer insures fully when insurance is actuarially fair; when it is loaded and utility still rises at the insured wealth, she insures at most fully. Problem Set 2
  2. Question 2. To feed the parking meter or not Corrected statement Paying a meter or risking a fine: elasticities of the gain from paying, a wealth threshold between payers and violators, and how fines, prices and policing move it. Problem Set 2
  3. Question 4. Should the young hold more stocks? Informal A discussion question on the advice that younger people should invest a larger share of wealth in stocks than older people. Problem Set 2
