Part III · Uncertainty and risk

Risk attitudes

For monetary lotteries, attitudes toward risk are read off the vNM utility. On an interval of prizes, risk aversion is concavity and being more risk averse is a concave transformation; first-order stochastic dominance is the ranking that every expected-utility maximizer who prefers more money shares.

Risk aversion, certainty equivalents, comparisons of risk aversion, stochastic dominance.

Results4

  • N24H First-order stochastic dominance Lecture 8 Machine-checked A lottery is preferred by every expected-utility maximizer who likes money exactly when its distribution function lies below the other’s.
  • CQGJ Risk aversion and concave utility Lecture 8 Corrected A vNM preference is risk averse exactly when its utility is concave, once the prizes form an interval.
  • VPWC Comparing risk aversion (Arrow–Pratt) Lecture 8 Corrected Three definitions of “more risk averse” agree for increasing, continuous utilities on an interval of prizes.
  • HEXC Constant absolute risk aversion and exponential utility Lecture 8 Corrected Monotone, risk-averse preferences that are invariant to wealth have linear or exponential utility on the real line.

Problems7

  • S37G Sign conditions for linear forms Problem Set 8 Machine-checked A linear form is nonnegative on positive vectors iff its coefficients are, and on positive decreasing vectors iff its partial sums are: the algebra of stochastic dominance.
  • K7S8 Second-order stochastic dominance Problem Set 8 Machine-checked Second-order dominance, the unanimous verdict of risk-averse expected-utility maximizers, follows from first-order dominance and from adding fair noise, and has a partial-sum test.
  • ASDQ Preferences over portfolios of risky assets Problem Set 8 Corrected A risk-averse expected-utility investor’s ranking of asset holdings is convex, and monotone once there is at least one asset.
  • 1S2E Who pays more for information? Problem Set 8 Machine-checked Is a more risk-averse agent ready to pay more to learn the state of nature? Under expected utility it could go either way, and both cases are exhibited.
  • 3Y6Y How much insurance to buy MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 2 Machine-checked A strictly risk-averse consumer insures fully when insurance is actuarially fair; when it is loaded and utility still rises at the insured wealth, she insures at most fully.
  • 185R To feed the parking meter or not MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 2 Corrected Paying a meter or risking a fine: elasticities of the gain from paying, a wealth threshold between payers and violators, and how fines, prices and policing move it.
  • AD12 Should the young hold more stocks? MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 2 Informal A discussion question on the advice that younger people should invest a larger share of wealth in stocks than older people.
