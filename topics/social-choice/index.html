Fields of economics/Part V · Collective choice

Social choice

Social choice aggregates individual preferences into a social ranking or decision. With at least three alternatives, Arrow’s theorem leaves only dictatorships under Pareto and independence of irrelevant alternatives; related entries treat strategy-proofness, majority rule and single-peaked preferences.

Source
Rubinstein

Results to know

The landmarks of the field, to read first.

  1. Par∧IIA⟹F is dictatorial Claim Arrow’s impossibility theorem With three or more alternatives, Pareto and IIA leave only dictatorial social welfare functions. Lecture 9, p. 131
  2. straightforward⟹dictatorial Theorem Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem Muller–Satterthwaite for monotone social welfare functions, Gibbard–Satterthwaite for strategy-proof social choice, Dutta–Jackson–Le Breton for procedure-invariant choice rules. Lecture 9, pp. 133–134 Corrected

Definitions

  1. Social welfare functions The basic model: individual orderings are aggregated into a social preference by a social welfare function. Lecture 9, p. 129
  2. Pareto condition (Par) Unanimous strict preferences are respected by society. Lecture 9, p. 130
  3. Independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA) The social ranking of two alternatives depends only on individuals’ rankings of those two. Lecture 9, pp. 130–131

More results

  1. Independence of the conditions of Arrow’s theorem Corrected Examples show that Pareto, transitivity of the social order, and the presence of three alternatives are each needed. Claim Lecture 9, p. 131

Problems

Exercises from the sources, with their answers checked by computer.

Practise these problems →
  1. Problem 1. Borda rules violate IIA With at least three alternatives and two individuals, every Borda scoring rule violates independence of irrelevant alternatives. Problem Set 9 Easy
  2. Problem 2. May’s theorem With two alternatives, majority rule is the only social welfare function that is anonymous, neutral and positively responsive, and the three axioms are independent. Problem Set 9 Moderately difficult
  3. Problem 3. Majority rule with single peaks With an odd number of individuals whose preferences over [0, 1] are single-peaked, pairwise majority voting yields a complete and transitive social preference. Problem Set 9 Standard
  4. Problem 4. Aggregating recommendations When individuals recommend actions instead of ranking them, unanimity and independence force a dictator once there are three actions, and each condition is needed. Problem Set 9 Moderately difficult
  5. Problem 5. Decisive coalitions and Arrow’s theorem Corrected The decisive-coalition route to Arrow’s theorem — field expansion, group contraction, a decisive individual — holds for distinct alternatives when there are at least three. Problem Set 9 Moderately difficult
  6. Problem 6. Who is an economist? Aggregating the faculty’s views of who is an economist: with three or more members, consensus and independence leave only methods that copy one member’s view. Problem Set 9 Moderately difficult

Also relevant, from other fields

  1. Single-peaked preferences Preferences on an interval with a unique ideal point, declining on both sides. Definition Lecture 4, p. 55
  2. Single-peaked preferences as Λ-strictly convex preferences On [0, 1], continuous single-peaked preferences are the Λ-strictly convex ones for the left and right criteria. Claim Lecture 4, p. 55
  3. Comparing income distributions by equality Income distributions look like lotteries, but an egalitarian ranking can violate independence; mixture quasi-convexity is proposed instead, with two rankings satisfying it. Problem Problem Set 7, Problem 5, p. 111
