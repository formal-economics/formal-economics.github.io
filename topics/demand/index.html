Part II · Consumers and producers

Consumer demand and duality

The consumer chooses a best bundle in the budget set, and demand records that choice at every price and wealth. The entries run from Walras’ law and first-order conditions to Roy’s identity and the duality with expenditure minimization.

Budget sets, demand, indirect utility, Hicksian demand, expenditure, Roy's identity, Slutsky.

Definitions3

  • BJEG Indirect preferences over budget sets Lecture 6 Machine-checked Prices and wealth are ranked by the bundles they allow the consumer to buy.
  • V65Y The prime problem and the demand Lecture 6 Machine-checked Demand is the unique solution of utility maximization over the budget set.
  • 2RVJ Hicksian demand and the expenditure function Lecture 6 Machine-checked The cheapest bundle at least as good as a target bundle, and its cost.

Results15

  • 50HD Existence of a solution to the consumer problem Lecture 5 Machine-checked A continuous preference attains a best bundle in every budget set with positive prices and wealth.
  • SSRC Convexity and uniqueness of the consumer’s solution Lecture 5 Machine-checked Convex preferences give a convex set of optimal bundles; strictly convex preferences give at most one.
  • 07JS First-order conditions for the consumer problem Lecture 5 Machine-checked A consumed good must offer the highest subjective value per unit of price.
  • RAY3 The marginal rate of substitution equals the price ratio Lecture 5 Machine-checked For two consumed goods, the ratio of subjective values equals the ratio of prices.
  • VKQK First-order conditions are sufficient under convexity Lecture 5 Machine-checked For strongly monotone, convex, continuous and differentiable preferences, a bundle that exhausts the budget and meets the first-order conditions is optimal.
  • 9A46 Walras’ law Lecture 5 Machine-checked With monotone preferences, optimal bundles exhaust the budget.
  • 41XD Continuity of the demand function Lecture 5 Machine-checked Continuous preferences with unique optima give demand that varies continuously with prices and wealth.
  • 989H Closed graph of the demand correspondence Lecture 5 Machine-checked Limits of optimal bundles are optimal at the limiting prices and wealth.
  • QJPA Revealed preference from demand Lecture 5 Machine-checked What a rationalizing monotone preference must say about chosen and affordable bundles.
  • 55RK The compensated law of demand Lecture 5 Machine-checked Under Walras’ law and the weak axiom, demand moves opposite to compensated price changes.
  • A62K Properties of indirect preferences over budget sets Lecture 6 Machine-checked Indirect preferences over budget sets are invariant to scaling, weakly decreasing in prices, strictly increasing in wealth, continuous and quasi-convex.
  • AWMW Roy’s identity Lecture 6 Machine-checked Demand is recovered from the indirect utility function as minus the ratio of its price and wealth derivatives.
  • BKP1 The prime and dual turtle Lecture 6 Machine-checked A one-dimensional illustration of duality: maximal distance in given time versus minimal time for a given distance.
  • JHQA Properties of Hicksian demand and the expenditure function Lecture 6 Machine-checked Homogeneity, the compensated law of demand, monotonicity, continuity and concavity of the expenditure function, and the dual of Roy’s identity.
  • CGE7 Duality of utility maximization and expenditure minimization Lecture 6 Machine-checked For monotone and continuous preferences, a bundle solves the prime problem exactly when it solves the corresponding dual problem.

Problems24

  • CEM1 Homothetic preferences and demand Problem Set 5 Machine-checked Homothetic preferences produce demand that is proportional to wealth.
  • C0C9 Quasi-linear preferences and wealth effects Problem Set 5 Machine-checked For a consumer quasi-linear in the first commodity, demand for that commodity is zero up to a wealth threshold and then absorbs all further wealth, or is zero throughout.
  • 2GSH The demand correspondence Problem Set 5 Machine-checked The set of all optimal bundles, computed for perfect substitutes and shown to be upper semi-continuous for continuous preferences.
  • WFXS Which behaviour patterns are rationalizable? Problem Set 5 Machine-checked Buying up to one unit of good 1 is fully rationalizable on its natural reading; choosing quantities in proportion to prices is not.
  • JD04 A consumer with a utility floor Problem Set 5 Corrected Securing a utility floor and then maximizing the amount of good 1 is rationalizable, spends all wealth and gives continuous demand; a closed floor set ensures a unique choice.
  • NZYS The representative consumer Problem Set 5 Machine-checked Aggregate demand need not behave like the demand of a single rational consumer.
  • Z50J Under the weak axiom, Giffen goods are inferior Problem Set 5 Corrected A good whose demand rises with its own price must, somewhere, be one whose demand falls with wealth — given consistent choice.
  • XPXN Strictly concave preferences Problem Set 5 Corrected Consumers who prefer extremes to mixtures choose corners, and their demand jumps at a threshold price.
  • 06XS Saving as a residual decision Problem Set 5 Corrected Saving what is left after pursuing an aspiration level is fully rationalizable where choice is unique, but for some value functions by no continuous or differentiable preference.
  • 3WZA Is v = w/p₁ + w/p₂ a rational consumer’s indirect utility? Problem Set 6 Machine-checked Roy’s identity turns v = w/p₁ + w/p₂ into a demand; the utility √x₁ + √x₂ generates that demand with indirect utility √v, and its square recovers v exactly.
  • Z1TD Continuity of the Hicksian demand Problem Set 6 Machine-checked Monotone, continuous and strictly convex preferences make the Hicksian demand h(p, z) continuous in prices and in the reference bundle.
  • P1YT Compensating and equivalent variation Problem Set 6 Corrected Two money measures of a price change: computed for perfect substitutes, compared with the area under demand, and equal under quasi-linearity when only the first price moves.
  • H6PS The envelope theorem and Roy’s identity Problem Set 6 Corrected The envelope theorem holds once the Lagrangian and the solution are differentiable, and applied to the consumer’s problem it yields Roy’s identity.
  • S9ZZ CES demand and the elasticity of substitution MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 3 Corrected CES demand and indirect utility, their linear and Leontief limits, and the elasticity of substitution: 1/(1 − ρ) for ρ ≠ 0, 1; 0 for Leontief; 1 for Cobb–Douglas; none for linear.
  • WPSR What utility maximization implies for two demand functions MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 3 Machine-checked Given demand formulas for two of three goods, Walras’ law yields the third, both formulas are homogeneous of degree zero, and utility maximization forces α = 100, β = γ = −5.
  • EQWH Quasi-linear preferences have no wealth effects MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 3 Machine-checked With preferences quasi-linear in the first good, demand for the other goods ignores wealth, Hicksian demand ignores the target utility, v = w + φ(p) and e = c(u) − φ(p).
  • EN4K Demand for the utility max{ax, ay} + min{x, y} MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 3 Machine-checked With u = max{ax, ay} + min{x, y}, indifference curves are kinked on the diagonal, and Marshallian and Hicksian demand pick a corner, the kink, or a segment by the price ratio.
  • 85D0 Renting or buying a home MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 3 Informal A discussion question on modelling rented and purchased housing in a family’s utility function when house prices rise much faster than rents.
  • ETHS Recovering utility from the expenditure function MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 4 Corrected From e(p, u) = p₁p₂u/(p₁ + p₂) one recovers utility on the axes, each indifference curve x₂ = (√u₀ − √x₁)², and finally u = (√x₁ + √x₂)².
  • ZTWY An economy with no positive representative consumer MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 4 Machine-checked With fixed wealth shares, aggregate demand need not be generated by any rational preference: two consumers with kinked utilities produce a violation of the weak axiom.
  • B245 How deadweight loss changes with the tax MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 5 Corrected The derivatives in the tax of two deadweight-loss measures: both are zero at t = 0; for t > 0 the measure at the old utility rises, while the one at the new utility can fall.
  • ZFCH Substitutes in compensated demand MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 5 Corrected Substitutability need not be symmetric or the same at all prices; with enough regularity every good has a substitute; coffee can raise or lower what one pays to keep tea.
  • 4C7H From indirect utility to welfare and price indexes MIT 14.121 · Problem Set 5 Machine-checked From v = I/√(p₁p₂): expenditure ū√(p₁p₂), Hicksian demand, a compensating variation of −400 for a fourfold price rise, and Paasche 8/5 < 2 < Laspeyres 5/2.
  • KM2S Demand systems and expected utility BU Qualifying Exam (2020) Machine-checked Cobb–Douglas demand; when a linear demand system is Slutsky symmetric and comes from a preference; a utility index that is not risk averse; rankings no expected utility allows.
