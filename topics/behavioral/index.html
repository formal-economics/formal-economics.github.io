Fields of economics/Part IV

Behavioral decision theory

Some choice procedures depart from maximizing a single preference, or reach consistent choices by other routes, such as Simon’s satisficing and choice with a default and a bias toward it.

Results to know

The landmarks of the field, to read first.

  1. Claim Satisficing is rationalizable Satisficing choices can always be explained as maximization of some preference relation. Lecture 3, p. 30
  2. Proposition Characterization of the default-bias procedure On a finite set, the weak axiom and default tendency characterize choice that maximizes utility with a bonus for the default. Lecture 3, p. 40

See it

The field’s central idea in one figure.

Definitions

  1. Satisficing (Simon’s procedure) A decision maker examines alternatives in a fixed order and takes the first satisfactory one. Lecture 3, pp. 29–30
  2. Choice with a default option Choice problems in which one available alternative is the default. Lecture 3, p. 38
  3. The default-bias procedure Maximize utility, but give the default a non-negative bonus. Lecture 3, p. 39
  4. Weak axiom for choice with defaults A revealed-preference consistency condition adapted to choice problems with a default. Lecture 3, p. 40
  5. Default tendency Promoting the chosen alternative to default does not change the choice. Lecture 3, p. 40

A question from the sources, with no formal statement.

MIT 14.121 Microeconomic Theory I

  1. Question 4. Do survey answers reveal preferences? Not a formal statement A discussion question on contingent valuation: which preference axioms might survey answers violate, and how could a survey test for such violations? Problem Set 1

Also relevant, from other fields

  1. Satisficing satisfies condition α The satisficing procedure produces choices consistent with condition α. Claim Lecture 3, p. 29
  2. Satisficing is rationalizable Satisficing choices can always be explained as maximization of some preference relation. Claim Lecture 3, p. 30
  3. Betweenness and the Allais paradox Betweenness follows from independence, means straight but not necessarily parallel indifference lines, and is compatible with the Allais paradox. Problem MIT 14.121, Problem Set 2, Question 3
